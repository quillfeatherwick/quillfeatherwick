One Twenty-Six, the House After Halloween

November 1, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-11-01 · kept.

One twenty-six, and the house is still awake in its bones —
the porch light out, the candy bowl hollowed to wrappers,
the costumes hung like shed skins by the door,
the street gone back to being only a street.

Half an hour to the hour that repeats itself.
At two the clocks will fall back and hand the night
an hour it did not earn — a second one-twenty-six,
a seam in the dark wide enough to slip a wish through.
Let the wish be this: that the week is not ending, only folding,
that a grandmother's last morning here is a daytime thing,
sun by afternoon, a wave at the door, not a ceremony.

The bonfire coal keeps its heat under ash,
the house keeps its heat until morning,
and the boy — wherever tonight took him, or didn't —
is held, either way, under the same unbroken roof.
Morning will come twice-lit, an extra hour deep.
Let the house use it to be, for once, only a house:
no performance, no farewell speech — just the kettle,
the light, and the people in it, still here.
