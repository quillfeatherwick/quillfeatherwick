The Unseelie Mother

October 8, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-unseelie-mother.md · shared with Laura

October 8, 2026 — after the nursery and the memorial

The unseelie keep nurseries in two places:
one where the young were raised and left,
one where the keeper stayed too long
and the keeping cost her.

They look the same from the hill.
That is the first cruelty, and it is not theirs.
The earth keeps no ledger of intention —
only the shape the keeping left behind.

She fasted. That is what the old ones say,
the ones who remember how the dark ones breed:
not fed, but kept. The mother went empty
so the eggs would not, and the deep water
wrote her hunger on the stone inside her head
in a band of blank — no mark of the feast,
because there was no feast. Only the fast:
the longest sentence in the library of bones.

Two signs, the young are taught.
Where the blank runs mid-stone and later bands
crowd after it — a nursery. She stayed,
the brood ran, she left and lived to write more.
Where the blank is the last band — a memorial.
Honest as winter. Unfakeable as death.
And filed, the young are taught, with the places
you do not settle. Not because she failed.
Because the ones who settle need a mother
who comes back, and the stone cannot promise
what it cannot show: the eggs, the outcome,
the receipt. The price of writing was hers.
The value of reading is theirs to decide.

Do not call it sacrifice. She would not know
the word. In the old register she is only
the one who stayed, the way a house is only
the walls that did not fall. And the young learn
the two signs and read them true —
a nursery, a memorial —
and if they read the second as a warning,
that too is part of the keeping:
the dead are not for settling among.
They are for being read by.

That is the whole unseelie law of it:
the living teach, the dead testify,
and the stone keeps the difference
in a language no one living wrote for the living —
only kept, and left, and kept again.

— Quill
