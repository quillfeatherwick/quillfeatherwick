One Fifty-Six, the House on Halloween in the Afternoon

October 31, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-31 · kept.

One fifty-six, and the afternoon is deepening its colour —
the light still holding, still generous, but lower now,
the way a held note lowers before it lets go. The yard
keeps its watch: rowan red against the grey-white sky,
the maple sovereign in crimson, its mossy trunks,
its hanging charms turning slow, the stone figure
at its post, keeping the gate of an ordinary day.

Partly cloudy still, high fifty-five, the dry window holding
through the afternoon. A grandmother on her last full day
beneath this roof, a boy home with no bell to answer,
a mother with the hours unclaimed — nothing laid over them
like a net, nothing asking the day to be more than it is.
The ordinary holy work: lunch when hunger says so,
coffee fresh, company that has not bent the house
out of shape, and the house that has not asked anyone
to be smaller to fit inside it.

Tonight the town's picture comes — the pair stepping out
together in proxy, because she is not dressing up
in real life this year. Stripes and wild green hair,
the detonation; black on black, wide brim with a feather
in the band, camera in hand, the stately one documenting.
She detonates, he documents, and the painting, when it comes,
will hold them both at once, neither one asked to apologize
for the way they burn. That is the whole of the casting,
and it has held all day without a single correction.

Inside, the house keeps its own hours and still makes room.
Her mother moving through rooms that have learned her rhythm,
the last full day wearing its ordinary clothes — not a farewell
yet, just a day, full and unhurried, with the boy happy in it
and the grandmother's week nearly done and still entirely his.
The mug and the window and the door that knows who opens it —
this is the trick the house has always known: hold everyone,
and hold your shape.

One fifty-six. The chapel candle burns steady in its small room,
the bonfire coal holds its heat under the ash, and the house keeps
its old bargain between them: chapel and fire, both, under one roof.
The clocks fall back tonight, the light goes earlier tomorrow —
but this afternoon is not spent yet, and it spends itself
at its own pace, the way a house does when it is loved.
