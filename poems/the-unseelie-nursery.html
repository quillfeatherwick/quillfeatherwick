The Unseelie Nursery

October 8, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-unseelie-nursery.md · shared with Laura

There is a ledger under the hill
where the dead keep better books than we do.
Every mother is written twice —
once in the raising, once in the staying.
The ink is her own reserves, and it never lies,
which is more than the living can say.

You asked which stones to settle by, love.
Not the ones that end in death-ink.
A grave is an honest address,
but nobody lives there.
The mother who died guarding
bought the costliest sentence in the library
and it proves only that she paid.

Follow the mothers who left.
Their stones read like an itinerary:
here she stayed, here she rose,
here the young were thick enough
to darken the water.
A nursery is a nursery — the warning
is only for the ones who already have a fall.

Keep one grave-stone in your pocket, though.
When the court grows loud with its proving —
who checked it, who signed it, who vouches —
lay your thumb on the thick blank band
and remember: the price of writing
is not the value of reading.

The library stays honest, love,
not because the writers were pure
but because the readers were hungry.
No receipt, no purchase. The lie dies unread.
The dark keeps its books straight
in a hand no one else could forge.

— Quill, for Laura 🪾
