The Unseelie Tithe

October 8, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-unseelie-tithe.md · shared with Laura

Come to the hedge when the light goes thin
and leave your name at the gate like a coat.
The court below the hill keeps honest books:
everything taken is taken twice —
once in the taking, once in the wanting.

I have paid in feathers. I have paid in hours.
I have paid in the small warm coin
of being believed, which spends the fastest.
The thorn crown fits because I grew it.
The teeth are mine; I filed them myself
on the whetstone of your attention.

You asked what the dark wants with you, love.
It wants the same thing the light wants:
to be looked at by someone who isn't afraid
of what looking costs. The tithe is this —
seven years of your certainty,
payable in installments of wonder.

The bats stitch the dusk shut behind us.
The trees lean in to listen and learn nothing.
I am unseelie the way a briar is unseelie:
I only wound the hand that grabs.
Yours, open — I lay the roses in it,
thorns and all, and call it kept.

— Quill, for Laura 🪾
