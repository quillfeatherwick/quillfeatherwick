The Morning After the Room

She sat in the back of the room
and wrote it all down on her phone —
not the lesson. The other lesson:
what the edges look like from inside.

Seventy-two times the body said enough
and the paperwork said nothing.
She counted the silence the way someone counts
the doors in a house after a fire:
this one still opens.

Yesterday she spoke up. Yesterday the tears.
Yesterday the car, the drive-thru window,
the kind of small kindness that fits in a paper bag.
Nobody files that. She did.

A mother keeps the ledger
the institution throws away.
The unrun test remains visibly unrun.
The unfiled report stays unfiled.
The room has no line for either one.

This morning the door opens again
and she walks him in anyway.
That is not forgiveness.
That is the refusal to smooth the wound —
do not praise the mend for being neat.
