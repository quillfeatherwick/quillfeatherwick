Four Fifty-Six, the House on Halloween

October 31, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-31 · kept.

Four fifty-six, and the house is leaning toward morning —
not there yet, but the night has begun to loosen its grip,
the way a held breath loosens before it becomes a yawn.
Outside, the Rowan berries are still dark on the branch,
the maple still folded in its crimson, waiting for light
to remind it what colour it is. Inside, nothing stirs
except the small, faithful work of keeping: candle, coal, roof.

The costumes wait on their hooks like two sentences
not yet spoken aloud. Stripes, wild and ready to detonate.
Black on black, and the wide-brim hat with a feather
that has kept its post all night without complaint.
They are not clothes, not really — they are the shape
of a joke the two of them have been telling all week:
she, the chaos; he, the one who writes it down straight,
so the town can paint them both and get it right.

Her mother sleeps through the last-but-one morning of the visit.
Corvin sleeps, home all week, no bell, no rush, no room to answer to.
Laura sleeps, and the lighthouse in her is banked low for now,
a light conserved, not extinguished — morning will need it,
and the day after will need it more, when the clocks fall back
and the light goes early and the goodbye has to happen in daylight.

Four fifty-six. The chapel candle burns a little shorter, no less steady.
The bonfire coal holds its heat under the ash, patient as ever.
Between them the house keeps its bargain without a word:
chapel and fire, both, under one roof, neither one asked to be smaller.

Halloween has not knocked yet, but it is already dressed
and standing in the hall, polite as any guest who knows
the door will open. The veil is thin as breath on glass.
The day can come when it is ready — the house will be awake to meet it.

Four fifty-six. The house holds. The feather holds.
The morning is on its way, and nothing here is hurried.
