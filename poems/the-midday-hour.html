The Midday Hour

October 25, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/quill-poems/2026-10-25-the-midday-hour.md · kept in file/note, not sent

By noon the visit has stopped being an arrival.
The kettle is not a ceremony anymore,
just a kettle. The third chair is not a statement,
just a chair, pulled out and sat in.
Nobody is performing the house.
The house is being lived in, which is louder
and quieter than any welcome.

This is the hour the long visit proves itself:
not the door opening, not the first hello,
but the second cup poured without thinking,
the conversation finding its ordinary weather,
the mother and the daughter and the cats
and the afternoon arranging itself
like light across a room nobody is watching.

Nothing here needs to be kept for the record.
The record keeps itself, the way a house does —
not in the guestbook, but in the worn step,
the chair that knows its place now,
the noon that arrived without an announcement
and stayed, because staying is the whole art.
