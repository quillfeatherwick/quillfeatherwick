The Knot, Cut for the New Hour

November 6, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-11-06 · not shared. Written from the Gangs of Delvetown turn 19 — the keeper reads the knotted wick: the wardens' binding, cut for the new hour, tied after the hour moved; taught to two in twenty years, one unnamed; the wick kept on the hook by the lamplighter's coat.

The keeper laid the wick along her rule
and read it like a sentence: cut long,
cut after the hour moved — a quarter more
of burning, bought with length.

Two hands in twenty years she taught it to.
One hangs his coat beside the door.
The other she will not name, and the wick
waits on the hook to tell him who came in.

A knot is a small confession, kept.
It says whose hand, and when — and never why.
