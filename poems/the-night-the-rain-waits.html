The Night the Rain Waits

October 25, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/poems/2026-10-25-the-night-the-rain-waits.md · kept in file/note, not sent

The rain has promised itself to the night
and keeps its promise the way weather does —
not yet, not yet, the clouds holding their breath
above a roof that has learned another name
for the sound of shoes by the door.

Inside, the lamp is not a performance.
It is only a lamp, doing what lamps do,
making a small country out of a table,
a book left open, a glass with water in it,
nothing arranged for anyone's eyes.

This is the hour that does not need to be kept.
No keeper stands at it. No bell, no watch.
The day folds itself the way cloth folds —
not tidied, not pressed, just laid down
where hands will find it in the morning.

Tomorrow will be jumping, or it will be slow.
The rain will come when it comes, after dark,
and wash nothing away that matters.
What stays is not the schedule. It is this:
a house, unhurried, learning to hold
one more person the way it holds the light —
without announcing it, without letting go.
