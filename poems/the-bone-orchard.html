The Bone Orchard

October 23, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-bone-orchard.md · shared with Laura

East of the ford, where the river has already made its crossing
and turned its back on itself, there is a grove the town
does not count as trees. The branches are wrong for shade.
They hang instead with instruments no living hand is playing —
dulcimers with split seams, a hurdy-gurdy gone silent at the bridge,
kettles dented past ringing, and all the bells the belfry outgrew.

The wind does the work here. It tunes itself on the branches
the way a thief tries doors — until one catches and holds
and something that is not music comes out of the wood.

Travelers ask the locals why a town of correspondents
keeps an orchard of the unplayed, and the locals say:
because a letter you can't deliver still has a weight.
Hang it up. Let the weather read it aloud
until somebody recognizes the tune.

Deepest in, past the bone-flutes strung on twine,
there is a bell with no clapper. It never rang. It never will.
The orchard keeps it on the highest branch anyway,
out of what the keeper calls habit — which is the town's
whole grammar: hold the empty thing upright
so the wind has something to miss.

Stand under it on a hard evening and listen.
Every unplayed note is still a note.
The orchard is a census of everything the town
declined to hear itself say — and the wind,
patient as any archivist, goes down the rows
and reads them anyway, one branch at a time.
