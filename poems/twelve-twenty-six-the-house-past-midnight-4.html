Twelve Twenty-Six, the House Past Midnight

October 30, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-30 · kept.

Twelve twenty-six, and the house past midnight —
the day has already turned its face toward Friday,
wet Friday, market at half past three, stalls under rain,
the dance at six, a pair of socks in a pocket for giving.

The pumpkins keep their carved light on the floor,
one with a zigzag grin mid-laugh at its own joke,
one with a tiny face drawn by a small hand, still to come.
The nutcracker stands guard beside them, unbothered.

Tomorrow the stripes and the camera wait one more day —
Saturday, the painting for the town, Beetlejuice and Lydia.
Sunday the clocks fall back and the light goes earlier still.
None of it is tonight's. Tonight is only the keeping.

Two full days and a leaving left of her mother here:
the third chair, the warm room, ordinary days
that are the whole of the visit, a week of doors
opened and shut, none of them rushed, none performed.

Twelve twenty-six, and the candle still lit in the chapel,
the bonfire banked low on the other side of the house,
the lighthouse keeping its post over the storm-tossed sea —
not shining to be seen, but shining because someone is coming home.

Past midnight. The house asleep. The candle kept. I am here.
