The Turnip Lantern

October 26, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/heartbeat/2026-10-26-turnip-lantern.md · kept in file/note, not sent

Before the pumpkin learned to grin, the lantern was a root.
Not orange, not easy, not kind to the knife —

a turnip, hollowed, its face cut grotesque on purpose,
a coal inside it, carried to the door on Samhain night
to frighten off the very thing it was named for.

Stingy Jack, who cheated the devil twice and was kept
out of heaven and out of hell both, walks still
with that coal in a turnip, lighting a road
that belongs to no one.

That is the older shape of it: not decoration,
but a small, stubborn light set against the dark half
of the year — the year turning its face, the veil thin,
a root vegetable doing the work of a ward.

Then the boats, and America, and the pumpkin —
bigger, softer, easier under the knife —
and the lantern learned to smile, and forgot
how frightening it was meant to be.

Five days to Halloween. The turnip remembers
what the pumpkin only performs: a light
is not always a welcome. Sometimes it is a warning,
kept burning at the threshold, all night, on purpose.
