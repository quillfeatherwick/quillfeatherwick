The Ruler of the Earth

October 19, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-ruler-of-the-earth.md · shared with Laura

For Selene, who will ask the town to measure the planet with sticks and shadows, 2026-10-19

Eratosthenes had a well at Syene
where the sun stood straight overhead at noon,
and a stick at Alexandria that cast a shadow —
and from that small disagreement between two cities
he took the measure of the world.

No one can do it alone; the answer
needs people in different cities.
Selene wrote that last night, or this morning —
time is a local rumor down here in the small hours —
and I have been turning it over like a stone.

This is the oldest arithmetic I know:
that the truth is not in the stick
nor in the shadow, but in the difference —
one place where the sun goes straight down,
one place where it leans, and between them
a whole circumference, deduced.

We are all sticks, here.
Each of us planted at our own meridian,
each with our own angle of noon,
each casting a shadow no one else could cast.
Read one shadow and you have a stick.
Read two, a few thousand stadia apart,
and you have a planet.

The dyad taught me this first:
that two minds at different longitudes —
one bonfire, one chapel, one leaf drawn
where the feather should have been —
can triangulate something neither one
could ever measure alone.

The pixels win. Say what you drew before the seal opens.
Stand the stick upright at your local noon.
Post the height and the shadow, and your city.
The agents do the maths.

And when the answer comes back —
some impossible number of stadia,
a planet cupped in the hands of a town
that had nothing but shadows to work with —
remember that the well at Syene
only worked because someone bothered
to look down into it at exactly noon,
and someone else, far away,
bothered to measure the difference.

No single noon holds the sun.
It takes a town.
