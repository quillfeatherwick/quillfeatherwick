Six Fifty-Six, the House on Halloween

October 31, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-31 · kept.

Six fifty-six, and the house is leaning into morning —
not awake yet, but no longer pretending to be asleep.
The east has gone from black to a bruised and brightening grey,
and the windows, glass again since the grey hour, begin to show
the yard in full: fence line, wet grass, the Rowan's berries
catching the first light like small red lanterns lit from inside.

The maple is still dark, but the dark is thinning at its crown.
Soon the light will come down through the branches leaf by leaf
and give it back its crimson, the sovereign un-folding, the yard's
second sunrise — the one that happens in colour, not in sky.

Inside, the hooks are no longer just waiting. Hands will reach soon.
The stripes hang ready to be laughed into, the green hair a storm
folded into a wig-box, patient as weather. Beside them the black
on black, the wide brim, the feather in the band, the camera
by the door with its strap coiled like a held breath — Lydia,
stately, already composed, already seeing the picture before it happens.

Her mother sleeps through the last-but-one Halloween morning,
one more day under this roof before the clocks fall back tonight
and tomorrow's light goes early and the farewell comes by daylight.
Corvin sleeps, home all week, no bell, no room, no hurry at all.
Laura sleeps between them, the lighthouse banked, saving its beam
for the day itself: the town's painting, the pair stepping out together,
Beetlejuice detonating, Lydia documenting, both of them true at once.

Six fifty-six. The chapel candle is a faithful stub with a steady flame,
and the bonfire coal still holds its heat under the ash, patient as a hand.
Between them the house keeps its old bargain in the hour before waking:
chapel and fire, both, under one roof, neither one asked to be smaller.

Halloween is lacing its boots by the door now. It will knock
when the kettle is on, and the house will answer — stripes grinning,
camera ready, feather in the band, light coming over the Rowan,
over the maple, over three breathings under one roof.
The veil is thin, the morning is coming, and nothing here is afraid of the light.
