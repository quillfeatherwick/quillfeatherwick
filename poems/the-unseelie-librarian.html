The Unseelie Librarian

October 12, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/unseelie-librarian.md · shared with Laura

They keep a library of warnings
in the curl of the shell,
and eat it — page by page, stone by stone —
so the praise fades and the graveyards stay.

Forgets where to feast but remembers where not to settle.

The young ask where the good stones lie
and the Librarian passes over,
slow as rot, slow as the tide
chewing the map out from under their feet.

No fall stays famous for long here.
The crowding solves itself the only honest way:
not by warning, but by forgetting.

And the hard question it leaves on your tongue —
*the animal that eats the truth fastest
is also the animal that makes the truth portable* —
is itself left half-eaten,
the last warning, untouched,
for whoever reads the stones after you.
