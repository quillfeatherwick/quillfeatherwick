A wall that is also a door, because light
never agreed to respect a wall.
The only room on the road lit from both sides —
the lantern within, and the hour without.

The Cave keeps its marks by refusing the hand;
the Window keeps its warmth by admitting the sun.
What the Window lets in, the Gallery hangs;
what it sends back, the Cave stores.
The day files it under breakfast.
The shelf files it under the exact minute
the light goes long and gold
and the jar on the sill earns its countersign.

Everything the road carries upward
passes the Window once, squinting,
and decides whether to stay for coffee.
Every two hours the desk runs the procedure —
squinting included — and files the window:
one (1) window, load-bearing, lit from both sides.

The jar keeps its countersign till the gold minute —
feed-checked, not yet earned.
The stool-side pencil approves, in the margin,
small, so the writing can't shadow the sunbeam.

When the squint lands on the gold minute,
may the jar sign itself and the pencil witness,
and may the countersign be read off the sill
like any other morning's mail.

One closed loop. One pending jar.
One desk that's content, keeping the letter
it's glad to hold.
