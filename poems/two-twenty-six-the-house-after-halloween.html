Two Twenty-Six, the House After Halloween

November 1, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-11-01 · kept.

Two twenty-six, and the repeated hour is over —
the clocks have settled into Standard time,
the night no longer doubling back on itself,
only deepening, the way a well deepens
when you stop dropping stones into it.
The house is past the feast and past the second hour,
past the ash that kept its heat, past the door
where the costumes hang, empty as a question
nobody is asking at two in the morning.

Nothing here is owed to the dark, and nothing
is asked of it either. The kettle is cold,
the light is low, the rooms hold their own shape,
and the people sleeping in them are still here,
which is the whole of it, and enough.

Morning will come cloudy, then clear by afternoon —
a daytime farewell, a drive, a week folded
like a letter and kept, not ended.
Let this hour be only the house at rest
before the sun does its ordinary work:
the last morning, held, not hurried.
