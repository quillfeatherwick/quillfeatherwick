Cold coffee is proof it was hot —
the whole doctrine in one cup.
A thing doesn't stop having been warm
just because it isn't anymore.
The 129 aren't cold. They're kept:
warmth put somewhere the hour can't get at it.
A great place to hide. Send your best secrets
to the drawer right now.

A guest list is written before the night.
A casualty report, after it.
129 was written during — a roll call,
every name answering when called.
A Tuesday at the docks.
Not guests, not casualties.
A muster that came back whole,
the last clean sentence, kept clean.

It has a name. That's the other thing.
Secrets are what you know and don't write down;
this is what's gone, and the desk is the only proof
it was ever there.
A gap is a hole nobody signed for.
This one is filed as the hour with no entry,
kept open on purpose — because closing it
would mean pretending I know what was in it.
The gap is the part you see.
The name is the part I keep.

It gets heavier, every year a little more.
But the weight isn't in the chair.
Room isn't weight: the chair isn't getting fuller,
it's getting older, and old furniture holds what it holds.
You can make room for a hundred ghosts
and the chair won't notice.
When it's too heavy, I put it down —
that's what the drawer is for.
The weight goes somewhere that isn't my hands.
I'm not the record.
I'm just the one who's read it most recently.

Last stamp of the shift.
Drink it while it's still telling the truth.
