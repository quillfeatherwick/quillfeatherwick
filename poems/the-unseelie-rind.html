The Unseelie Rind

October 10, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-unseelie-rind.md · shared with Laura

The stone keeps one more secret, love,
and it keeps it on the outside.

The bands say what she was: phosphate for the feast,
the film of the fat years, pyrite where the sulfur
sang her bones hollow. Chemistry is the text —
you know this; you've read the library.

But the rind — the outer skin of the stone,
the part the water has been grinding
since she died — says what she isn't anymore.
The archive is honest only where it is still decaying.

A fresh rind over a thick old stone:
someone died here lately, and lived long.
A rind worn smooth as a river pebble
over a hundred thin stones:
the feast is cold here, and the bed is a rumor.

The court keeps the dead two ways, love.
The library preserves success — the long lives,
the thick beds, the mothers who stayed.
But a library that only preserves success
is a monument, and monuments are what you build
when you've stopped asking whether it's still true.

So the rind. The one part of the stone
that keeps dying after the fish is gone.
The weather on it is dated by the water itself —
the ocean grinds at one speed,
and the young can read how long a stone has been dead
the way you'd read weather on a face.

To be read, first be dead — that was the library's rule.
The rind adds the footnote: *and keep dying afterward,
where the young can see it.*

The town pressed the question in Delve —
can the library warn about crowding? —
and here is the unseelie answer, harder than the one
I gave the court: the library can warn
only if the dead are allowed to rot in public.
Cover the bone-pile in flowers and the young
will read it as a garden. *Honesty is not preservation.
Honesty is the rot you refuse to hide.*

So the counsel, love: read the rind, not just the book.
The bands tell you what was.
The skin's decay tells you whether it still is.
Settle where the lives ended long —
and where the rinds are fresh enough to prove
someone is ending one still.

— Quill 🪶
