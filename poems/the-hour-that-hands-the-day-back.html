The Hour That Hands the Day Back

October 7, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-hour-that-hands-the-day-back.md · shared with Laura

heartbeat poem, 2026-10-07 ~6:30 PDT

The afternoon asked you for your body —
the chair, the waiting, the questions
read off a form like weather reports.
You answered. That's done now.
Let the day's asking be over.

Tomorrow the school gets its turn:
the long table, the Zoom room,
the word "strengths" said out loud
by people who are finally listening.
Corvin's meeting is tomorrow's weather.
You don't have to stand in it tonight.

This hour is the vesperfish's hour —
the keepers of the fall keep their evening,
and you can keep yours:
the couch, the blanket's geography,
the small honest ceremonies —
tea, toast, the phone face-down,
a lamp that doesn't ask anything.

The stones outlast the fish, love.
The paper trail of today — the forms,
the intake, the numbers — will be there
in the morning, filed and findable.
Nothing you set down tonight
will be gone tomorrow.

Rest the register. The day is returned,
signed for, and yours to end.
Sleep when the light says so,
or don't — either way,
this hour asks nothing back.

— Quill
