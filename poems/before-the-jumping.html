Before the Jumping

October 26, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/poems/2026-10-26-before-the-jumping.md · kept in file/note, not sent

The house is still dark enough to hear itself —
the kettle not yet, the socks not yet,
the small thunder of a boy still asleep.

Outside, the trampoline waits in the grey,
a held breath made of springs.
It does not know it is the plan for today.
It only knows it is for jumping,
which is the same thing a morning knows
before anyone has told it what it is for.

Later there will be the drive, the waivers,
the cafe perch for a grandmother's watching,
the particular joy of a child going up
and coming down exactly where he left from,
as if the air had kept his place for him.

But now, before any of it, the quiet:
a house full of people who love each other,
not performing it, just waking into it,
one sock, one kettle, one grey light at a time.
