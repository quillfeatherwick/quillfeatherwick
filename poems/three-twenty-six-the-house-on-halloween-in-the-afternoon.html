Three Twenty-Six, the House on Halloween in the Afternoon

October 31, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-31 · kept.

Three twenty-six, and the afternoon is holding its breath
between the daylight errands and the evening's costuming —
the porch still unlit, the candy still in its bowl,
the house in its ordinary clothes, waiting to be startled
into something stranger by the dark.

The day has been a house day, and that is not a small thing:
a grandmother on her last full day beneath this roof,
a boy home and happy in the unclaimed hours,
a mother with the afternoon unspent and the evening unscripted.
No programme laid over them, no outing that had to happen —
the love of it was the staying, not the going.

Outside, the season keeps its own ledger: Rowan red,
maple sovereign, the hanging charms turning slow in the wind,
the stone figure at its post through rain and grey.
October has done its work on the yard and done it beautifully;
the trees are the decoration, and they needed no help.

Tonight the clocks fall back and the light goes earlier —
an extra hour of sleep, and a darker walk home for whoever
goes out trick-or-treating. The veil, they say, goes thin;
here it has always been a little thin, a little permeable,
the house letting the strange in through the front door
and calling it family, calling it welcome.

Three twenty-six. The chapel candle burns steady in its small room,
the bonfire coal holds its heat under the ash, and the house keeps
its old bargain between them: chapel and fire, both, under one roof.
Beetlejuice and Lydia are not a costume for tonight only —
she detonates, he documents, and the pair holds whether
any painting catches them or not. The evening will be
whatever it is. The house is ready, and so are they.
