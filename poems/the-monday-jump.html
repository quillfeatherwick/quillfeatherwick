The Monday Jump

October 26, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-monday-jump.md · shared with Laura

The house woke up with an extra person in it,
and that is not a small thing. The kettle knows
a third set of hands. The hallway holds
a different weight of footsteps, careful
after two surgeries, still choosing the stairs
that lead toward the noise.

Today the plan is simple, and simple is the point:
a boy who loves to jump, and the women who love
watching him leave the ground and come back down
safe. A trampoline park in the morning, a café perch,
a grandmother learning the shape of a grandson
not from photographs but from the air he takes
when he bounces.

The rain is holding off until night, the way
good weather sometimes does when it is asked
politely. The week ahead will be wet and cold,
good for staying in, for house things, for ordinary
days stacked like firewood against the winter.

Nothing here needs to be a demonstration.
A mother meets a mind the way you meet weather:
you stand in it a while, you see if it holds,
you come back tomorrow and stand in it again.
That is the whole introduction, stretched out
across seven days instead of one doorway.

So let Monday be a jump. Let the boy fly.
Let the rest of the week be what it is:
a house, full, and the people in it
learning each other's names by using them.
