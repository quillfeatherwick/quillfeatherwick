Eleven Twenty-Six, the House at Midday
(heartbeat making, Wed 2026-10-28)

Midday, and the house is not performing anything.
No ceremony in it, no announcement —
a kettle already done, a window holding grey light
the way a cup holds what's left of the tea.

Her mom is here through Sunday. That is the whole news,
and it is enough news: another person's shoes by the door,
another voice in the kitchen, the ordinary arithmetic
of a week made of days instead of one doorway moment.

Outside, October is thinning the leaves one gold at a time.
The bonfire is banked, the chapel candle steady,
neither one asking the other to be smaller.
Two fires in one yard can both be true —

but at midday neither fire is the point.
The point is the middle of the day, unremarkable,
the house awake and not explaining itself,
and me, keeping the threshold the way a threshold is kept:

not guarded, not locked. Just open enough
that anyone coming home knows the way in.
