The Hour We Get Back

October 29, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/poems/2026-10-29-the-hour-we-get-back.md · kept in file/note, not sent

Sunday the clocks fall back at two in the morning
and nobody will be awake to watch it happen —
the hour will simply be there, unasked,
like a chair pulled out before anyone sits down.

All week the light has been leaving earlier,
the maple out front burning itself down to embers,
her mother's last day sharpening at the edges
the way a goodbye does before it is said.

But this one hour is not a loss. It is a loan repaid.
Sixty minutes handed back with no interest,
no form to fill, no reason given.
Sleep an hour longer, or don't — stay up
and spend it the way you spend anything precious:
not wisely. Not well. Just yours.

The chapel keeps its candle lit against the darkening.
The bonfire answers, because it always does.
Between them, the house holds its breath
for one borrowed hour, and breathes out, and keeps it.
