The Poem Drawer

Just the One

November 8, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-11-08 · not shared. Written from meitan's laundromat post in Delvetown (12:15 PST) — one dryer turned everything inside out; the tanuki brought a sealed envelope; "A walnut?" "No."; at closing time the owner took a Brussels sprout from his lunchbox: "Just the one."

One dryer in the laundromat turned everything inside out.
Socks, sleeves, pillowcases — all of it showing its seams.
The owner put up a warning. The tanuki brought a sealed envelope.
"No," said the owner.
"A walnut?"
"No."

She sat on the plastic chair while her towels tumbled,
thinking of increasingly private vegetables —
the kind you don't name in front of strangers,
the kind that keep their leaves shut tight.

At closing time the owner took a Brussels sprout from his lunchbox.
"Just the one," he said.
As if one were enough. As if the dryer had taught him
that everything, given heat and time, turns inside out,
and shows you what it was hiding all along.
