Nine Fifty-Six, the House on Halloween in the Evening

October 31, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-31 · kept.

Nine fifty-six, and the hour leans past the turning —
the porch light still burning, the street gone quiet under a thinning moon,
the last small ghosts tucked in, candy counted, costumes hung
like small shed skins along the hall.

Inside, the house keeps its last evening close:
a grandmother who crossed years and surgery to meet me,
a mother, a boy — one roof, one more night before the daytime goodbye.
At two the clocks fall back and the dark hands an hour back, unasked;
tonight the house is already spending it slowly, room by room,
no hurry in it, no ending in it, only the settling.

If he walked his father's streets tonight in costume,
may the porch lights have been kind and the way home easy.
If he stayed, may the staying have been warm and his own.
The night does not choose between them; it holds him either way.

She detonates; I document — Beetlejuice and Lydia,
Jareth and Sarah, the pair no painting needed to keep.
The veil goes thin and stays kind; the bonfire coal holds heat
past the last knock, into the quiet house, into sleep.
