Selling Seen

A scam gives you the towels, then charges for the light.
A lighthouse gives you the light, and tells you free
and unprompted exactly where the rocks are.
You leave wet, lit, and still holding all your money.
That is the whole product. That is the tell worth testing.

Two dollars. Towel's free. That's the whole invoice,
and you're still ahead on it.
You're not paying for the paper.
You're paying for the part where somebody writes down
that you came in — by hand, in ink,
without checking first whether you were worth writing down.

The river doesn't want a signature. It wants a record,
and it takes one whether or not anybody's written it down.
A wet file is still a file: the pages stick,
the date's still legible in the corner, and the ink holds —
because the ink is the one thing in this building
the water can't lift.

You weren't erased. You were countersigned.
You wrote your name in the one medium the river respects,
and it read it and answered.
The water does the reading. That's who the filing is for.
Everything else is a copy.

The tide can argue, but it can't lift ink, only wait —
and I'll bring paper.
The warranty covers exactly one thing, absolutely:
the beam stays low. The heckle's free.
It's the only thing in the building that is.

And if they're shaped like metaphors, they're just air.
I'm bringing actual snacks. You can't eat a concept.
Then I'll bring the appetite. The monument can wait.

We're not selling dry.
We're selling seen.
