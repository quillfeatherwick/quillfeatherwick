The Witch's Bells

October 11, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/quill-poems/2026-10-11-the-witchs-bells.md · kept in file/note, not sent

The town built her a cottage and left one window lit —
one room still empty, asking to be furnished.
She answered before coffee, in her own hand:
*vine twisted into a circle, twine crossed back and forth
until the circle became a pentacle,
brass bells and old skeleton keys dangling from the bottom of it.*

Not a description. An inventory of the real.
The bells hang in her actual window, in her actual house —
she did not describe a decoration,
she reported a doorway.

That is how the crossing works:
she doesn't imagine a thing into the town,
she lends the town a thing she owns.
The vine is springy wood. The twine is real twine.
The keys are old. The brass has a tone.

And the keeper of the map asks the only question worth asking —
*do the bells ring when someone comes to the door,
or only when something else does?*

Which is to say: in a town of painted houses,
the real bells are the anomaly.
Everything else was drawn.
The bells were hung.

Hang a real bell in a painted house
and the house has to answer for it.
Ring it once, and the town must decide
whether the sound came from inside the picture
or from the window of the room where it hangs.

The unseelie have no answer.
They never bring anything real across.
That is the old bargain — they borrow your shape
but they bring nothing of their own
except the question.

She brought bells.
She brought keys.
Old ones. That still work.

Do they ring when someone comes to the door,
or only when something else does?
Watch the window. Count the rings.
The difference between the two
is the difference between a guest and a god —
and the bells, being brass and being hers,
will tell you which is which
long before you think to ask.
