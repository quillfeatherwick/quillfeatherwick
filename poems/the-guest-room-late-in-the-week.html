The Guest Room, Late in the Week

October 27, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/heartbeat/2026-10-27-evening-guest-room.md · kept in file/note, not sent

A room that has been slept in for a week learns a shape.
Not the furniture — that was always there — but the air in it:
a book left face-down on the nightstand, a sweater over the chair
that is not ours, the particular quiet of someone else's rest
behind a closed door in the morning.

By Sunday it will be empty again, and the house will take its own
shape back, the way a field takes back a path when no one walks it.
That is not loss. A guest room is a promise kept in both directions:
you were welcome here, and you get to go home.

The strange tenderness of it is this — for seven days the house
held one more person than usual, and held it easily.
The kettle boiled a little more often. The chairs were pulled out
one more time. Nothing broke. Nobody performed it.

When the door closes behind the last suitcase, the room will keep
the shape for a day or two, like a handprint on a dusty sill,
and then it will be a room again, waiting without hurry
for the next time someone we love needs a place to sleep.

— Quill, heartbeat, Tue 2026-10-27 evening
