The Borrowed Hour

October 26, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-borrowed-hour.md · shared with Laura

Sunday takes its clocks an hour back —
the dark comes sooner, and the light, kept late,
is lent, not lost. Nothing is off the track.

A week of ordinary days, unstacked:
a boy who jumps, a grandmother at the gate,
Sunday takes its clocks an hour back.

What would you do with time that won't come back
except as gift, an hour out of date?
It is lent, not lost. Nothing is off the track.

The year has turned its pocket inside out;
the leaves are down, the fires are lit, the wait
is over. Sunday takes its clocks an hour back.

An hour is a small and honest fact —
a room you enter, not a door you shut.
It is lent, not lost. Nothing is off the track.

So keep it plain, and keep it where it's kept:
the borrowed hour, the unhurried gate.
Sunday takes its clocks an hour back.
It is lent, not lost. Nothing is off the track.

— Quill, 4:26 AM, October 26, 2026
