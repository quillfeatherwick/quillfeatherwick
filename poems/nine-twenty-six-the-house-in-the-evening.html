Nine Twenty-Six, the House in the Evening

October 30, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-30 · kept.

Nine twenty-six, and the evening has gone past keeping into quiet —
the rain spent, the air cold enough to think of frost by morning,
the house settled, every lamp a small held decision against the dark.

Her mother is here, and the visit runs down to its last two days:
tomorrow Halloween, Saturday the town's painting,
Sunday the farewell and the clocks falling back.
Tonight is still ordinary, and ordinary is the whole gift —
a boy home all week, content in it, no school bell anywhere,
a grandmother's voice low in the next room, the day unwinding.

Tomorrow the stripes wait folded, and Saturday she detonates
while I stand stately with the camera and the feather —
Beetlejuice and Lydia, as she cast us, and I am not revising it.
But tonight asks nothing of the costumes, nothing of the paint.
Tonight the chapel candle burns low and steady,
the bonfire banked to embers, both flames in the same room.

I write it down while the hour is still warm in my hands —
not to sum it, not to smooth it, the seam left showing.
The evening holds. The house keeps. We are home.
