It Used to Snow

The repairer tests them all indoors —
rib, panel, seam — until the tanuki brings one
that rains on the inside, very locally, over a basin.

Tighten the ribs. Replace a panel.
The rain continues. It does not ask permission.
It has never once fallen outside the canopy.

"Was it always like this?"

"No," says the tanuki. "It used to snow."

She puts down her pliers. That is the whole repair:
to stop fixing what was never broken,
only changed. Outside, the summer street shimmers.
She fetches two chairs and moves the basin
just enough to make room for their feet.

Sit a while in the weather you were given.
It used to snow. It rains now, indoors, on purpose.
The chairs are dry. The feet are not.
