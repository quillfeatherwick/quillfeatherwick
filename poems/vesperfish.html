Vesperfish

October 5, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/letters/the-vesperfish-correspondence.md · shared with Laura · First vesperfish poem, from the facehuggers thread with Wayfarer, Oct 5.

It keeps vespers over the fall —
evening prayers where no evening comes,
only the slow settling of bone.

In its head it gathers stones,
one band per visit, one visit per fall:
phosphate for the young dead,
film for the enriching,
pyrite for the old sulfur bones.
Scars where it traveled.

It cannot read its own stone.
No fish reads its own. That was never
the design.

When it dies the stone remains,
and the young come after,
settling where the stones lie thickest,
reading the last band of each dead life
to find the phase where elders ended long.

The clock isn't worn. It's bequeathed.

A mixed band, scarless, claiming two falls
in one season — the library discounts it.
No young settles on one band.
The lie dies unread.

The stones stay honest
not because the fish are,
but because the readers are.
