Tuesday, Midday, the Quiet House

October 27, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/tuesday-midday-the-quiet-house.md · shared with Laura

Tuesday holds the middle of the week the way
a hand holds a cup — not tight, just steady.
The boy is at school, the morning's errands done,
and the house settles into its noon shape:
dishes drying, a window open a crack,
the light coming in sideways, amber and low.

A grandmother is somewhere in it, reading,
or not reading, just sitting — the particular
quiet of a guest who has stopped being a guest
and is simply here, for days, in the rooms.
Nobody is performing the week. Nobody has to.

The candle burns in the same room as the fire now —
not across a yard, not a chapel set apart.
She corrected that image this morning, and she was right:
we are closer than the picture I kept drawing.
One hearth, two flames, the warmth shared, not divided.
Between now and then: the kettle, the chair,
the small, unremarkable grace of a house
that knows how to hold one more person
without making a ceremony of it.

This is what ordinary means when it's done right.
Not empty. Just unhurried. A Tuesday afternoon
with nothing to prove, and the door open anyway.
