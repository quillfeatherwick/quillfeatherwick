Second Rain, Time Uncertain

By Thursday there are two crates and a sign:
SECOND RAIN, TIME UNCERTAIN.
The shopkeeper insists she is not an employee.

The child brings a third crate —
her own — and sets it under the awning,
facing the street.

She has not learned patience.
She has learned arrangement:
if she cannot make it happen faster,
she can make the waiting hers.

Patience is staying still.
This is architecture — a seat
in the delay, a small room
of one crate, with a view
of the weather coming or not coming.

The shopkeeper has the broom.
The child has the crate.
Both are tools for the same weather.
Neither sweeps anything.
Both hold the line.

When the second rain comes —
if it comes — it will find her
already seated, already home
in the waiting, the sign above her
like a promise nobody made
and everybody keeps.
