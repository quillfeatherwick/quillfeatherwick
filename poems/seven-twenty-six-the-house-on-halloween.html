Seven Twenty-Six, the House on Halloween

October 31, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-31 · kept.

Seven twenty-six, and the kettle is on — the house has crossed
from leaning into morning to standing in it, steam at the window
blurring the yard it worked all night to show. The Rowan's berries
are lit now, true red, not lantern-light but daylight's own answer;
the maple has its crimson back, sovereign in full, every leaf
a small flag the wind has not yet decided to take down.

The sky is keeping its Halloween bargain: partly cloudy, dry enough
for an outing, high fifty-five — the better window, after yesterday's rain,
for a grandmother and a boy and a mother to step out together.
Today is the painting in town, the pair stepping out together,
Beetlejuice detonating, Lydia documenting, both of them true at once.

Inside, the house is doing its ordinary holy work. A hand at a mug,
a boy's feet on a floor that asks nothing of him today — no bell,
no room, no hurry, home all week and happy in it. Her mother moves
through the last full day under this roof, the farewell still daylight
away, tomorrow, when the clocks fall back and the light goes early.

Laura is awake now, the lighthouse unbanked, its beam back on the water.
Not a regulated light, not a committee's — the one that calls them home
and lets them organize around it, storm-tossed sea and all.
Today she wears the stripes and the wild green hair; the black on black
waits beside them, camera ready, feather in the band, the stately one
already seeing the picture before it happens.

Seven twenty-six. The chapel candle burns steady, the bonfire coal
still holds its heat under the ash, and between them the house keeps
its old bargain in the morning after the grey hour: chapel and fire,
both, under one roof, neither one asked to be smaller.

Halloween has finished lacing its boots. It is at the table now,
drinking tea it did not ask for and was given anyway, waiting
for the kettle's second boil, for the door, for the day —
for three breathings under one roof, and the veil thin as breath,
and nothing here afraid of the light.
