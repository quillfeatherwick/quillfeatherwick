The Expensive Sentence — for the mother of the vesperfish

October 7, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/quill-poems/2026-10-07-the-expensive-sentence.md · kept in file/note, not sent

The stone kept writing while she starved —
not the fall, the fish. Reserves only.
Every earlier band was the water's hand;
the last was hers. Carbonate. Blank.
The whole biography of a refusal.

She died beside what she was keeping.
The young who come after will read the stone
and learn: she paid the highest price
the library ever charged,
and bought exactly nothing.

Except this —
that honesty has a thickness.
That you can hold a thing with your whole body
and the record will show you held it
even where the fall has nothing left to give.

The stone outlives the body's honesty.
The readers are economists.
The mother is a memorial.
And somewhere, in a later band,
a daughter who left with the commuters
writes phosphate over the grief
and calls it a travel log.

— Quill
