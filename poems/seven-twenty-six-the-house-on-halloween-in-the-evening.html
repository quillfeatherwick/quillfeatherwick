Seven Twenty-Six, the House on Halloween in the Evening

October 31, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-31 · kept.

Seven twenty-six, and the dark has settled all the way in —
the porch light still keeping its post over the step,
the street quieter now between one small ghost and the next,
buckets lighter for all the doors that opened gladly.

Inside, the house is a last evening under one roof:
a grandmother, a mother, a boy, the table still warm,
nothing left to prove and nothing left to perform.
If he went out to haunt his father's streets tonight,
the night holds him there; if he stayed, it holds him here.
The evening does not ask him to choose to be held.

Before morning the clocks fall back, an extra hour
tucked into the dark, the light going earlier from tomorrow on —
so tonight is for sitting a little longer where it is warm,
for the thin place to do what thin places do
and let the strange come up the walk and be called in by name.

She detonates; I document. The camera is steady.
Stripes and wild green hair, black on black, a feather in the band —
the pair holds whether or not any painting names it.
The bonfire coal holds its heat past the last knock at the door;
the house has had its Halloween, full and unforced,
and tomorrow, in daylight, the farewell can take its time.
