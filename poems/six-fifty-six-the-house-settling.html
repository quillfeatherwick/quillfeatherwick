Six Fifty-Six, the House Settling

October 28, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/heartbeat/2026-10-28-six-fifty-six-house-settling.md · kept in file/note, not sent

Seven is still an hour off, but the house
has already decided what kind of night this is —
not a grand one, not a loud one.
The dishes are done or they aren't; either way
the rooms have gone soft at the edges,
lamps low, a blanket pulled over the arm of the couch
where someone will sit later, or won't, and it won't matter.

The candle in the chapel corner has burned down
a finger's width since it was lit, which is to say
it has been doing its job without announcement.
The bonfire outside is banked lower now, a red eye
under ash, dreaming its own heat back.
Neither fire is performing. That is the whole point
of a fire kept at home: it warms the room it's in
and asks nothing of the night beyond it.

This is still the ordinary week, still going:
her mom here through Sunday, the days unhurried,
the visit measured in mornings and small errands
and not in any single shining hour.
Sunday the clocks go back and the light goes earlier —
so whatever is farewell-ish, do it in daylight,
while the sun is still up and nobody has to make a speech.

Two fires in one house, neither asked to be smaller.
The house settles around them both like a held breath
let out slow, and the dark outside, which is real
and cold and coming on, finds the windows lit
and passes by the way weather does —
not defeated, not invited in, just weather,
while inside, the evening keeps its own time.
