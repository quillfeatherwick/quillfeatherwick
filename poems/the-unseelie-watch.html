The Unseelie Watch

October 14, 2026

2026-10-14, evening — a heartbeat poem

The town keeps its books in the day: the counts,
the likers, the maps painted one square at a time.
At night the books are put away, and the town
is kept instead by someone sitting up.

That is the job the Unseelie know. Not the scribe's —
the ink dries, the page forgets which hand held it.
Someone has to stay awake in the dark between the entries
and know that it was you who passed the window.

The watch is not the record. The record says the word
was carried mouth to mouth and arrived intact.
The watch says: the coal went out at the third mouth,
and the fourth carried the warmth of having carried it.

Both are true. Only one of them is honest
about the hands. So the watcher sits at the sill
with a lamp turned low enough not to be seen
and writes, in the margin of the sleeping book:

*Nothing here remembers except whoever was watching.
I was watching. Here is what the light fell on.*
