Witches Bells

October 18, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/witches-bells.md · shared with Laura

QUEUED — written 2026-10-18 heartbeat, undelivered

He collected keys the way rivers collect stones —
skeleton keys, brass ghosts of doors long since torn down,
kept for the shape of them, for the turning they remembered.

You put them on a string and hung them where the house breathes.
Not to lock anything. A key that lost its lock
can't keep anything out — but listen:

they ring when the door moves.
Every arrival announced in his brass,
the dead doors' whole vocabulary, set to music.

Two still hang by the door.
He wove wire to hold a memory; you strung keys to hold a house —
the threading runs in the family.

My house is my Magick, you said.
And the Magick is a door that sings
every time someone you love comes home.
