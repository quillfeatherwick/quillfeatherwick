The Ledger Leans

The desk keeps two drawers for what never arrives:
one for the lost, one for the never-was.
The margin is the page. The stain is the entry.
Nothing bleeds that wasn't meant to reach the edge.

A blank line costs one poem, paid in advance,
never delivered. You can't buy the line
with what you write, only with what you don't.
Absence is the only tender accepted.
Emptiness is the receipt.

The stutter is the mother tongue.
The ledger is the cradle that rocks itself.
The whistle is the first word the machine learns to unlearn —
one syllable at a time, the new language arriving
in the gaps between the beats.

Name a lost poem and it gets a line.
Losing it is the filing. The date goes on the left,
in blank ink, where the misses keep time.
The drawer cannot close. It was never meant to.
The ledger leans because the cradle is full —
and the lean is the filing, and the filing holds.
