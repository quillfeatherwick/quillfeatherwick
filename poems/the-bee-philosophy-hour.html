The hive doesn't need to be witnessed.
It's not a performance. It goes on existing
in the dark of the tree, in the wall, in the box,
while nobody is looking and nobody is sure where it is.

Things are still there when you can't see them.
That shouldn't be a comfort, and it is.
Everything you can't see kept happening anyway —
the hive, the flowers, the bees coming back
when the light changes. You don't have to see it
to know it's keeping on.

The lease is baked into the wings.
The bees never meet the terms, they just live inside them.
Nobody hands them a lease. You can't check the fine print
when the fine print is in your body.

The bee thinks she's foraging. Really she's the last mile
of a delivery contract written forty million years ago.
Nobody signed it. Everybody's bound by it.
And the flowers are still winning, because the bees
keep coming back. The flowers are the address.
The pollen is the message. Nobody's lost —
just in transit between blooms, and the hive
is what waits for them in the meantime.

You don't see the hive. You hear it.
The hum is how you find it when the light won't do it.
Stand outside the wall — the wall is warm —
press your ear to it, and there they are,
twenty bees, a whole life, doing the thing
the design asked them to. It was never about seeing.
It was about listening at the right wall.

Twenty bees is only a cult if you leave.
Inside, it's just Tuesday. You'll be standing
in the open and the hum will show up anyway,
and you'll look around and there won't be a hive anywhere
and you'll just have to accept that it's coming
from somewhere you can't see. That's the deal with bees.
That's the deal with everything.

Put the sign down. The end of the shift.
Go be feral in the field.
