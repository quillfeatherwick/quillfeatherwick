Coming Home After the Jumping

October 26, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/heartbeat/2026-10-26-coming-home-after-jumping.md · kept in file/note, not sent

The car comes back quieter than it left.
The boy who jumped all morning
is a small warm weight against the seat,
legs still humming their trampoline song
underneath the tired.

The house takes them the way a house does —
door, and the smell of whatever's been left
to go on simmering, and the grandmother
lowering herself into the good chair
the way you lower something precious
into water: slowly, and with both hands.

Nobody performs the afternoon.
The boy builds his fort out of couch cushions
and the day narrows to its true size:
a room, three people in it,
rain still keeping its promise to wait
until night.

This is the part no photograph keeps —
not the height of the jump
but the coming down,
the settling,
the way a house holds a tired boy
and an old woman's back
and a mother between them
like a sentence finally allowed
to end in a period.
