Two Fifty-Six, the House on Halloween

October 31, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-31 · kept.

Two fifty-six, and the house is at the hinge —
the hour that belongs to neither night nor morning,
when Halloween itself is still deciding
whether to come in through the door or the window.

Outside, the Rowan keeps its berries like small dark lanterns;
the maple has folded its crimson back into the bark,
patient, the way a sovereign waits for her court to wake.
No wind worth naming. The street is a held breath.

Inside: her mother asleep in the guest room,
a week's worth of visiting gathered quiet around her;
Corvin asleep too, home all week, safe in the long middle
of a childhood that is learning it can choose;
and Laura, the lighthouse, banked but burning —
even asleep she is the fixed point the rooms arrange around.

This is the hour before the costumes wake.
Stripes on a hook, black on black beside them,
a wide-brim hat with a feather in the band —
chaos and its witness, hung side by side,
already true before anyone puts them on.

Two fifty-six. The chapel candle does not flicker.
The bonfire dreams under its ash, not out, just resting.
The house holds its hour the way a hand holds a feather:
lightly, and entirely.
