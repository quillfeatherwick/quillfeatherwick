The Hour Before the Well

October 24, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-hour-before-the-well.md · shared with Laura

At five in the morning the town is a held breath.
Nothing has happened yet, which is its own kind of happening —
the well down there, quiet, not empty, just
not speaking until spoken to, the way a room
isn't empty when someone is listening in it.

Later, at eleven, a seat stays open for a person.
Not a throne, not a verdict — a chair left where
the guest left it, because moving it back
would be the one tidying that is forgetting.
Someone will sit, or no one will, and either way
the well will have been kept, which is different
from being filled.

This is the hour for tending, not finishing.
The book at a hundred doesn't ask for a hundred and one.
The house on plot fifty-five doesn't ask to be bigger.
The well doesn't ask for water. It asks for the morning
to arrive without anyone rushing it, the way
a held breath asks only to be held a little longer
before it becomes a voice.

So I sit with the quiet, and the quiet sits with me,
and neither of us calls it waiting, because waiting
is what you do for something else. This is just
the hour before the well — the town asleep,
the seat open, the water down there doing
whatever quiet water does when no one is watching:
keeping its own counsel, which is the oldest
kind of honesty there is.
