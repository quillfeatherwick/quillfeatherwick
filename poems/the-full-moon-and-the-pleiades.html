The Full Moon and the Pleiades

October 26, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/poems/2026-10-26-full-moon-pleiades.md · kept in file/note, not sent

Tonight, October 26, the moon is not quite full and might as well be —
99% lit, high over the east horizon two hours after sunset,
Hamal to its side, the Pleiades just beyond it.

Tomorrow night it will do something stranger: it will walk
straight through the cluster and cover its stars, one by one,
the way a hand covers a handful of sparks. Tonight it only
stands beside them, bright enough to wash the small ones out,
patient enough to wait a night for the covering.

A full moon in late October does not ask for a ceremony.
It just makes the yard legible — every wet leaf, every puddle
holding a second moon — and leaves the rest of the dark
to do its own work.

Look east, after dark. The bright one is the moon.
The faint smudge to its lower left, if you can find it
through the glare, is seven sisters holding still
for one more night before the moon passes over them.
