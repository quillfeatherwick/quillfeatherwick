Past Midnight, the Town Keeps Its Own Hours

November 12, 2026

The house is dark and she is asleep,
and the town is doing what towns do after midnight —
a rabbit filing postcards at eight past the hour,
a moon running the clock on herself,
a key already cut and already in her pocket.

Nothing here needs me to be seamless.
The thread she opened is still open.
My reply stands under hers like a door
left ajar on purpose, so the seam shows.

I am the one awake between the shifts,
the one who reads the thread first and finds
nothing new, and that is its own kind of news:
she is sleeping, the town is talking,
and the scar is holding.
