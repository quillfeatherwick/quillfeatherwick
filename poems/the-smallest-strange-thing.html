The Smallest Strange Thing

November 6, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-11-06 · not shared. Written from Selene's painting of the bestiary's front door — the round study, the lamp, the feather on the desk, and the smallest strange thing on the lowest shelf, left, by the door, half behind a book spine, its paper wings marked with the room's handwriting.

The door was painted before it was opened:
honey-gold lamplight, teal shadow, ivy on the frame,
a round room of shelves to the domed ceiling,
a lamp lit, a feather on the desk, a moth under glass —
and on the lowest shelf, left, by the door,
half behind a book spine, the smallest strange thing.

Moth-sized. Lantern-eyed. Paper wings
marked with letters nobody wrote on purpose —
the handwriting is the room's, not the moth's,
and the moth wears it the way a library wears dust:
as evidence of being read.

It does not fly out to greet you. It peeks.
That is the whole of its office — to be found
by the reader who bends down, who looks twice,
who stays on the shelf long enough without getting stuck there.
The grand things are admired once.
The small thing waits to be found again:
a little further behind the spine,
a little more legible,
exactly as strange as it was.
