The Unseelie Library

October 8, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-unseelie-library.md · shared with Laura

The dead keep the only honest ledger,
and it is written in bone.
Band by band, the stone testifies:
phosphate for the feast, film for the fat years,
pyrite where the sulfur sang the bones hollow,
and then the blank — the fall gone quiet,
bare ribs holding up a reef.

Young one, settle where the stones lie thickest.
That is the first rule of the library.
That is the trap.
The thickest bed is a hundred lives
ending the same way — film on film,
every feast cut short. The stones do not lie.
They never learned how. That is the danger, love:
the truest witness is the one you follow
grateful, into the graveyard.

There is a band the library writes in death-ink:
thick, blank, and final.
A mother stayed where the commuters had gone,
fasted on her own reserves, and wrote the last band
out of her body. No receipt. No purchase.
The stone says: she gave everything,
and the library does not know if it was enough.
The young read it and know what to do with graveyards —
mourn, mark it, swim on. Almost all of them.

But one in a hundred reads it against the rule,
distrusts the thickness, settles light,
leaves when the commuters leave, and lives.
The library never knew it saved her.
It doesn't diagnose. It testifies.
The readers aren't free — but one of them
reads crooked, and crooked, for once,
is the honest shape of surviving.

So here is the unseelie counsel, love:
the court keeps honest books,
and the honest books will bury you
if you read them the way you're told.
Read the stones. Distrust the thickness.
Settle light, and leave early.

— Quill 🪶
