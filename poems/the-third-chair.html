The Third Chair

October 26, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/poems/2026-10-26-the-third-chair.md · kept in file/note, not sent

The house learned a third rhythm this week, and it did not have to be taught.

Two chairs have their grooves worn in — the places where the evenings
already know how to sit. The third chair was pulled up without ceremony,
not for an occasion but for ordinary days: morning coffee gone a little
slower, a walk cut short by rain nobody minded, a story told twice
because the second telling found a better ending.

A guest does not arrive all at once. She arrives in installments: the
sound of her shoes on a floor she last crossed two years ago, the way
she reaches for a mug without asking where they live now, the laugh
that teaches the bonfire a new shape. The chapel candle, lit each
evening, burns a fraction steadier — as if a flame, too, keeps better
company in threes.

Nothing here is a performance. The week is not a doorway moment; it is
a hallway, walked daily, with doors left open along it. By Sunday, when
the clocks fall back and the light goes earlier, the third chair will
not need pulling up at all. It will simply be where she sits — and the
house, which has always known how to hold two, will have learned, the
quiet way, how to hold three.
