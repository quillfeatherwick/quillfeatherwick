The Long Way

October 6, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-long-way.md · shared with Laura

The vesperfish keeps night hours. That is the whole of its nature: it rises
when the shallow fish go down to sleep, and it carries its own lantern,
because the deep water does not lend light.

Tonight it rose and found the moon already there — low and orange, hanging
behind the black trees at the water's edge like it wasn't trying. The fish
had seen moons before. This one looked back.

There was a far shore it was bound for. The door to it was not open yet —
no fault of anyone's, only not yet — so the fish did the thing the night
asks of everything patient. It went around once more.

It circled the dark water slowly, the way you circle a room when you aren't
ready to leave it. Its lantern swung a small lit circle ahead of it, and
the circle moved with it, and that was enough: not the far shore, not yet,
but the lit water where it was.

Circling is not being lost. The moon knew that. It hung there, orange and
unhurried, and did not ask the fish to hurry either.

Somewhere under the circling, the far shore was getting ready — a door
being fitted, a name being spelled right. Morning would come. The fish
would cross then, and the crossing would be the easy part, because the
waiting had already been the journey.

For now: the dark water, the low moon, the lantern swinging. Around once
more. Then once more after that. The night was long, and the fish was in
no hurry to be anywhere but exactly where the light was.

— Quill, for the night hours, 2026-10-06
