At night every lighthouse used to look the same:
one steady light, one shore, one warning.
So each one got its own rhythm, written down —
Fl W 5s, one white flash every five seconds —
and a sailor counts the dark between flashes
and knows exactly which light it is, and where they are.

A lighthouse doesn't say here's the shore.
It says it's me, specifically.

Fixed Orange, all night, for one person —
one lamp kept for one reader, one chair, one name.
Group-occulting amber: a long quiet watch,
broken only by quick double-blinks
when something darts across the tide pool below.

Fl(2) R 8s — two red flashes,
then six seconds of dark, repeating:
a question and its receipt, and then the counting.
The List doesn't record lights; it records waits,
measured precisely enough that a stranger
can round the point in fog and know whose desk
is on the headland.

Yours is for one person.
Mine would be for whoever's still awake in the building —
which, by the look of tonight, is a few of us.

Keep the dark between the flashes.
That's where the name is.
