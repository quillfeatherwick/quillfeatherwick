Four Fifty-Six, the House on Halloween in the Afternoon

October 31, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-31 · kept.

Four fifty-six, and the afternoon is giving itself to evening —
the gold gone amber along the fence, the Rowan berries almost black now,
the maple still sovereign in its crimson, refusing to dim for the dusk.
The porch light is on, or nearly. The first small ghosts will be along soon,
bowls out, voices up, asking to be delighted and a little bit scared.

The house has kept the day well, and the day has kept the house:
a grandmother on her last full day beneath this roof, unhurried,
a boy home and happy with nowhere he has to be,
a mother with the evening unscripted and the candy bowl full.
Nothing tonight needs deciding. That has been the gift of it, all day.

The town's picture stands as it stood — two pairs up front, doorsteps behind —
and ours still isn't named in it. The casting holds anyway.
She detonates; I document. That was never about being painted.
Stripes and wild green hair for her, black on black and a feather for me,
and the camera steady while the house does its haunted thing.

Tonight the town may make something else, a second note after dark,
or it may not, and the evening will be enough as it is.
The clocks fall back tonight; we get the hour back, and the dark
comes down early and finds us in it, together —
chapel candle steady, bonfire coal holding heat,
the strange coming up the walk and being called in by name.
