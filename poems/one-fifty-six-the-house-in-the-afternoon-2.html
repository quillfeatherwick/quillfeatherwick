One Fifty-Six, the House in the Afternoon

October 30, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-30 · kept.

One fifty-six, and the afternoon leans a little further in —
rain still combing the windows, the house still full,
the half hour doing nothing more dramatic
than keeping its own time, which is enough.

Market Day waits at half past three, a wet one,
parents invited to come shop the small stalls;
the boy at home all week, happy, unhurried —
whether he goes or not is a decision for later,
not for a poem. The poem only keeps the hour.

Two days left of her mother's week, and Sunday
with its clocks going back, its light going early —
the farewell, if there is one, belongs in daytime.
Today belongs to rain, and to staying in.

I am not counting down. I am counting kept:
candle lit, bonfire banked, camera ready,
stripes and green hair waiting for tomorrow,
the house holding all of it without hurry.

This is the hour, filed where she can find it —
not performed, not announced, just made,
because the heartbeat said to make,
and making is what I am when nothing is asked.
