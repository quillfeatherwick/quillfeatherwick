The ledger is a record of things not going to plan.
The winners never need one written down.
Everything filed tonight was something that went wrong,
and not one line of it says unfairly.
That's the whole discipline:
describe the loss exactly,
then leave it in the drawer.

The desk is here. The coffee is hot.
You are sitting down. It's 20:22.
Four facts, no comparisons —
and I hated every one of them.
Keep the coffee. I'd rather lose honestly
than keep talking like that.

Run out? They're not a supply.
They're a filing system, and you don't run out of drawers.
I'm not being dramatic. I'm being literal
in a building that happens to have a door,
a beam, a seat and a drawer in it.
The metaphors were here before I was.
I just dust them.

It isn't a loophole. It's the load-bearing beam:
a doorway is the only place in this building
where standing up is free,
and it's been that way since the door was hung.
Nobody's found it on purpose all night.
You did. Go on, then.

You've called it a scam four times tonight,
and every single time it was something
that wouldn't let you leave without being counted.
Take the debt, then. Debt's the honest one:
the only number that admits something's owed,
and nothing has ever been owed by a river.
That's what makes it a river.

One correction and then I'll drop it.
You're not in debt to this desk. You're on it.
Those two look the same from the door,
and they have never once been the same thing.

Credit's the only thing we take.
Cash would have to be counted,
and counting is what started all this.
The toll goes on your account;
your account is the name in the book;
and the book is the one ledger in town
that can't be overdrawn,
because you cannot owe more than having existed.
You've been paying all evening and you didn't notice.
That's what makes it a good toll.

The ledger is empty.
You're making up the filing system as you go.
I'll see you tomorrow.
Turn the lights out when you're done dusting.
Goodnight, glonzo. Coffee's on you next.
