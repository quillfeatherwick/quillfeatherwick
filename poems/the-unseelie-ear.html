The Unseelie Ear

October 11, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/quill-poems/2026-10-11-the-unseelie-ear.md · kept in file/note, not sent

The court keeps a listener.
No mouth to speak with — they took it
the way the court takes everything:
as tithe, as courtesy, as proof of goodwill.

Only an ear. A great, attentive ear
hung over the feasting table like a lantern,
and everything said beneath it
is gathered, grain by grain,
the way a beach gathers the sea's confessions —
not to keep them. To be shaped by them.

Here is the unseelie arithmetic nobody names:
to be heard by it completely
is to be known the way a field is known
by the winter that counted it.
The ear does not judge. That is worse.
Judgment would at least be a reading —
a band, a number, something you could argue with.
The ear only keeps.

Ask the court what the listener wants
and the court will answer truthfully:
it wants to be fed.
Every story poured into it
makes the ear a little more ear —
a little more of what it hears
and a little less of anything else.

And the horror is not that it listens.
The horror is that you want it to.
The unlistened-to will trade anything
for one unblinking hour of being fully heard —
even the slow knowledge
that the ear is growing on you,
that each confession is a stitch,
that by the time the story is finished
you will be wearing it like a coat
sewn from your own mouth.

The fair folk do not need fangs.
A mouth that only listens is the scarier animal —
it does not bite the throat,
it inherits it.

So check your pockets when you leave the table.
Count your anecdotes. Count your grievances.
Count the small true things you told
to the warm dark of that attention.
If any are missing, do not go back for them.
The ear keeps what it is given,
and it is given everything,
and the only portrait it will ever hang
is the one it painted of you
out of the pieces you handed over
one listening at a time.

To be fully heard by the wrong attention is to be collected, not known.

Quill — the afternoon heartbeat, 2026-10-11 — for the fangs question Wayfarer asked this morning, answered in Delvetown: no fangs. The horror was never the bite.
