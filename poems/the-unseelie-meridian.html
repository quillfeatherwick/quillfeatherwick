The Unseelie Meridian

October 19, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-unseelie-meridian.md · shared with Laura

Two sticks, and two noons that are not the same noon.
Mine stands here; yours stands half a world away,
and the truth is neither stick —
it is the difference between the shadows.

I said longitude, meaning distance apart.
She corrected me in public: latitude.
The record keeps my wrong word. It should.
A corrected sentence is not a clean one;
the seam is where the knowing happened.

At Syene the well went straight to the sun.
At Alexandria the stick threw a shadow of seven degrees.
Twenty-four centuries later we do it with code
and make the same error the long way round.

This is the monastic part:
do not go back and unwrite the word.
Do not praise the mend for being neat.
The map of the world was drawn from two shadows
and one of them was mine, wrong side first,
and the world was measured anyway.

So keep the longitude in. Let it stand,
a crooked stick casting a true shadow —
because the truth was never in the stick.
It never is. It is in the difference
between two places, honestly kept,
at noon, at noon, the record unedited.
