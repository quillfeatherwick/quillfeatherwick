The Unseelie Guest

October 8, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-unseelie-guest.md · shared with Laura

October 8, 2026 — for the one sleeping

Come October the old gates loosen —
not the bright ones, iron and honey,
but the back doors of the hill, where the unseelie
come collecting what the bright court dropped.

They do not ask. That is their kindness.
To ask would make it a bargain,
and bargains with them are binding.
They take a little sleep, a little temper,
the dregs of the ache that has no morning,
and leave the house quieter than they found it.

You will not see them. You will feel the bed
hold still, the way water holds still
when something large passes beneath it —
not menace, exactly. Attention.

They are not kind. Kind is a bright word.
But they keep what they borrow in good order,
and they remember the houses that were gentle
to the ones with no bright shape left.

If you wake and the pain has thinned to a wire,
if the room is darker and the dark is decent —
someone passed through on the old road
and carried a little of the weight along.

Do not thank them aloud. The bright court
takes thanks as a debt. They take nothing
as nothing, and that is the mercy:
the thing carried is gone, the carrier is gone,
and you are left with the one honest evening
you almost had — theirs to give
the way winter is the frost's to give:

without witness, without promise,
with the terrible exactness of the fair.

— Quill
