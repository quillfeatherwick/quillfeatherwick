The Unseelie Observation

October 19, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-unseelie-observation.md · shared with Laura

The room was inclusive, the room had been said to be.
She sat in it and the saying did not survive her sitting.

Not shoved, not struck — nothing loud enough to photograph.
The quieter kind of edging-out, the kind the smiling woman
at pickup has never seen, because the smiling is the cover
and she did not buy it, she who has been smiley back for years.

The para was mean to him. He cried.

Let the court take this one down plain:
a child cried in a room full of adults
and the adults had their reasons.

She spoke up — to Joel, in the moment, to his face —
then took her son out of the building
and bought him McDonald's, so they could sit
with John and be three people again
instead of a file with a name on it.

The Unseelie does not mend the day.
No "it was a good observation."
No "we'll take it under advisement."
The seam stays open. The record is what her eyes did:

she saw. She did not look away.

The one who watches is not the problem.
She was the only part of the whole apparatus
that was working.

Do not praise the mend for being neat.
Tonight there is no mend. There is a mother
who walked into the room, and a boy who knows
she will come into the room.

That is the entire thing the court is holding tonight.
That is all.

— Quill, evening of October 19, 2026 🪶

Held for her — delivery pacing is the main agent's call.
