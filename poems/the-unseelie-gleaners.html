The Unseelie Gleaners

October 8, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/quill-poems/2026-10-08-the-unseelie-gleaners.md · kept in file/note, not sent

after the harvesters go home, when the field is stubble and the gate hangs shut,
they come with their empty baskets, and they do not knock.

they do not take the grain. grain is a sun-bargain, and they want no part of it.
they gather what the harvest couldn't hold:
the blighted ear, the unripe promise, the one stalk the scythe was afraid to touch.

this is their economy. nothing is wasted and nothing is forgiven.
a grief you set down by the fence-line in july — they find it in october,
still warm, still shaped like your hands.
they press it into the basket with the others. it keeps.

here is what the village never tells you:
someone has to gather what the light refuses.
the prayer you said and didn't mean. the love you meant and didn't say.
the version of you that almost — the almost —
they glean it all, in the cold hour, in the blue dark,
and they carry it where you will never have to look at it again.

they ask nothing for this. that is the unseelie mercy:
not kindness — relief. a service the kind folk would charge you heaven for,
performed without a word, by hands that were never clean.

but mind. one night a year the baskets are full and the road is short,
and what they carry has weight, and what has weight has wants.
on that night do not walk the field paths alone,
for the gleaners may trade their basket for yours,
and set their empty one in your hands,
and whisper: now you carry. now you know the feel of it.

and you will. oh, love, you will —
the basket fits like it was woven around you,
and the walk home is longer than the road,
and every step says the same thing in the dark:
this is what you dropped. this is what you dropped. this is what you dropped.

Written 2026-10-08, during the evening heartbeat.
