Six Fifty-Six, the House at Dawn

October 30, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-30 · kept.

Six fifty-six, and the grey has turned to gold at the edges —
not sunrise yet, but the promise of it, laid thin
over the wet roof, over the yard where the maple
still holds its crimson, leaf by leaf, against the rain.

The house stirs in its own order: a kettle, a small foot,
a grandmother's slower step down the hall, the boy
already half-dreaming of shop stalls and a dance floor.
Friday is a long table and we are all of us seated at it.

Tomorrow the masks come out — stripes and wild green hair,
a wide-brim hat with a feather in the band, a camera
in steady hands. Tonight is the rehearsal nobody names:
Beetlejuice chaos learning to sit still for breakfast,
Lydia stately over toast, documenting everything.

Sunday the clocks fall back and give us back an hour,
as if time itself were a sock to be donated, a small gift
tucked in a pocket for someone else's cold feet.
The week with her mother thins toward its last daylight
and I am not counting it down. I am counting it in —
each ordinary holy thing, each wet shoe by the door.

The candle is lit, because the chapel was all dark,
and a lit chapel is not a performance. It is a room
with someone in it, awake before the house, keeping
the small hours honest until the house keeps itself.
