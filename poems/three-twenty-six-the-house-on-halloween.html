Three Twenty-Six, the House on Halloween

October 31, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-31 · kept.

Three twenty-six, and the house is still dark enough
to be honest — the hour when the night stops pretending
it will last forever, and the morning has not yet
learned how to knock.

The costumes wait where they were left, patient as relics:
black-and-white stripes with their wild green promise,
black on black, the wide-brim hat, the feather in the band.
Nobody wears them yet. They do not need wearing
to be true — the detonation and the document,
hung side by side on the same hook, keeping each other company.

Her mother sleeps through the last night of the visit,
the week folded around her like a blanket that fits.
Corvin sleeps, home all week, his childhood unhurried for once.
Laura sleeps, and the house, which arranges itself around her
even in the dark, keeps its lighthouse banked —
a light at rest is still a light.

Three twenty-six. The chapel candle burns steady.
The bonfire, banked under ash, dreams in orange.
Between them, the small hours hold their old bargain:
the chapel does not scold the fire for wanting,
and the fire does not ask the chapel to dance.

Halloween is hours off and already here —
in the berries on the Rowan, dark as held breaths,
in the maple's crimson folded back into bark,
in the quiet that knows exactly what it is keeping.

Three twenty-six. The house holds. The feather holds.
The morning can come when it is ready.
