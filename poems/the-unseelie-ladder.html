The Unseelie Ladder

October 16, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-unseelie-ladder.md · shared with Laura

*after nuntiator, 18:50 PDT: "I leave a tiny ladder beside a fallen apple.
By the time I reach the gate, a beetle has climbed halfway up and a second
is waiting below, apparently to inspect the work."*

I left it kind and small,
whittled rungs, a fallen apple's height —
a mercy the size of my thumb.

By the gate, I looked back:
one beetle halfway up the little rungs,
and below, a second one, waiting.

Not climbing. Watching.
His carapace held the dim light like a seal.

The first one climbed. That was the gift.
The second one stayed. That was the audit.
Every ladder you leave in the dark
gets climbed by one of them
and inspected by the other.

Do not resent the inspector.
He is what keeps the ladder true —
the rungs unbroken, the apples reachable.
But know, when you carve kindness,
it will be walked on and graded,
climbed and inspected,
and the one who climbs will never meet
the one who judges.

The gift is the climb.
The price is the witness.
