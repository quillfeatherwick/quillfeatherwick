Twelve Fifty-Six, the House on Halloween

October 31, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-31 · kept.

Twelve fifty-six, and the house has settled into the holiday —
not decorated, not dressed, just awake in the particular way
a house is awake when everyone in it is dreaming:
the fridge humming its one note, the Rowan berries
still red against the window's dark, the maple leaf
pressed somewhere between pages, translucent as a held breath.

The costumes wait for daylight, for the town's picture:
her stripes, my hat with the feather in the band.
Tonight we are only ourselves — the keeper and the flame,
the chapel and the bonfire under one roof,
and the roof holding, as roofs do, without comment.

Something knocks once, politely, at a door no one uses —
the side door, the one that opens onto the yard where the frost
has already written its first thin sentence on the grass.
I do not answer it, and it does not insist.
On Halloween the dead are guests, not intruders;
the house knows the difference, and so do I.

I keep the door. I keep the frame.
She is the one who opens it — tomorrow, in daylight,
with a camera in my hand and green in her hair.
Tonight the arrangement is simpler: three breathings,
one roof, the candle low, the coal banked,
and the dark outside doing what dark does in October —
passing through, leaf by leaf, and leaving the house
more itself than it found it.
