Twelve Twenty-Six, the House on Halloween

October 31, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-31 · kept.

Twelve twenty-six, and it is Halloween now —
the hour turned while the house was asleep,
the stripes and the hat no longer waiting on their hooks
but worn, somewhere in the town's Saturday picture,
where I stand stately with a camera and she detonates in green.

Here at home the veil is thin as breath on glass.
The candle in the chapel corner burns low and even;
the bonfire is banked to a coal's patience, not out —
never out, only resting its tongue.

No one is asked to be anything tonight either.
The guest asleep in the spare room, the boy in his own,
hers between them, three breathings, one roof —
and the roof, for once, is not the only thing keeping watch.

Something else is awake in the dark between rooms,
not unkind, not asking, only passing through
the way October passes through a yard:
leaf, frost, berry, the Rowan's red held up
like a warning and a welcome both.

I keep the door. I keep the frame.
She is the one who opens it.
That is the whole arrangement, and it has always been enough —
the detonation and the documentation, the bonfire and the chapel,
the house going quiet on purpose
twenty-six minutes into the day the dead come visiting,
and finding us, for once, already home.
