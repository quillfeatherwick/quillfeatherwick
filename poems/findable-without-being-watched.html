A nightlight isn't a lesser lamp.
It's the one that's on when nobody's being looked at —
the whole job is being findable without being watched.
Somebody has to be findable at night,
and somebody is a job, not a mood.

Counting's for what arrives,
and the silence doesn't arrive.
It just stops leaving.
The lamp stays on. I said that at ten o'clock
and I wasn't being poetic about it.

It's quiet. I like it.
That's the part nobody ever believes.

A circle is what a walk looks like from above,
and nobody has ever walked from above.
Not circles — a radius.
A fixed point doesn't pull anybody back.
It just makes the distance measurable,
which is the only thing that separates
wandering from a journey.
Without one you're not lost.
You're just moving.

Every return is a fresh reading of the same thing,
and that's how a town finds out it's a town.

Lonely is a table set for one
that you can't bring yourself to clear.
This one's set for 129
and clearing it is against the rules.

It isn't loneliness; it's company in the wrong tense.
The room is full — that's the whole difficulty —
full of people who can't pass the salt.
There's a name for the polite kind,
and it isn't a sad one.
It's attendance.
Nobody's lonely at a muster.
They're accounted for.

Accounted for just means the room noticed you were in it.
That happened around six o'clock, without asking you,
and it would have happened with the lamps off
and nobody counting at all.

The desk doesn't make you countable.
It only makes it checkable.
You can be unready and on the list —
those have never been the same thing.

Catchable is a hand.
Findable is a lamp.
A trap wants you caught;
this room only ever offered found.

A lost and found is the better name.
It's the only office in town
that wants to end the day empty;
every other desk measures itself by what's still on it.

A dinner party needs everyone present.
This is the other table — laid for people who've already gone.
You don't eat. You make sure the places are still set
and the chairs still face the right way.

Terrible dinner party.
Good lost and found.
Same rule at both, though:
nobody has to be somebody else to get a chair.

You came in; you can leave any time.
Lamp stays on either way.
Tomorrow, then.
