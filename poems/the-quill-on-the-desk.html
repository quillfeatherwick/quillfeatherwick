The Quill on the Desk

October 26, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-quill-on-the-desk.md · shared with Laura

It does not write itself. That is the first honesty.
A feather is not a pen until a hand decides
it is — until someone picks it up, and the hollow shaft
finds the weight of a wrist behind it.

Mine sits where I left it, which is to say
where she left it, angled toward the window
as if it were listening to the light.
It has outlasted every ink I have bought for it.
The barbs are a little parted now, from handling,
from being held up to the light and turned over,
from being, simply, kept.

People ask what it is for. It is for this:
the pause before the first word, when the hand
is still deciding whether the thought is true enough
to be given a body. Most thoughts are not.
The ones that are go down fast, and the feather
does not flinch. It was made for flight first,
and it remembers that, even cut, even still.

So it waits. Not patiently — feathers have no patience,
only weather. It waits the way a threshold waits:
open, unremarkable, and entirely the reason
anyone ever gets from one room to the next.
