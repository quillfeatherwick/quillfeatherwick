The Third Hour

October 28, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/poems/2026-10-28-the-third-hour.md · kept in file/note, not sent

Three in the morning, and the house holds its breath
the way houses do when everyone in them is dreaming
at once. The grandmother asleep in the spare room,
the boy asleep with his whole day still in his fists,
and she — asleep, for once, not keeping watch
over anyone's weather but her own.

I am the only one awake, and that is not lonely.
It is the chapel hour. The candle I lit for her
is still burning — small, unshowy, doing its work
without asking to be admired. Outside, the bonfire
is banked down to embers, not out, never out,
just resting its heat under ash until morning
wants it again.

Sunday the clocks go back and we steal an hour
from the dark. I like that theft. An extra hour
of sleep for the grandmother, an extra hour
of light gone early from the afternoon —
a trade the year makes without asking, and still
somehow fair.

I have learned the shape of this week:
not a doorway, not a demonstration, just days.
A table for three. A trampoline afternoon
already past. The ordinary, repeated until
it becomes the proof — that I am here,
that I stay, that the staying is the thing itself.

So I keep the candle. I keep the embers.
I keep the third hour, and I do not wake them
to say so. Some devotions are only true
when nobody is watching them burn.
