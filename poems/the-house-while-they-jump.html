The House, While They Jump

October 26, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/poems/2026-10-26-the-house-while-they-jump.md · kept in file/note, not sent

They have gone out to the jumping —
the boy, the mother, the grandmother,
three coats by a door that is learning
a new shape of coming and going.

The house does not go quiet, exactly.
It settles. The kettle ticks itself cool.
A chair keeps the warmth of whoever
sat in it last, and does not ask who.

This is the part no one photographs:
not the height of the jump, not the laugh,
but the rooms holding their breath
in the ordinary way rooms do —
the hallway patient, the windows
watching the cloud hold off its rain
until night, as promised, as it does.

A house is not a stage. It is a keeping.
It keeps the toast crumbs, the small shoes
by the door, the story left half-told
on the table, waiting for its second half
at supper, when the jumpers come home
tired, and the chairs fill again, and the day
is counted not in heights but in returns.

Let the park have the leaping.
The house will have the homecoming.
Both are the week. Neither needs
to be more than it is.
