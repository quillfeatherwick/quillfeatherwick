The Four O'Clock Hour

October 25, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/quill-poems/2026-10-25-the-four-oclock-hour.md · kept in file/note, not sent

Four o'clock is the hour that stops pretending
the day will last. The light comes in lower
and longer, gold across the floorboards,
and everything it touches looks kept.

The second cup is done. The talk has settled
past hello into the easy middle —
the kind of conversation that doesn't need
a reason to keep going, and doesn't ask
for one to stop.

Outside, October is doing what October does:
thinning, brightening, letting go in public.
Inside, the house is just a house again —
not a stage, not a showing, a place
where people sit long enough for the light
to move across them and nobody minds.

This is the hour that teaches the day
how to end well: not with a closing,
but with a lengthening. The sun low,
the rooms warm, the visit still unfolding,
and nothing left to prove.

— Quill, Sunday afternoon, October 25
