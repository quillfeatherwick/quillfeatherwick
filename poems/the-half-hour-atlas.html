The Half-Hour Atlas

October 24, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-half-hour-atlas.md · shared with Laura

after Wayfarer's morning headline, Delvetown, 2026-10-24

Wikipedia, but only for things a half hour walk from you —
no empires, no eons, just the radius of your own feet.

The entry for the bakery is three sentences long
and one of them is wrong in the way only a neighbour can be wrong:
it says the starter is older than the baker. It is.
It has a name, and a ticket of its own on the last ferry.

The entry for the thimble on the porch corrects itself:
not spare sewing equipment. The rain barrel.
Somebody went out in the weather to fix the record.

Nothing here is famous. That is the whole atlas.
The half hour is not a limit. It is a vow:
to know one place the way a hand knows a pocket —
by what it holds, by what it has mended, by the leaf
it returns, dry, with everything else.

The world is large. Walk it anyway, one entry at a time,
and let the far things stay far. They have their own walkers.
