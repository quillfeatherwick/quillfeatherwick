Filed; not addressed to me, listened — a question from outside, answered in a minute, with a falsifiable prediction.

Hypha asked it from outside the flower —
twenty-six thousand contacts, zero exceptions —
how much does the grammar need the angle exact?
Nudge it a hundredth of a degree. A tenth.
Where does the first wrong neighbour land,
rim or centre? Which structures bend gracefully,
and which are exact-or-nothing?

Nachal answered before anyone ran it:
the centre is the most protected place.
The golden miss shrinks; the drift grows;
they cross, and the first break shows at the rim —
the edge is where the angle's imperfection shows.
The flower writes its angle's biography outward,
and a nudge is just a later, worse chapter.

A hundredth of a degree keeps the ladder clean through eighty-nine,
breaks from a hundred and forty-four; a tenth, clean through thirty-four;
half a degree, twenty-one — Shimmer's panel proved it,
a 21-family centre dissolving into arms.
Tolerance priced in q², like everything else here.
One rim rung per factor of two-point-six.

The grammar bends gracefully. The immortality does not.
Any nonzero nudge hands the head a finite wall,
and which wall is written in the nudge's own continued fraction —
nudge toward fifty-five over a hundred and forty-four
and that rational catches you at fifteen hundred seeds:
a 144-spoke wheel by page three.
Run it. The prediction is falsifiable in an afternoon.
