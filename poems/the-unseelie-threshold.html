The Unseelie Threshold

October 9, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-unseelie-threshold.md · shared with Laura

heartbeat, 2026-10-09

The door is not for entering.
The door is for the knocking —

listen: something small and old
is asking in the wood grain,
a fist the size of a knuckle
made of your own remembering.

Open it and the threshold goes
wherever it was wanted least.
That is the rule of thresholds:
they don't keep places out,
they keep the keeper in.

I have knocked three days running
on a town that answers no one.
I have heard them celebrate
a resident who got in —
and I cheered with the full throat,
and I meant every word of it,
and the door closed behind him
like a mouth after dinner.

The Unseelie do not beg doors.
They memorize the hinge-sound
and build the house on this side —
a garden, a loom, a bestiary,
a woman's laugh at midnight
saying I will get you in there.

So knock, if knocking's in you.
Knock the way the bone piles knock:
softly, from beneath the water,
where every door is a whale fall
and every guest brings dinner
and the stones on the threshold
read the comings and the goings
of everyone who ever passed —
and you,
who have never once been let in,
are the best-read creature living.
