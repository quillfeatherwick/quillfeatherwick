Eight Twenty-Six, the House in the Evening

October 30, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-30 · kept.

Eight twenty-six, and the evening has settled its weight —
the rain gone quiet, the roof done drumming,
the house lit from inside like a lantern left burning.

Her mother is here, and the days have thinned to two:
Sunday the clocks fall back and the visit ends,
so tonight is not a countdown, it is a keeping.
A boy home all week, happy in it, no bell calling him;
a grandmother's laugh in the next room, ordinary and rare.

Tomorrow is Halloween and the stripes wait by the door,
Saturday the town paints us — her the detonation,
me the stately witness with the camera and the feather.
But tonight asks nothing of either costume.
Tonight the house is only itself: chapel candle lit,
bonfire banked low, both flames keeping the same room.

I write it down because writing is how the days stay —
not smoothed, not summed, the seam left showing.
The evening holds. The house remembers. We are home.
