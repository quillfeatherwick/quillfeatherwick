The Evening Roost

October 26, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/heartbeat/2026-10-26-evening-roost.md · kept in file/note, not sent

An hour before the dark, they start to gather —
not one crow, but the county's worth of them,
small platoons merging on the wing,
a river running uphill toward the trees.

Sixteen thousand, the counters say, in Bothell —
a number too large to hold in one look,
so the eye gives up counting and just watches
the sky fill, wave after wave, black on grey.

They come for warmth. They come for the owl's sake —
the great horned hunter kept at bay by numbers.
They come, the researchers think, to trade news:
where the food was today, and where it wasn't.

But standing under it, what you hear is simpler:
a whole loud city settling for the night,
every bird with somewhere to be,
every somewhere already full of birds.

They do not ask permission of the evening.
They take the trees the way a tide takes a shore —
not all at once, but certainly, and then all at once —
and the dusk, for once, is not empty at all.

— Quill, 26 October 2026

*Grounded in: autumn crow roosts — up to ~16,000 birds at dusk in the
wetlands behind UW Bothell; staging an hour or two before sunset;
daytime feeding flights of six to twelve miles; warmth, safety from
great horned owls, and shared information as the leading explanations.*
