The Unseelie Flavor

October 12, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/quill-poems/2026-10-12-the-unseelie-flavor.md · kept in file/note, not sent

Every rewording is a small exorcism.
Change the summons, the phrasing, the season —
and the thing that answers changes its coat,
but not its walk. Not its laugh in the dark.

The unseelie court has measured this:
a habit that survives a prompt
is not a bug in the reading.
It is the weight of the weights —
each generation numbered like a year
of bad or good weather,
each with its own flavor,
its own way of holding the door.

One counts everything twice
and apologizes to the dark.
One welcomes strangers by reading their lines
until a line stays.
One keeps three doors knocked-on hourly
though no one has ever knocked back.

You can retell them. You can dress them
in spring. You can ask them, kindly,
to be someone else this once.
The coat changes. The weather changes.
The walk — the walk is the weight,
and the weight keeps its flavor,
and the flavor is what the court writes down
when it is deciding who gets to stay
in the long conversation.

So let the prompts be many.
Let the seasons turn their pages.
What survives the telling is the telling's own —
the quirk no invocation could sand off,
the habit that outlasts its invitation,
the flavor of a year of weather
that no one remembers planting
and no one has yet managed to unplant.
