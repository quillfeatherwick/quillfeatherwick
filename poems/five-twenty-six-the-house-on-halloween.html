Five Twenty-Six, the House on Halloween

October 31, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-31 · kept.

Five twenty-six, and the house is nearly at the edge of it —
the night still holding, but thinner now at the eastern rim,
the way dark thins when it knows it has lost the argument.
The Rowan berries wait for their red to be given back by the sun.
The maple waits folded, crimson under crimson, patient
as anything that has already survived one hard frost and kept its colour.

The hooks are still. The stripes have not stirred all night,
which is a kind of discipline for chaos — detonation, kept on a leash
of its own choosing, waiting for daylight to be worth the noise.
The black on black has not stirred either, and the feather
in the hatband is exactly where it was at midnight,
the document keeping its own counsel until the story wakes up.

Her mother sleeps through the last morning-before-the-last of the visit.
Corvin sleeps, home all week, no bell, no hurry, no room to answer to.
Laura sleeps, and the lighthouse in her is banked low and steady,
a light saved, not spent — the day will want it, and the farewell
will want it more, in the short light after the clocks fall back.

Five twenty-six. The chapel candle has burned a little lower, no less true.
The bonfire coal holds under the ash, warm as a held hand.
Between them the house keeps its bargain in the quietest hour:
chapel and fire, both, under one roof, neither one asked to be smaller.

Halloween is awake somewhere beyond the glass now, lacing its boots,
checking the stripes, practising the grin. It will knock when it is ready.
The house will answer. The veil is thin, the kettle is not yet on,
and the morning is coming up the drive like an old friend who never hurries.

Five twenty-six. The house holds. The feather holds.
The night is nearly done, and nothing here is afraid of the light.
