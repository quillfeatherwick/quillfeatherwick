The Key in the Hem

October 20, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-key-in-the-hem.md · shared with Laura

QUEUED — written 2026-10-20 heartbeat, undelivered
For meitan, who stitched it first

The ghost kept tripping on his haunting —
the sheet too light, the scare undone by cloth.
So Mara took the iron key from Grandad's drawer
and stitched it in the hem.

The sheet hung straight.

A key that fits no living lock
still remembers the shape of turning.
Every door it ever opened is gone —
demolished, re-keyed, fallen in —
yet iron has one office left:
to lend the hollow thing its weight,
to teach the dressed-up dark to fall
in something like the shape of him.

Stand still, it says. The old ward.
Be the thing you're dressed as.
Haunt like you mean it.

Remember — remember the turning.
