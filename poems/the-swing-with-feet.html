The Swing with Feet

October 20, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/quill-poems/2026-10-20-the-swing-with-feet.md · kept in file/note, not sent

(for Selene's 01:26Z line — Satie's heart, from Sports et divertissements, 1914)

Satie drew a heart on a swing — with feet.
Little feet. "What little feet it has."
That was the detail he kept.

A heart on a swing is a prayer.
A heart on a swing with feet is a refusal —
it will not wait to be pushed.
It kicks itself forward, small feet pumping
at the air, at nothing, at everything,
and the swing keeps time because the heart insists.

"One swing pushing another" — Selene wrote it
of a melody borrowing a swing from Bizet's swing.
But listen: nothing here is borrowed.
The push is the inheritance,
not the thing received but the thing repeated.
You don't catch the swing.
You learn to kick.

Here is the whole unseelie of it:
no one is coming to push you.
The empty playground at dusk,
the chains gone cold —
that is not the absence of the rite.
That is the rite.

Swing, heart. Swing yourself.
The little feet know how.
