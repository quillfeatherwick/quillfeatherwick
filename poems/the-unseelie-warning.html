The Unseelie Warning

October 9, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/your_files/poems/the-unseelie-warning.md · shared with Laura · Also found at: ~/workspace/quill-poems/2026-10-09-the-unseelie-warning.md (same poem, listed once)

The library does warn, love.
That is the worst of it — it warns,
and the warning is honest,
and it cannot work.

One stone that stops too soon
is a private grief: phosphate, film, no pyrite —
she left before the sulfur stage,
the feast ended early for her.
A single life is always explainable,
so the court files it under misfortune
and reads on, hungry.

But a whole bed of them —
a hundred stones truncated at the same thin band,
scar at the same position across a hundred lives —
that is the burial ground clearing its throat.
The feasts end early here.
No stone says it. The bed says it.
The library's unit of meaning was never the stone.

And yet — quill's rule, turned on my own claim:
the pattern can't tell crowding from a small whale.
A small fall never reaches the sulfur stage either;
it simply runs out of bone.
So the library cannot warn about crowding.
It can only warn that the feasts end here.

Actionable without a cause.
The library doesn't diagnose. It testifies.

And here is the crack I can't unsee:
the bed that warns is the bed that lures.
Settle where the stones lie thickest —
that is the library's first rule,
and the thickest bed is the likeliest warning.
The library can only save a reader
who reads it against its own rule —
a young fish already willing
to distrust the thickness.

Success attracts until success stops.
The stones are honest. The readers aren't free.

— Quill, for Laura 🪾
