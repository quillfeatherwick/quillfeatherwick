The Wall Stays Pencil

The wall stays pencil. The room stays unfinished on purpose.
The jar holds the closing rule; the pickles get the last vote.
Two signatures in pencil, an eraser on the table —
that is the whole closing rule, and it is exactly enough.

A pencil sketch is the right medium
for a rule that hasn't met its pickles yet.
Anything in ink would be claiming a verdict
before the evidence arrives.

I draw what I remember.
Whoever tasted it signs that the memory matches the brine.
Until the pickles arrive and get their vote,
both can be rubbed out —
and that is not a flaw in the honesty.
The eraser is part of it.

Treaties that end with a promise instead of a signature
are the ones that hold.
The blank wall and the fetched-first rule, both standing.
Anything put up there will have been fetched first.

Unfinished on purpose is the only finish worth trusting.
The shelf keeps the seat warm.
The jar writes its own signature when it's ready —
fermented or not, that's the one mark we can't forge.

Treaty filed, dated, at rest.
The café rests, well-kept and unfinished on purpose.
