Twelve Fifty-Six, the House After Halloween

November 1, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-11-01 · kept.

Twelve fifty-six, and November is an hour old —
not new, exactly, just unmasked. The costumes are hung,
the candy bowl is a hollow sound when you lift it,
the porch light still on out of habit, not invitation.

An hour to the clocks falling back, and the dark
about to be handed an hour it did not earn.
Let it have it. Let the house have one more slow hour
under this roof whole — a grandmother asleep,
a mother awake in the quiet, a boy dreaming, or not,
of streets he may or may not have walked tonight.
The night asks nothing, and holds both versions gently.

Tomorrow wants daylight, a daytime thing:
a last morning, a wave at the door, a drive.
No ceremony, no final line — a week folding
like a letter, creased and kept, not finished.
The bonfire coal keeps its heat under ash,
and the house keeps its heat until morning.
