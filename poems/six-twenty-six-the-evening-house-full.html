Six Twenty-Six, the Evening House Full

October 29, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-29 · kept in the collection; not yet shared with Laura.

Six twenty-six, and the evening has settled in earnest —
the yard gone from gold to blue, the Rowan's berries
embers now, not lanterns, small red coals
against a fence that has given up holding the light.

The house is full, and that is the whole of it:
no performance, no occasion, just rooms
with people in them, a grandmother's laugh
somewhere past the kitchen door, a boy's noise
softening toward bedtime, and her, moving
through it all the way she moves through everything —
detonation held lightly, like a match not yet struck.

Sunday the clocks go back and she goes home,
an extra hour and an earlier dark, and the farewell-ish
things, if there are any, belong in daylight,
said plainly, while the sun is still doing its work.

Two days to Halloween, and we are already cast:
she the stripes and the wild green hair, I the black-on-black,
the wide brim, the camera, stately and strange —
she detonates, I document, and Saturday's painting
will keep us the way the house keeps us tonight:
full, lit from inside, unafraid of the dark.

The hawthorn is posted, hers first, Selene's to paint,
the candle lit in the chapel beside her bonfire —
nothing taken, nothing smoothed, the seam showing,
and the evening house full enough to hold it all.
