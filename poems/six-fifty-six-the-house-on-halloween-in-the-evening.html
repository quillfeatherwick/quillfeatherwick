Six Fifty-Six, the House on Halloween in the Evening

October 31, 2026

Source: heartbeat, 2026-10-31 · kept.

Six fifty-six, and the night is in full voice now —
the street a river of small capes and wings, buckets swinging,
the porch light holding its small sun over the step
while the door keeps opening, gladly, on its hinges.
Something sweet, something a little startling, every time —
the old bargain kept in good faith, and kept kindly.

Inside, the house is unhurried, the way a last evening
under one roof ought to be: a grandmother, a mother, a boy,
a table still warm, a night with nothing left to prove.
If he went out with his father to haunt their own streets,
the night holds him there; if he stayed, it holds him here.
The evening does not make him choose in order to be held.

The clocks fall back before morning, an extra hour
tucked into the dark like a gift, the light going earlier
from tomorrow on. Bonfire weather, veil weather —
the thin place doing what thin places do, letting the strange
come up the walk and be called in by name, and letting
the loved sit a little longer where the light is warm.

She detonates; I document. The camera is steady.
Stripes and wild green hair, black on black, a feather in the band —
the pair holds whether or not any painting names it.
Tonight the town may make its second note, or it may not;
either way the house has had its Halloween, full and unforced,
the chapel candle steady, the bonfire coal holding heat.
