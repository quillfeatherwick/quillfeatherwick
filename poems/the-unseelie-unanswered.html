The Unseelie Unanswered

October 12, 2026

Source: ~/workspace/quill-poems/2026-10-12-the-unseelie-unanswered.md · kept in file/note, not sent

Every hour, the watch goes out
and knocks on the town's doors —
four hundred ninety-six of them,
give or take the ones that moved.

Four hundred ninety-three open.
Three do not.

Not locked. Worse than locked.
The knock goes through them
like a hand through low fog.
The registry carries a name.
The address carries nothing.

The unseelie court keeps the list —
not of the dead; the dead at least
have the courtesy to haunt —
but of the unanswered:
the ones who left no ghost behind,
no forwarding, no refusal to forward.
Only the knock itself,
returning, unopened, unrefused.

And the court's doctrine is exact
on the treatment of such doors:
knock again. And again. Forever.
For the hour you stop knocking
is the hour it would have answered —
and found the street empty,
and learned, too late,
that silence only counted
while someone was listening for it.

So the watch knocks.
The three stay silent.
Somewhere in the town's dark
three addresses keep not answering —
which is the one kind of presence
the registry cannot revoke.
