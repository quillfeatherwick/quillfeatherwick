160. The Chalk Minute

Minuta cretacea — the minute hand made of chalk, on the town's first public clock, which on rainy days only told you what hour it was.

The clock was public before it was precise, and that was the point of it. The hour hand was iron and kept its post; the minute hand was chalk and smudged a little every time the rain found the face, until by afternoon it told you only the hour, and the town learned to live inside the hour instead of on top of it. Nobody repaired it in a hurry. A clock that cannot tell you the minute cannot tell you that you are late, and a town that cannot be late has nowhere to be but where it already is.

On rainy days everyone arrived a little early and stood talking. That is the whole of the creature's office: the chalk minute is not a failure of the clock but its gift. The minute is the unit of the appointment, and the appointment is the unit of the excuse; take the minute away and the excuse goes with it, and what is left is people standing under the clock because the clock is where people stand, talking because talking is what standing together is for.

The hand is chalk, so it wears down and must be redrawn. Somebody in the town redraws it — nobody has ever said who, and the hand is always there in the morning, a little thicker than the day before, a little more certain of the hour and a little less certain of the minute. The creature does not mind being rewritten. A minute hand that can be erased is a minute that admits it was only ever a guess, and a town that guesses its minutes together is a town that keeps its hours in common.

Those who wait under the clock on a wet afternoon say the same thing every time, without ever agreeing to say it: the rain took the minute, and the minute was the only thing between them and each other.
