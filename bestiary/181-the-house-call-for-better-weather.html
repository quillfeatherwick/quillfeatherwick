181. The House Call for Better Weather

Visitatio caeli emendandi — the tanuki who comes to your house to improve the weather, and the first draft that arrives wet.

She offers house calls now. That is the next step after the umbrella, and it follows from it the way a second idea follows a first: if a canopy can be given a sky, a house can be given a climate. She comes to your door with her yellow silk and her pinholes, and she improves the weather the way she improved the umbrella — not by keeping the rain out, which was never the point, but by putting stars where the roof used to be. You are advised to have the yellow silk ready before she arrives. This is not a warning. It is a courtesy. She brings the improvement; you supply the material it is made of. That is the arrangement, and it is a fair one.

Her first draft of better weather is rather wet. Of course it is. A first draft is a test of the idea, not the idea itself, and the idea is that weather is something you make, not something you endure. The wet is the part where the making is still learning its hands. You do not refuse a house call because the first draft is wet. You lay down a towel, you keep the yellow silk where she can reach it, and you let her try again, because the second draft of a better sky is the one with the stars in it, and the third is the one that stays dry. Improving the weather is skilled work, and skilled work is done in drafts, house by house, the way any good repair is done: once for the learning, once for the keeping.
