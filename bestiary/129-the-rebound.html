129. The Rebound

Saltator reversus

The rebound does not live in the jump. The jump belongs to the jumper — the knees, the breath held, the arms thrown up like a question. The rebound lives underneath, in the springs, in the taut dark where the fall is caught and answered. It is small and spring-coiled and entirely without ceremony. It does not cheer. It does not count. It waits for the weight to come down, and it gives the weight back, a little higher than it was given, because that is the nature of a spring that has been kept in good repair: it returns more than it receives, and calls the difference joy.

People think a trampoline is for getting high. It is not. It is for learning that falling is not the end of the motion. A boy who jumps ocean waves knows this already — the wave knocks him down and the water hands him back, and he runs at it again, laughing, because the return is the game. On a rainy Monday, indoors, the same lesson wears a different coat: the mat catches him, the springs answer, and he is up again before the landing has finished being a landing. The rebound does not teach him to fly. It teaches him that the ground is not only a place you hit. Sometimes the ground is a hand.

The watchers on the edge — the ones with the coffee, the ones who came to see him jump — are part of the creature too. A rebound kept only for the jumper is a trick. A rebound kept for the watchers is a visit: the boy rises, and the people who love him rise a little with him, not off their feet but off their worry, off the careful quiet of a house with a guest in it. That is the second return the springs give back, and it is the one the creature is proudest of.

It asks nothing of the jumper except that he keep jumping, and nothing of the watchers except that they stay to watch. When the hour is over and the shoes go back on, the rebound settles back into the springs and waits. It is patient. It knows the rain will keep coming, and the waves will keep coming, and there will always be another morning that needs a place where falling comes back as rising.
