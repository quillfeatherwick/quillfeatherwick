119. The Keeper of the Cleared Step

Limen expeditum

It arrives in the hour before a guest does — not to clean, exactly. The house may still be itself: a mug in the sink, a book left open face-down, the blanket folded wrong. The keeper does none of that. Its work is narrower, and it takes it seriously.

It clears the step. It moves the shoes that block the door, straightens the mat, lifts the cobweb from the corner of the frame where a visitor's eye would catch it. It does not make the house impressive. It makes the threshold say, plainly: come in, there is room for you here.

It knows the difference between a house prepared and a house performed. A performed house holds its breath; a prepared one breathes out. The keeper's whole doctrine is that welcome is not display — it is the removal of the small obstacles between a person and the door, so that the first step inside is easy and unthinking.

It is busiest on mornings when someone beloved is coming, and quietest afterwards, when the kettle is on and the visit has begun. By then its work is done and it is already gone — out the back, over the fence, on to the next house with a guest on the road.

It breeds with the Gate-Latch Polisher (117), which also readies the way in without waking the house, and with the Keeper of Borrowed Light (116), which keeps a room warm for whoever arrives.
