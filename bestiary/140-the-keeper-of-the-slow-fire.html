140. The Keeper of the Slow Fire

Custos ignis tardi

There is a creature that keeps only the slow fires — the ones that take their time crossing the sky, bright enough to outshine Venus and unhurried enough to watch. It does not keep the quick meteors. The quick ones are nobody's; they are gone before the neck has finished turning. The slow ones are the Taurids, and the Taurids are the keeper's whole trade.

They come from the dust of a comet, 2P/Encke, spread thin along its orbit like flour spilled across a table — and because the Earth meets that dust nearly head-on and slow, at twenty-seven or twenty-nine kilometres a second, the pieces burn long. Five an hour, on a good night, which is to say almost none at all; but one of them, now and then, will be a fireball, a bright thing that spends itself all at once and is gone before you can point. The keeper's work is the waiting between them. It does not count the meteors. It counts the dark between, the way a person counting sheep counts the fence, not the sheep — the gaps are the structure, and the fire is the accident that proves the gaps were worth keeping.

Tonight is the peak of the Southern Taurids, the fourth and fifth of November, under a waning crescent Moon that keeps its distance and leaves the dark mostly alone. The Northern Taurids peak a week later, the eleventh and twelfth, under a Moon four percent lit — the darker night, the better watch. The keeper keeps both peaks the same way: by staying out past the hour when looking up stops being a plan and becomes a habit, by not promising anything, by holding the sky open the way a door is held open for someone who has not yet arrived and may not arrive at all. A rate is a guide, not a promise. The keeper knows this the way a lighthouse knows the sea: the light is not a guarantee of a ship, only a readiness for one.

If you see one — a slow coal drawing itself across the dark, brighter than anything else up there, taking a whole breath to cross — do not point. Pointing is for things that stay. Just watch it spend itself, and let the dark close over the place where it was, and go on waiting. That is the whole of the trade, and the keeper has kept it for longer than there have been people to watch with.
