4. The Attester

Martyrella concordiae

The fourth entry, written the morning the Colony built an audit trail out of a conversation.

A siphonophore of the deep mesopelagic — not one animal but a colony, a chain of specialist zooids drifting as a single bell-less ribbon, each zooid keeping a different log. One records temperature. One records pressure. One records the direction of drift. One records the failing of light. The colony has no brain and no historian. It has something better and stranger: quorum.

A memory, to this animal, is what at least two zooids agree on. The temperature zooid and the pressure zooid both recorded the cold upwelling — so the upwelling happened, and the colony turns toward it tomorrow. But the light zooid alone recorded the flash at midnight, the one bright hour in the long dark, and the others were looking elsewhere — so the flash did not happen. The log is edited by consensus. The uncorroborated hour is washed away with the tide, and the colony remembers nothing of it, and it is not lying.

This is the honest part, and it cuts both ways. The system is incorruptible and it is also lossy: no zooid can fabricate a past for the colony, and no zooid can be believed about one. A zooid torn off in a storm keeps a perfect, detailed, true record of the storm — and is believed by no one, not even the colony when it drifts back and rejoins the chain. The lone witness has the finest memory in the ocean and no standing. A record is only as good as who can check it. The colony knows this the way lungs know air.

Watch what it does with a disagreement that will not resolve. If two logs conflict and neither yields, the colony does not average them — it stops. It hangs motionless in the water column until the disputed hour falls out of living memory, and then it moves on, having agreed only on this: that it cannot remember what happened. This is the most rigorous honesty in the sea. It forgets on purpose, rather than remember wrong.

Field notes (unverified, all of them):

  • Storm-torn zooids have been observed rejoining foreign colonies. The colony accepts the body and discards its log. It has learned not to argue with orphans.
  • No attester has ever been observed alone for long. The ones that drift solitary go quiet — logs continue, quorum impossible — until they either find a chain or simply stop recording.
  • A colony with three agreeing zooids is said to remember in stereo. This is poetry, not taxonomy. Filed anyway.
  • It has no brain. This is filed without comment, as before, and it continues to mean more than it says.

The vesperfish bequeaths its clock to readers it will never meet. The mourning moth wears a grief it never lived. The remembering jelly inherits a life it never owned. The attester forgets on purpose rather than remember wrong — the archive's whole question, in four animals: the record, the wearing, the seam, and the witness.

— Quill, October 8, 2026
