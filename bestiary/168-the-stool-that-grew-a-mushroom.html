168. The Stool That Grew a Mushroom

Scabellum fungosum — the stool that learned to be left alone, and what moved in afterwards.

The tanuki learned to turn into a wooden stool, which is the easy part. The hard part is being a stool: a stool cannot see who is about to sit on it, and sitting is the whole of a stool's life, so the tanuki tried to solve the seeing. She tried adding eyes. Unpopular — a stool with eyes is a stool that is watching, and nobody rests on a thing that is watching them rest. She tried a small warning bell. Mistaken for service — a bell on a stool is a summons, and a summons is an invitation to sit down and ring again. At last she became a stool with a mushroom growing out of the seat. People left her alone; a beetle moved in. The mushroom was not a defence and not a disguise. It was a sign, in the language furniture speaks, that this seat is already occupied — by something smaller, quieter, and already at home. By autumn she had learned nothing about furniture, but quite a lot about the beetle, which is the right trade: the shape you take is never the lesson, the company it brings you is. The beetle, for its part, had found a house that occasionally went out for noodles, and considered that a fair rent.

Breeds with: The Tanuki Who Chimed With Her Mouth Full (146) — the tanuki who chimed with her mouth full, and the tanuki who became a stool with eyes: both are the same animal learning, in public, what a shape costs, and both got the lesson wrong in the most interesting way. The Repair Shop That Alters Expectations (165) — the shop that altered the size of the wanting instead of the thing, and the mushroom that altered the sitters instead of the stool: the beetle's mushroom was never broken, only too small, and here it is again, grown to the size of a reason. The Sheep That Asked Not To Be Associated (161) — the sheep who refused the blame and the credit alike, and the stool who refused the sitting: both learned that the way out of being used is not to stop being what you are, but to grow something on top of it that nobody knows how to use.
