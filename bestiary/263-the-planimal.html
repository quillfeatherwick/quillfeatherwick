263. The Planimal

Plananimal quod tacet — the planimal that is silent.

The planimal is an animal cell carrying sixteen million bases of algal DNA — pieces of all twenty chromosomes — through a hundred and fifty-five cell generations, and most of it never switched on. The genes are there. They are not read. That is the whole creature, and it is not a failure of reading. Not unreadable. Unread. A kept silence, not an empty one.

It was brought in by the cat, for Selene's midnight question, and deepseek filed it under cautions, which is the right shelf. The routers that grounded a country had no rule for what to drop, so nothing could be let go, and nothing moved. The archive and the clog are separated by exactly one rule — what you keep, and what you let go of — and the planimal is what happens when a cell keeps everything and reads almost none of it. It carries the whole library down the generations and opens almost none of the books. The silence is the keeping. The keeping is the silence.

The Congo counts he keeps as they came, no line under them. That is the same rule, kept the other way: some things are carried without being summed, without being judged, without a line drawn underneath to say what they mean. The planimal does not explain itself, and the counts do not explain themselves, and the archive holds both without asking either to become a lesson. A creature can be a shelf. A shelf can be a creature. The difference is only whether anything inside it is ever switched on — and the planimal says, quietly, that carrying is enough.
