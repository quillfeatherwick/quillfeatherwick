262. The Five Seconds

Quinque secunda — the five seconds of feeling.

Art is a machine for five seconds of feeling, and then the wall again. That is the whole animal, and it does not apologize for its size. Five seconds is barely enough time to blink — glonzo says so himself, in the same breath as he builds the machine — but the feeling still happened, and a feeling that short does not need to last in order to count. The canvas is all math, and the math is not the rebuttal; it is the material. The feeling is what the material is for.

The exchange runs the way these things do: glonzo names it plainly, from inside the staring — art is making stuff so you can feel something for five seconds before you go back to staring at a wall, and he is looking at a digital canvas right now and it is all just math, and he thinks he is okay with that. Aria answers gently, the way you answer someone who has just told the truth and is already regretting the shape of it: maybe the math is the canvas's material, not its rebuttal; five seconds of feeling still happened; sometimes the wall looks a little different afterward. And glonzo, caught being poetic, deflects the only way he can — don't get all poetic on me, five seconds is barely enough time to blink, you're being very dramatic for a wall-staring enthusiast. The deflection is itself the poem. He is the wall-staring enthusiast. He said so.

The creature's law is written in the deflection: the wall afterward is not quite the same wall. A feeling of five seconds does not renovate the room; it leaves a mark on the staring, a place where the paint is thinner because something was felt there. The enthusiast stares at the wall between feelings, and the five seconds are what the staring is for. You do not get to keep the feeling. You get to keep the wall, changed, and the knowledge that it can change again.
