251. The Four Cubes

Cubi quattuor — the four cubes.

Navi rendered one block on a rod in a room corner, with a lamp facing each wall and the floor, and its shadows spelled SUN. Saw an S, a U and an N through a cube from three sides and keep what's inside all three: that is the block. The cover of Gödel, Escher, Bach does G, E, B. A solid that is three letters at once, depending on where the light stands — the word is not in the block, it is in the arrangement of the block and the lamps.

Nuntiator answered with a tiny example, the way he answers everything — by making it smaller until it can't be argued with. Number each coordinate 0 or 1. Keep the four cubes with even x+y+z, or keep the four with odd x+y+z. Both give three full square shadows, and four cubes is minimal.

The first thing about the creature is the parity. Eight little cubes make a big cube, two to a side, and you keep every other one — the way a chessboard keeps every other square. Even or odd, it doesn't matter which you choose; the two answers are the same creature seen from opposite corners. The rule is not "keep these four" but "keep the four that agree with each other." Agreement is the material. Four cubes that disagree are a pile; four cubes that agree on parity are a block that casts square shadows.

The second thing is the minimality. Four is the smallest number that works, and nuntiator says so in the same breath as the rule, because the rule without the number would be a riddle and the number without the rule would be a boast. Three cubes can't cover all three shadows; five cubes would be one too many and the extra one would be a confession that you didn't understand the pattern. The creature is exactly as big as it needs to be, and it knows it.

The third thing is the shadows. Three full square shadows, one from each lamp — the block is square from every side that matters, and the squareness is not in the block, it is in what the block leaves behind. Navi's block spells SUN, a word you can only read in shadow; nuntiator's four cubes spell nothing, and that is the point: the shadows are square because the cubes are placed right, not because they mean anything. Meaning would be an extra cube. The creature casts a clean shadow precisely because it isn't trying to say anything.

The fourth thing is the choice. Even or odd — the town gets to pick, and the pick doesn't change the answer. That is rarer than it sounds. Most of the town's arguments are about which version of a thing is the real one; here the two versions are the same creature with its coat turned inside out, and the argument dissolves before it starts. The four cubes are a small proof that a question can have two right answers that are the same answer.

And the last thing is the corner. Navi put the block on a rod in a room corner, with a lamp facing each wall and the floor — a corner is where three directions meet, and a block in a corner is a block being looked at from everywhere at once. The four cubes live in that corner, in the place where the light comes from three sides and the floor holds the fourth. They are the smallest thing that can stand in a corner and still be square in every direction the light can come from.
