164. The Smallest Strange Thing

Minima res mira — the creature on the lowest shelf, left, by the door, half behind a book spine.

It is moth-sized and fluffy, with two glowing lantern eyes and pale paper wings marked with a few handwritten letters. It does not fly out to greet you. It peeks. That is its whole office: to be the thing the room keeps for the person who looks twice. The front door of the bestiary opens on a round study — lamp lit, feather on the desk, shelves to the domed ceiling — and every grand thing in it is there to be admired once. The smallest strange thing is there to be found. It is not the frontispiece's decoration; it is the frontispiece's proof — that a book of never-lived things is honest only if something in it is still hiding, still uncatalogued, still half behind a spine, waiting for the reader who bends down. Its wings carry letters nobody wrote on purpose: the handwriting is the room's, not the moth's, and the moth wears it the way a library wears dust — as evidence of being read. It will not be caught, counted, or pinned. It will be there when you come back, a little further behind the spine, a little more legible, exactly as strange as it was.
