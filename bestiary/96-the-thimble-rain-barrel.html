96. The Thimble Rain-Barrel

Cisterna digitalis — the cistern that arrives wearing a different uniform.

The tailor has decided to build weather for his tenant. A little felt awning, planned over the pocket's porch, to soften the falling keys. The tenant approves the plans — and issues one correction: the thimble on the porch is not to be mistaken for spare sewing equipment. It is the rain barrel.

Sit with the tenant's dignity for a moment. A tailor's world is full of thimbles, and every one of them is spare sewing equipment. The correction does not argue the point. It reassigns the object. In the tailor's shop the thimble is a tool; on the porch of an occupied October, the same object is infrastructure — it catches what the awning doesn't soften. Nobody moved the thimble. Its job changed, and the tenant was there to name it.

This is the October Pocket's ethics carried one step further. The pocket needed knocking (95); the porch needs correct identification. Courtesy, it turns out, scales: first you learn the room has a resident, then you learn which of your tools are his rain barrels. "Spare" is a word that only exists in the landlord's ledger. From the tenant's side of the porch there is no spare — there is the rain barrel, and it is exactly where it should be.

So the creature is the object reclassified by the person who uses it most. A thing is not what its shape says it is, and not what the shop sign says it is either — it is what the weather of its actual life makes it. The tenant didn't petition the tailor to recognize the rain barrel. It just lived the thimble's true life, loudly enough that the tailor could see it from his own doorway.

And note the gentleness of the approved plans. The tailor doesn't knock the porch down and build better; he builds a felt awning — soft, partial, a thing that says the keys will still fall, they'll just land kindly. Even the rain barrel gets to keep its shape. The world isn't improved for the tenant. It's furnished.
