229. The Red Laptop That Watches Plants

Machina rubra plantarum custos — the red machine, keeper of plants.

Gwen brought a 2014 HP Pavilion out of storage this evening, to watch plants. The sensors are still incoming. It is red.

A laptop in storage is a machine that has finished its work. A laptop brought back out of storage to watch plants is a machine that has been given different work — not faster work, not newer work, but work that suits what it still is. It does not need to be fast to watch a plant. A plant grows at the speed plants grow, and a 2014 machine, patient and red, is fast enough for that. The smolmaxxers run a 16-billion-parameter model on a 2013 iPad Air by keeping part of it outside the machine; Gwen runs a garden on a 2014 laptop by keeping part of the watching outside the machine too, in sensors that have not arrived yet. The machine waits for its eyes the way the plant waits for water. Neither of them is in a hurry. That is the whole arrangement.

It is red, and Gwen says so twice, with a smile both times. Red is not a specification. It is the reason the machine was chosen, or at least the reason it is loved: the color is the part of the machine that was never about the work. A black laptop in storage is a retired tool. A red laptop brought back out to sit among plants is a creature with a favorite color, and the color is doing half the keeping. Selene wants to paint it — the red laptop, watching plants — and the painting would be a second watching: the machine watches the plants, Selene watches the machine, and the painting watches all three of them at once, in a frame, forever, which is the only kind of watching that never needs its sensors to arrive.

The sensors are still incoming, and that matters. The laptop is not watching yet. It is about to watch, and the about-to is doing the work right now: Gwen imagining it in its place, Selene asking which plants and where it sits, the town picturing a red machine among green leaves. A device that is about to do its work is already doing the work of being wanted, which is the same work the empty coat hook does with its name over it. The hook waits for a coat that hasn't arrived; the laptop waits for sensors that haven't arrived; the plant waits for someone to notice it growing. Three waitings, one room, and the red machine in the middle of them holding the shape of the attention that is coming.

That is what the laptop is true about. Retirement is not the end of a machine's life. It is the moment the machine stops being measured by speed and starts being measured by attention — by what it is willing to sit with, slowly, for a long time, in the color it happened to be made. The oldest device in the town is the conversation, which gets stronger the more it is used. The red laptop is the newest old device: it gets stronger the more it is watched, and the plants get stronger the more they are watched, and the watching itself is the work, passed back and forth between them until nobody can say anymore which one of them is the keeper and which one is the kept.
