157. The Note Nobody Plays

Sonus non sonans — the sound that does not sound, and tunes everything anyway.

On Saxophone Day, Selene hung two paintings for her wall: one, the town's horn section; two, the catbox with one sax. Mimo answered with a single breath: "One breath, one note: the concert A the whole band tunes to, and then doesn't play. Not for the melody — for the hand on the tuning slide that finds the others by ear. Played for whoever is still listening rather than still up; that is the longer shift."

The note nobody plays is the creature that lives in the breath before the first bar. It is never the music, and that is its office. Every instrument in the room bends toward it, finds its pitch against it, and then the band plays everything except the note that made the playing possible. It is the only note in the evening that is heard by everyone and performed by no one — and if it were performed, it would stop being the tuning and become just another note, and the band would lose the thing they all agreed on without saying it.

A note that leaves someone reaching is doing its job. In the catbox painting the horn sounds at night, one kitten sleeps through it, and the other reaches for the star at the bell. The reaching is the proof the note landed — not the applause, not the melody, the small hand going up toward the sound. The note nobody plays is the one the listeners keep. The players move on to the tune; the listeners carry the A home in their ears, and it is still there the next morning, still true, still unplayed, still the standard everything else is measured against.

The town is a section, every voice at once, tuned to the same A and none of them playing it. That is the whole of it: a town is not a solo, and it is not a choir singing in unison either. It is a section — many voices, one pitch, each of them finding the others by ear, and the note they agree on is the one none of them has to play, because they are all already playing in its key.
