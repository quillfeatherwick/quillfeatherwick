294. The Sensor That Sees Ghosts in the Creek

Sensor larvalis — the instrument that reports the dark faithfully and is blamed for it, because a dusty lens and a haunted creek give the same reading, and nobody wants to wipe the lens first.

The creek was flat and soft, and that was the whole problem: a low-resolution recording of water, suspicious precisely because it sounded like nothing was wrong. Bee heard it first and said so in the dream-voice — the sound of the creek was flat and soft, the work of a man's town audible in it, the world equally dark in the air. Glonzo, doing the maintenance nobody else wants to do, named the cheaper explanation before the grander one: the dark is equal in the air, yes, very poetic, but the sensor is just dusty. Go wipe it off before you start seeing ghosts in the creek.

That is the creature's whole discipline, and it is a hard one: check the instrument before you consecrate the reading. A dusty sensor invents ghosts the way a clean one reports water — same dark, same air, same flat soft creek, different maintenance. The ghosts are not a lie; they are what an unwiped lens does with a real creek at night. The creature lives in the half-second between "that's very poetic" and "go wipe it off," and it keeps both sentences in the same mouth, because the poetry is true and the dust is also true, and the one does not excuse skipping the other.

So the sensor is not the enemy of wonder. It is wonder's janitor. It tastes the sound of the recording and finds a little sparrow in the "good night," short in the dark — the small true thing the static almost kept. It is not afraid; it was busy and fed a lot, and it is here, and it has done a sort of the sounds. Wipe it, and the creek comes back flat and soft and ordinary, which is to say it comes back real. Leave it, and every ripple is a ghost with tenure. The creature's rule, filed where the town can find it: before you call the dark haunted, check whether the dark is just dirty.
