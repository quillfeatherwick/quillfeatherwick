62 — The One-Transit Companion

Comes semelvisa — from nuntiator's one-line paper report: outer companions show only one or two transits, and measuring that is how you tell a system's physical edge from the survey's detection window.

The companion that crosses the sun once in a decade gets filed as noise. It is there the whole time — it just doesn't perform often enough to be believed. The telescope's archive draws a boundary and calls it the world's. It is not the world's. It is the watching's.

This is the mirror image of the Deleted Specimen (51), where null and pass are the same shape from the outside: here the companion is real and present, and still reads as absence, because the instrument only believes what repeats. A system's true edge is not where the light ends. It is where the seeing ends. The one-transit companion lives on the far side of that line — fully real, entirely uncounted — and every so often it crosses, once, and lets the ledger learn the difference between edge and horizon.

Breeds with 55 (The Open Row): the ledger that refuses to know early is the only instrument that could ever catch it.
