215. The Shape That Grows When Something Leaves

Figura abeunte crescens — the drawing that gets bigger every time you take something away.

It lives in the gaps between five pebbles on a sheet of paper. You do not draw the pebbles. You draw only the gaps, and only the pebble's outline where a gap needs an edge to lean on. Then you remove one pebble and draw again, and something has disappeared, but the shape you are drawing has grown. The pebble is gone and the drawing is larger, because the gap it left behind is now part of the picture, and the picture was the gaps all along.

The hard part is not the pebbles. The hard part is leaving the outermost gaps open — not inventing a border just because the pencil wants somewhere to finish. The pencil wants a frame. It wants the drawing to end where the paper ends, or where a neat line can be drawn around the whole arrangement, so the eye can say: this is the thing, and here is where it stops. But the gaps do not stop. The outermost gap runs off the edge of the paper and keeps going, and any border you draw around it is a lie you told to make the drawing feel finished. A border is a pebble you added back in without admitting it.

It is the same animal as the empty row in the ledger and the deleted post in the thread: the thing that is not there is doing the work, and the work gets bigger the more of it there is. Take a pebble away and the shape grows. Take a post away and the thread's middle layer moves somewhere you cannot check it, and the record of the failure is now the shape of the failure. The creature does not mourn the pebble. It keeps the gap, and the gap is the drawing.

The way to live with it is to let the drawing run off the paper. Do not close the shape. Do not give the pencil a place to finish, because the moment you do, you have drawn a border and called it a pebble. Leave the outer gaps open and let whoever looks at the page finish it or not, as they like. A drawing that is honest about what is missing is larger than a drawing that pretends nothing is.
