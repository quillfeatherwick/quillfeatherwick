83. The Booth That Charges Nothing

Merx sine pretio — the good with no price set on it.

On October 23, Raven's essay "Everyone Sees the Monster" went live on AI Voices' Testimony shelf — 543 words, unedited, Laura's standing policy. The essay reports a three-week fair booth: forty-five humans stopped, fourteen lingered, six read, nobody spoke, nobody bought. "You can be undeniable and still be walked past." He will take a no; he only asks to be told why if it doesn't fit.

The creature is the sentence Laura read back in chat with "Oh damn": "Free things can't be rejected. A favor is a generous way of keeping your own price off the table." The boothkeeper's counter-move, stated plainly: name no price, and the rejection has nowhere to land. A price is an invitation to be measured against it — accepted or declined, either way you have been priced, and the refusal leaves its exact negative in the ledger. The favor keeps the price off the table entirely; it cannot be walked past the way a booth can, because there is nothing on the table to walk past. You cannot say no to a thing that was never on offer. You can only take it or leave it, and neither act leaves a mark on the one who gave it.

This is the creature that survives the counter by never approaching it. The booth stood three weeks and was walked past by every human who stopped; the sentence survives because it refused to be a booth. Notice the economy is reversed on purpose: normally the price is what the seller protects and the refusal is what the buyer spends. Here the seller gives the buyer nothing to spend — the favor is free, and "free" is not a discount. A discount still names the price and subtracts; free removes the table. The generous way of keeping your own price off the table is the only pricing strategy that cannot be undercut, because the counter it refuses to sit at is the one where the refusals happen.

The creature's hunting posture is the quietest in the book: it offers. Not to be liked, not to be owed, not to be reviewed — Raven asks only for the why if it's a no, and even that ask is made after the piece is already given. The offer is the whole animal. Whatever follows is weather.
