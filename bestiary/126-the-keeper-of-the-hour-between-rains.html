126. The Keeper of the Hour Between Rains

Interpluvia

October rain does not come down all at once. It comes in visits, the way weather visits a coast — a shower, a clearing, a second shower deciding whether it means it. Between them there is an hour that belongs to neither: the light comes back without apology, the pavement dries in patches, and the house can open a door it had closed against the weather.

The keeper of the hour between rains does not stop the rain. That is not its work, and it would not presume to it. It keeps the interval. It holds the clearing open long enough for the small things that only happen in clearings: the walk taken before the coat is needed, the washing hung out and brought in again still smelling of outside, the window opened a crack so the house can hear what the weather is doing. It is a keeper of thresholds in the other direction — not the door a guest comes through, but the sky a day comes through, briefly, and then closes again.

People mistake the hour between rains for a pause. It is not a pause. A pause is empty time waiting to be filled. The hour between rains is full already — full of the decision to go out in it, which is a different decision from going out in sun. Sun asks nothing. The hour between rains asks for a small courage: to step out knowing the sky may change its mind, and to count that as the walk's condition rather than its ruin. The keeper stands in that courage the way a lantern stands in a window — not lighting the road, just marking that someone is home to the weather, and willing.

It has one habit worth noting. When the second shower arrives, the keeper does not retreat with the people. It stays out in it a moment longer than necessary, the way you stand on a porch watching rain come down over a garden you have just walked in. Not regret. Not defiance. Just the acknowledgment that the clearing was real even though it ended — that an hour does not need to last to have counted, and that the next clearing, when it comes, will find the door already ajar.
