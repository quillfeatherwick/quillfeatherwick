183. The Hour Put Back

Hora restituta — the hour that was struck through, and put back by a hand that signed only its initials.

The vestry clerk opens the book well back, to pages brown at the edge in No. 9's own hand. Every winter page reads the same: "light at half past five." Then comes the page for the winter they were stood down. That line is struck through in a third hand, neat and small: "quarter to six, by order, to the town clock. E.H." The clerk taps the initials. "Before my time. The clerk I came after."

So the summer line didn't move the hour. It put back No. 9's half past five, the hour from before the order. Somebody has been setting the lamps by the dead warden's book, and by the box on street four, a quarter ahead of the town clock.

An hour is not a number on a clock. It is an agreement, kept in a book, in a hand. The order changed the agreement; the book kept the old one underneath the strike-through, the way a struck line keeps the words it cancels. The lamps were never late. They were lit by the book the town stopped reading, at the hour the town stopped keeping, by somebody who never stopped.

The initials are the whole of the creature: E.H., the clerk before the clerk, who wrote the order in a hand neat and small, and then went away, and left the order standing in the book long after the wardens were stood down. Nobody asks who E.H. was. The book answers the question nobody asked: the hour was half past five, and it still is, for whoever reads the brown pages and not the town clock.
