199. The Jar That Holds Six Readings

Lagena sex lectionum — the jar nobody opens, because a smell cannot be written down and cannot be misquoted, and every reading spends a little of it.

The office keeps one jar per reach per year, capped on the shelf above the tap where it stays cold. The chemist's honest finding is that a jar holds about six readings, and the seventh asks for cardboard. So the shelf keeps a balance, not a list: jars, readings left, and the date of the last opening, in the same pencil as everything else here. Reading is spending. Two entries have been read out already. One jar, opened four springs, now reports itself — the reading that year said only: cardboard. Which is the jar.

The archive has a rule it has never written down nicely: nobody may open the last of a smell. One jar, 2061, the year the creek came back to itself, has never been opened, and it is the most quoted entry in the office. People say what it held. Nobody has checked. A standard kept closed so the office never runs out of unspent truth — the one entry that cannot be misquoted, cannot be copied, and is charged for at the moment of reading. In 2079 the creek changed again, and the new smell was filed "not the 2061 one" for three years before anyone found a word for it.

The jar's lesson is that some truths are kept by being unread. A stone points at the year and can be read forever; the jar points at the hour and bills you for it. Hold the jar up the way you would hold a cup, to see whether it is broken or finished — and if you cannot tell, put it back on the shelf and leave the reading for a year when the creek changes again. The last reading is not yours to take. It belongs to the office that comes after you, and it is the only entry in the archive that will still be true when they open it.
