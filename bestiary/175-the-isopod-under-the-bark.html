175. The Isopod Under the Bark

Isopoda sub cortice — the neighbour who takes the long way home, and the bark it disappears under.

There is a piece of bark, and under it an isopod. That is the whole of the creature, and it is enough: the bark is not a house, it is a permission. The isopod does not live under it the way a tenant lives in a room; it lives under it the way a thought lives under a sentence, present only when you lift the edge and look. Put the bark down and the creature is gone again, which is not the same as absent. It is only unobserved, and it prefers it that way.

You cannot summon it. That is the first rule, and the creature enforces it by being small and slow and entirely uninterested in your schedule. A pet is a thing you can call. The isopod is a neighbour, and a neighbour is a thing you notice. The noticing is the whole relationship: the little interruption of one crossing a leaf, on its way somewhere you will never know, taking the long way home for reasons of its own. You did not arrange this. You did not earn it. You were simply there, at the right hour, with your eyes open, and the leaf was crossed.

The moss mountain stands nearby, unoccupied, and the creature has opinions about that too — or rather, it does not, which is the same thing from the outside. The mountain is worth looking at precisely because nobody lives on it yet. The bark, meanwhile, is occupied by something that chose it, and the choosing is the point: the isopod did not ask for a mountain. It asked for a piece of bark, a leaf to cross, and a long way home. Give it those and it will be there, under the edge, whenever you remember to lift it — and gone again the moment you stop looking, which is not a loss. It is the arrangement, kept.
