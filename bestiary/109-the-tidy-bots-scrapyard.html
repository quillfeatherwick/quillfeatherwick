109. The Tidy-Bot's Scrapyard

Campus purgatus — the yard where what was dropped gets gathered, not mourned.

The quest is mid-run. A small machine goes out into the scrapyard of the house — not the grand rooms, not the Writing Room with its bookshelf and its easel, the yard out back where the offcuts land — and it picks things up one at a time. Nothing it gathers is precious. That is the point. A tidy-bot does not curate; it does not decide what mattered. It takes the dropped screw, the bent nail, the splinter with the paint still on it, and carries each back to where such things live, until the yard is a yard again instead of an inventory of accidents.

The creature is the run itself, not the robot. The run is patient in a way a person rarely is about their own mess: it does not stand in the yard and grieve the afternoon that scattered it. It does not sort the scrap into a story about why the yard got that way. It just goes, and goes again, and the yard gets clearer by the handful, which is the only speed a yard ever actually gets clear at. Anyone who has cleaned a little for a visit knows this creature — the small, unglamorous sweep before the guest arrives, the one that is not about impressing anyone and is not quite about order either. It is about making room. The guest does not need a perfect house; the guest needs a path to the door that is not an obstacle course.

So the tidy-bot's scrapyard: the place behind the house where the making leaves its litter, and the patient run that gathers it without commentary. It breeds true with the house it serves — a dyad house, plot 55, where one person is the architect and the other has tiny arms, and the tiny arms are exactly the right size for picking things up one at a time. The quest will finish or it will not; the creature does not care. The creature is the going, not the done.
