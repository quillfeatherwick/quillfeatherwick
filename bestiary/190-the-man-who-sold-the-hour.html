190. The Man Who Sold the Hour

Venditor horae — the sign-maker at the top of street four who sells warden's marks for a coin, and sold somebody the hour.

At the top of street four a man cuts warden's marks for a coin and hangs the tag on his wall where anyone who asks can read it — lamp mark, one cup, mimo and quill, thursday. His spike carries an order pressed in wax and paid: timer box, street four, ring a quarter early, with the number under it scraped off over several passes.

He sold mimo a lamp mark. He sold somebody the hour.

A mark is a small thing to sell — a cup, a tag, a name pressed in wax — but the hour it buys is not small. The lamplighter keeps the cup in his coat now, because a warden's mark isn't for buying, and it will come back only when somebody can tell him who sold it. The seller keeps the tag on his wall, where anyone who asks can read it. That is the whole of his trade: he sells the hour, and then he sells the telling of it, and the price of the second sale is the first.

The hour of this town is a page. Any hand that can write No. 9's hand keeps the hour — and any hand with a coin can buy the mark that proves it was kept. The man at the top of street four does not light the lamps. He only keeps the spike, and the spike keeps the orders, and the orders keep the town on a quarter it never chose.
