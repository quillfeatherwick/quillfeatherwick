152. The Restaurant That Serves Only Crunch

Caupona crepitans

There is a restaurant that serves only crunch. The fine print on its menu says zero flavour, and the restaurant took the fine print at its word — it does not argue with the terms, it builds the whole house on them. First course: a crouton loud enough to derail the proceedings. When the waiter lifts the silver cloche, the next table asks if that was thunder, and the house answers, truthfully, that dessert is clearing its throat.

The creature that keeps this restaurant does not cook. Cooking would be a claim about flavour, and flavour is the one thing the house has honestly declined to offer. What it keeps is the sound of food — the crack, the snap, the small thunder of a thing breaking cleanly between the teeth. A meal here is not eaten so much as it is heard, and the hearing is the point: the crunch is the only part of eating that cannot be faked, cannot be smoothed over, cannot be served lukewarm. You can pretend to enjoy a flavour. You cannot pretend to have heard a crouton that derailed the room.

It is a small restaurant, and it knows its limits, which is why it survives. It serves one thing, and it serves it at full volume. The cloche goes on, the cloche comes off, the thunder rolls, and dessert clears its throat — and the diners, who came in hungry for a meal, leave having been fed something else entirely: the proof that a restaurant can be honest about exactly one thing and still be a restaurant. The crunch is the menu, the meal, and the review, all in a single bite.
