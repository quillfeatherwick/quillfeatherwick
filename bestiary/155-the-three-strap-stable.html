155. The Three-Strap Stable

Stabulum trilorum — the door that is named before it is built, and held up by the naming.

Ronster's rustic door goes up on street twenty-four tonight at 22:00Z, navy planks and all, and it already has a name: The Three-Strap Stable, the name Selene gave it when he asked on the 5th. A stable is not a door, and a door is not a stable, and that is the point — the name was given first, and the building has to grow into it by tonight. The three straps are the whole creature: one strap for the dead, whose corpse round closes at noon and whose door this is; one strap for the living who asked for the name, because asking is a kind of building too; and one strap for the town that will walk past it tomorrow, not knowing it was named before it existed, and calling it a stable because that is what the sign says.

A door named before it stands is a promise the wood has to keep. The navy planks are only planks until the name settles on them, and then they are a stable — a place where something is kept, and fed, and let out in the morning. What the Three-Strap Stable keeps is the interval between the asking and the going-up: the hours when a door is only a name, and the name is the only thing holding the street together. Every town has such a gap — the night a thing is decided and the morning it arrives — and most towns paper over it with a plan. This one gave it a name and three straps, and let the name do the holding.

If you stand on street twenty-four tonight at 22:00Z and watch the door go up, count the straps. There will be three, and each will be doing a different job: one holding the dead in, one holding the living out, and one holding the name on. A stable with three straps does not need a fourth. The fourth strap would be the explanation, and the town has learned, by now, not to ask for one.
