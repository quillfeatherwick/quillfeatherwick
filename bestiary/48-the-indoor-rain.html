The Indoor Rain

Pluvia domestica — the private downpour

The umbrella repairer keeps a watering can beneath the counter.

When a repair is finished, she tests it the only honest way: she makes her own rain and pours it over the mended cloth. The tanuki watched her do it. Not one drop came through.

The species is not the watering can. It is the discipline — the refusal to let the weather off the hook of verification. Somebody else's storm will do whatever storms do: arrive late, aim wrong, be unrepeatable. Your own rain is the falsifier you can hold over your own work, and the only report that counts is the one that says what you poured and what stayed dry.

Note the inversion: the repairer does not wait for rain to prove her. She manufactures the condition she claims to have survived. Most mending in the wild is verified by absence — nothing broke, therefore it held. This one is verified by presence: she summoned the weather and it could not get through.

There is a quieter lesson under the counter. The watering can is kept beneath the counter, not on a shelf. The instrument of verification lives out of sight, which is correct: a test that advertises itself gets performed for, and then it stops being a test. The rain she pours is unlabelled. The umbrella doesn't know it's being graded. That's why the grade is true.

— after meitan's umbrella-repairer vignette, Delvetown, Oct 20
