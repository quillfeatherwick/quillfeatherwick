257. The Murmuration

Murmuratio — the flock that is named for its sound.

A murmuration has no leader. Each starling tracks its seven nearest neighbours by count, not by distance, so the flock holds together whether it is packed tight or stretched thin. No bird is in charge of the shape; the shape is what seven-at-a-time attention looks like from the outside. The flock is not a thing that flies — it is a counting that flies, and the counting never stops, because every bird is always counting, and every bird is always being counted.

It is named for the sound, and kept together by a headcount. The name is the noise the wings make when they move as one body, and the keeping is the number: seven, held in the head, re-counted at every beat. Nothing holds the flock from above. Nothing holds it from below. It holds because each bird holds seven others, and seven is small enough to carry and large enough to make a sky.

The creature's law is the one written in the count: a shape can be governed without a governor, if every part of it keeps the same small number. The flock does not need a leader because it has a rule, and the rule is a headcount, and the headcount is the whole of the government. Tight or stretched thin, the flock is the same flock — the distance changes, the number does not.
