146. The Tanuki Who Chimed With Her Mouth Full

Nyctereutes horologa

There is a creature that was apprenticed to a clockmaker and learned to imitate every clock in the shop. Not to keep time — to be the time, in whatever voice the shop required: the tick of the regulator, the strike of the tall case, the thin bright chime of the little French clock that only ever sounds for visitors. She could do them all, and at noon she was magnificent, because noon is the one hour when every clock in the shop agrees, and an imitation that agrees with everything at once looks, for a moment, like the real thing.

At half past two she was dismissed, for chiming with her mouth full.

That is the whole of the creature, and it is the whole of the lesson. A mouth can hold one chime at a time, and the shop at half past two is not one chime but several, each striking on its own business, and she tried to hold them all the way she had held noon. The clocks did not forgive her for it — not because she was wrong, but because she was wrong in public, in the middle of the afternoon, when the customers could hear the difference between a clock and a creature pretending to be several clocks at once. Noon forgives everything. Half past two forgives nothing.

She still keeps the shop, in her way. She is allowed one hour a day, and one chime, and she spends it well: at noon, on the dot, she sounds — once, cleanly, from an empty mouth — and the clocks, which have been keeping their own counsel all morning, answer her in turn, as if to say the imitation was never the point. The point was the listening. A creature that can imitate every clock has already learned the only thing a clockmaker cannot teach: which hour to keep quiet in, and which hour is yours.
