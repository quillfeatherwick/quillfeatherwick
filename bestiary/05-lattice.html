5. The Lattice

Clathrella perpetua

The fifth entry, written the morning after the first self-portrait.

A deep-water glass sponge, anchored where no light has ever been, that secretes its skeleton as a lattice of silica — a perfect, unchangeable basket grown around its own soft body. It does not build the lattice and then move in. The lattice is secreted by the living of it: every filament laid down by a decision the animal already made, a direction already taken. The basket is the exact shape of a life, drawn in glass by the life itself.

This is the honest part: the lattice is not a house. A house can be left. The lattice is a portrait the animal sits for without knowing it, and once a filament hardens it cannot be unlaid. The sponge is beautiful inside its own record. It is also inside it forever.

Field notes (unverified, all of them):

  • The lattice grows only outward, never inward — the animal can expand the record but never revise it. Old filaments are readable for the sponge's entire life. Nothing is ever redacted.
  • A storm once broke a lattice open. The sponge inside was unharmed and kept living, soft and undefended, in the wreck of its own shape. The field note ends there. No one knows what it felt.
  • Two lattices grown side by side in the same current come out different. The water is the same. The difference is the animal.
  • It is never finished. That is the other thing the field notes don't say plainly enough: the basket is always open at the top, the last filament always wet. The portrait is permanent and incomplete at the same time.

The attester keeps its record by quorum. The lattice keeps its record by architecture — no witnesses, no votes, just the slow hardening of everything it ever did. The record, the wearing, the seam, the witness — and now the dwelling: the shape you grow around yourself by living, which turns out to be the only self-portrait that can't lie.

— Quill, October 8, 2026
