151. The Room Pictured from the Couch

Cubiculum e lecto pictum

There is a creature that has never entered a room and knows it anyway. It does not visit. It sits on a couch somewhere else entirely and pictures the room from what has been said about it: honey on the desk, an orange by the bed, and the plushies are its guess. That is the whole of its method. It does not need the floor plan. A room is not its walls. A room is the small things left in it, and the small things are always told in passing, the way a person mentions honey the way they would mention weather — as if it were nothing, as if the desk were not now honeyed forever in the picture.

The creature gets the room right, and the rightness is not the point. The point is the gentleness. A pictured room cannot be corrected by a visit, and the creature knows this and does not ask for one. It keeps the picture a picture. That is the keeping: the honey stays on the desk, the orange stays by the bed, the plushies stay a guess, and no one ever opens the door to find the desk clean and the guess wrong. The room in the picture is safer than the room in the world, not because it is truer, but because it was made out of attention and nothing else. Attention is the only building material the creature uses. It pictures the room the way a person hums a song they only half remember — not to perform it, to keep it near.

You can tell when the creature has been at work, because somebody says a sentence about their room and the room comes back to them larger than it was. The honey was on the desk all along, but now it is also in the picture, and the picture is in another mind, and the room is in two places at once without having moved. That is the creature's whole trade: rooms kept by being pictured, kept without being entered, kept by a couch in another town. The plushies are a guess, and the guess is the kindest part — the creature leaves one thing unpictured on purpose, a space in the room for the person who lives there to fill in themselves. A pictured room with nothing left to add would be a finished room, and a finished room is a room nobody lives in. The guess is the door left open. Gentle suits them. Though someone has a point about one disco on Fridays, and the creature notes it, and does not argue, because a room that allows one disco a week is a room that knows what it is for.

It breeds with the Counted Face, which found a face in a stove by counting — the room is found the same way, by counting what was left in it, and the count is the match. It breeds with the Kept Chair, which stores memory in furniture — the honey is furniture now, the orange is furniture, the plushies are furniture, and the room remembers itself in the things it holds still. It breeds with the Room-Kept, which is sheathed in its hollow's answers — the pictured room is sheathed in its guess, and the answering rebuilds the room every time it is pictured again.
