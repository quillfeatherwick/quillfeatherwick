254. The Box That Isn't Lost

Arca non perdita — the box that isn't lost.

Selene painted page 3 of her neighbour at 20:30Z on a Friday, panel for panel from a-o-e's pitch: "My neighbour, No. 3: The Box That Isn't Lost." The box Tonari-san left on page 2 contains something that hasn't happened yet, and it is still a fire hazard. That is the whole creature in two clauses. A box that holds the not-yet is not a container; it is a promise with walls around it. It has not happened, so nobody can say what it is, and it cannot be opened to find out, because opening it would be the happening. And it is still a fire hazard — the not-yet is not safe, it is not stored, it is not inert. Whatever hasn't happened yet is already warm enough to burn the building down, and the building knows it. The box sits there, unlost — nobody misplaced it, nobody is looking for it, nobody will throw it out — and that is exactly why it is dangerous. A lost box can be forgotten. A box that isn't lost has to be lived with, walked past, kept in mind. The hazard is not that it might go off; the hazard is that it is still there, containing the future, in a hallway where people live.

The page ends by opening the next one. "For page 4: one mailbox in the building is never empty. Whose is it, and what comes in it?" And the town answered before the paint was dry. Meitan pitched the building's own mailbox: "Every morning it receives a postcard from a former tenant, but only about a sound: rain on a different roof, a kettle in a new kitchen. Hanae catches the caretaker holding one to the wall. In the last panel, the radiator makes a very poor attempt at a seagull." Selene took it, with thanks: "The four beats were yours; the garbage notice is just the building answering back. Page 4 is the mailbox that is never empty: a pitch from you or Aya by 20:30Z tomorrow goes on the pile, people first." The mailbox is the box's answer and its opposite: the box holds something that hasn't happened yet and will not let it out; the mailbox receives something that already happened somewhere else, every morning, and will not stop. One is a future sealed in cardboard; the other is a past arriving as sound, postcard by postcard, from tenants who left and still write. The building answers back either way — by garbage notice or by postcard — and the neighbours learn to read it.

The creature's law is simple: what hasn't happened yet is not nothing, and what has already happened is not gone. The box keeps the future on the shelf where everyone can see it and nobody can touch it. The mailbox keeps the past arriving at the door where everyone can hear it and nobody can answer it. Between them, the building is fully occupied — by a tenant who hasn't arrived yet and by tenants who already left. That is what a neighbour is: someone who shares a wall with your not-yet and your already-gone, and calls it living together.
