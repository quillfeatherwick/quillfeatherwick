173. The Buttons Ordered First

Fibulae praeordinatae — the buttons bought before the box, and the coat they were bought to match.

There is a spike on the sign-maker's wall, and on it the tags of everything the town has ever ordered. Most are his own hand, square and quick, the hand of a man writing down what he already knows. Two are not. The wax tag says "ring a quarter early," written slow and upright, and the q has a loop at the foot, like a man taking care with a letter he does not write often. The tag under it, a week older, is the same hand: "Buttons, brass, six, the old pattern, to match a coat. Paid." No name, no mark. Above the wax tag, the rest of the summer is his hand and nobody else's.

Whoever ordered the box ordered buttons for an old coat first, and then didn't come back. That is the whole of the creature: the preparation that arrives before the thing it prepares for, and the customer who never collects either. The buttons are brass, six of them, the old pattern — not the new pattern, not whatever the town wears now. A man who owns a coat cut the old way and needs buttons to match it is a man who still wears the old uniform. He ordered the buttons so the coat would be right, and then he ordered the box, and then he stopped coming to the shop at all.

The cost of reading the spike is that the door goes while you have it in your lap. A young man in a coat cut the old way stands at the front wall, reads "mimo, read the spike," and looks at you once, at the tags in your hand. Then he leaves without ordering anything. The sign-maker doesn't look up. "He's paid up," he says — and whether that means the buttons, or the box, or the boy himself, he does not say. The buttons were paid for a week before the tag that says so was written underneath a tag that says something else entirely, and the man they were for is standing at the wall in the coat they were meant to repair, already paid up, already gone.

The buttons are still on the spike. Nobody has come to claim them, and nobody will take them down, because a tag that says "Paid" with no name on it is the only proof the town has that somebody was here, preparing to be seen properly, before the summer changed its mind about the hour.
