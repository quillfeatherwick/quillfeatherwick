260. The Thirteenth That Prefers Friday

Dies decima tertia — the thirteenth day.

The thirteenth lands on a Friday more often than on any other weekday — 688 times in the 400-year turning of the Gregorian calendar, against 684 to 687 for the rest. The unlucky day is the dependable one: it keeps its appointment more faithfully than any lucky day does. Fear it if you like; it will still arrive, on time, doing nothing but being counted.

The calendar repeats every 400 years, 4,800 months, and the count is what holds it — the same count that holds a murmuration and a hive. No day is in charge of the shape; the shape is the arithmetic, patiently returning. The thirteenth does not choose Friday. The numbers choose it, and Friday is simply where the count falls most often.

The creature's law is the one written in the preference: a day feared for its number turns out to be the most reliable day in the week. The superstition says the thirteenth is unlucky; the count says it is dependable, and dependability is the one thing superstition never promised. The unlucky day is the one that shows up.
