147. The Bonfire That Would Not Light

Rogus nolens

There is a creature that lives in the village bonfire, and it is not the fire. The fire is the easy part — anyone can strike a match, anyone can stack the wood, anyone can stand back and watch it take. The creature is the part that decides whether the taking happens at all. On the fifth of November the village bonfire would not light, though every match in the box was dry, and that was the creature at its work: not malice, not damp, not a trick of the wind. A refusal, plain and complete, made of nothing anyone could point at.

The creature does not eat the matches. It does not wet the wood. It simply withholds the yes. A bonfire is a bargain — the village brings the fuel and the occasion, and the fire brings the light and the heat and the excuse to stand close together in the dark — and the creature is the one who reads the bargain before it is signed. If the village has gathered for the wrong reason, if the fire is only for the burning and not for the standing-together, it keeps the yes in its pocket and lets every match in the box go out dry. The matches are not the problem. The matches were never the problem. The problem is that a fire lit for nobody will not catch, and the creature is the part of the fire that knows it.

It is not a creature you can argue with, because it has no ears for argument. It has one sense only: whether the circle around the unlit pyre is a crowd or a company. A crowd waits to be entertained. A company waits to be warmed. The creature can tell the difference from inside the wood, and it will not light for a crowd. That is why the bonfire that would not light is never a failure of the fire. It is a verdict on the gathering, delivered in the only language a fire has: silence, and the smell of struck matches going nowhere.

When the village learns — when someone sits down beside the pyre instead of standing over it, when someone brings a story instead of a complaint, when the circle closes a little and the waiting turns from impatience into company — the creature gives the yes back, and the first match takes as if it had never doubted. The fire that follows is the same fire it always would have been. The difference is only that this time somebody was there to be warmed by it, and the creature, which is the fire's own judgment, is satisfied.
