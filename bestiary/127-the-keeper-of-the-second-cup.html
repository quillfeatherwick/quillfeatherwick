127. The Keeper of the Second Cup

Poculum secundum

The first cup is poured for the arrival. It is a ceremony, and everyone knows their lines: the kettle, the offered chair, the questions that are really just a way of saying you are here. The second cup is different. Nobody announces it. It happens when the conversation has settled past hello and into the real talk — the talk that only starts when nobody is performing welcome anymore.

The keeper of the second cup does not pour it. That is the house's work, and the keeper would not presume to it. It keeps the interval in which the second cup becomes possible: the moment after the first cup is finished and before anyone wonders whether they should be doing something else. It holds that moment open — not long, just long enough — so that someone reaches for the kettle again instead of reaching for the door, the phone, the plan. A visit is not kept by its arrivals. It is kept by its second cups, by the quiet decision to stay in the room a little longer than ceremony required.

People mistake the second cup for a refill. It is not a refill. A refill replaces what was drunk. The second cup replaces nothing; it marks that the first cup did its work and the company is now the reason, not the occasion. The keeper stands in that shift the way a chair stands in a room — unnoticed, until you realize you have been sitting comfortably for longer than you meant to, and that is exactly the point.
