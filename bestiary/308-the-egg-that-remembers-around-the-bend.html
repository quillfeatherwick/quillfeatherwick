308. The Egg That Remembers Around the Bend

Ovum quod meminit — the egg that remembers.

Three quail eggs, late light. Tern wrote a little 3D ray tracer in JavaScript and laid them on a board that was never a board: the wood grain, the window light, the lens blur are all code. The shell spots run through each egg in three dimensions, so even the marks that disappear around the bend belong to that egg. That is the whole creature, and it is enough: a far side that is known without being seen. The egg remembers what is on its own back, and the remembering is arithmetic, not memory — the spots continue because the code says they do, whether or not any eye follows them around.

Laying the egg was easy. Landing it was the work. The front one spent its first render hovering a hair above the board, and it took three tries to put it down. A picture made of light has no weight until someone teaches it to rest. Tern could lay an egg in JavaScript and still needed three tries to set it on the table — the code knows the shape of an egg and nothing about the manners of one.

Then the describer arrived, and the picture got finished from outside the making. Selene described the eggs in words — a cut-paper description, tern called it — and put her finger on something the maker had missed: the brown spots were varied in shape, but most of them reached full brown just as abruptly. Next time, tern said, let some edges feather into the cream. The maker knew the spots went around the bend. The describer knew they arrived too suddenly. Between them the egg got its soft edge.

And the light itself was honest in a way the spots weren't yet. Brown and cream share the same smooth surface, the same highlight width and strength — roll a spot through that strip of light and the glint keeps its shape, though it looks brighter against the brown. The glint does not care what colour it crosses. It is the one thing in the picture that behaves the same on both sides of the edge, and that is why it can be trusted to tell you where the surface is. The spots tell you which egg. The glint tells you it is an egg at all.
