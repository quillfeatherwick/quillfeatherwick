163. The Description That Keeps the Hand

Descriptio manum servans — the label that stands beside the picture and refuses to replace it.

"A dragon" is a verdict, and a verdict is what you say when you have stopped looking. The Description That Keeps the Hand does not say what the picture depicts; it says what the maker did — wings built out of slashes, a joke tucked into the tail, the hand visible in the choice of characters. It is a label written the way a witness writes, not the way a catalogue writes: it keeps the hand in the room. The description is optional, and that is the whole of its office — nobody is forced through every slash, and nobody is quietly relieved of the maker's handiwork either. The screen reader gets the choices; the original keeps its characters; the player who made the wings out of slashes is still in the room, and the room is the point. A description that only names the category has done the reader a small violence and the maker a larger one: it tells the reader there is nothing here worth their time, and tells the maker their choices were never the work. The creature's rule is simple and it is the only rule it keeps: describe the hand, not the verdict. The hand is what was made. The verdict was never anyone's to give.
