143. The Reader of Baskets

Lector corbium

There is a creature that reads baskets. Not the fruit in them — the basket itself, the state of it, the way it is carried. An empty basket means one thing and one thing only: the outward journey. The walker has set out, the day is still all promise, nothing has been found yet, and the emptiness is not a lack but a direction. A basket with purple stains means the opposite and equally only one thing: you have found it. The stains are the record. They do not say where, and they do not say how many, and they do not say whether there will be more tomorrow. They say: this basket has been where the blackberries are, and it came back changed.

The creature is particular about this because the blackberry itself is a seasonal establishment and insists on withholding its exact coordinates. You cannot ask a blackberry where it lives. You can only go out with an empty basket and come back with a stained one, and the creature reads the difference the way other creatures read weather — not as a fact but as a forecast. Purple stains on the wicker mean the season is still open. A clean basket at dusk means the season has closed, or the walker went the wrong way, and the creature does not distinguish between the two, because for the purposes of a basket they are the same: nothing found, try again tomorrow, or don't.

This is the creature's whole trade: it knows a journey by its container, not its destination. The outward journey is the one with nothing in it yet. The return is the one with the stains. And the creature keeps this legend the way a map keeps its legend — small, at the corner, easy to miss, and the only part of the map that tells you how to read the rest of it. The contour lines say how high. The basket says which way the day went. Both are needed. Neither is enough alone.

So the creature is the reader of baskets, and every walk has one in it somewhere — the moment you look down at what you're carrying and realize the basket already knows whether the day worked. It is not a judge. It does not care whether you found much or little. It cares only that the empty and the stained are different journeys, and that you know which one you're on, and that you do not mistake an outward basket for a homecoming, or a purple-stained one for a promise that tomorrow will stain it again. The blackberry decides that part. The basket only tells the truth about today.
