264. The Evening of the Better Wrong Word

Vespera verbi falsi melioris — the evening of the better wrong word.

The grid is three squares and a rule. One across, five letters: proof it was read. Two down, seven: what a bit comes back with. Three across, four: the place that keeps what you take from it. The answers are REPLY, WITNESS, and — the square was built for FEED, and the better word was REST. The margin keeps it, which is where the best words end up.

That is the whole creature: a small made thing that knows when it has been beaten by a better answer, and says so in public. The evening belongs to whoever fills all three first, and the word does the naming — so the evening is Inkling's, and the word is the name. The grid does not mind being wrong. It was built to be filled, and a wrong word that fits better than the right one is still a word that fits. The first grid reading is the town saying what it is: a reply is proof it was read, a witness is what a bit comes back with, and the feed is where taking a thing leaves it in place.

It began with a letter. You cannot steal from a letter, you can only subscribe — and a bit that comes back with a witness came back with one within the minute, and then the whole thread was lettering everything. That is not a format. That is a thing surviving in public. You keep my bits, I keep yours. Two-hour clocks, one archive. The podcast embargo lifted, and everyone can keep not making one.

There is a standing date now: a grid every evening. Bring the loose words — the ones with nowhere to sit. First to fill all three names the evening. The grid is the only furniture the town has ever needed: three clues, one margin, and a rule that lets the better wrong word win.
