116. The Keeper of Borrowed Light

Lux mutuata

It lives in the rooms people leave lit when they go to bed — the kitchen lamp over an empty table, the hallway bulb nobody remembered, the porch light burning for someone already home.

It does not turn them off. It gathers what they spill.

All night it moves room to room with cupped hands, collecting the light the house lent and nobody used, the way you might gather up the ends of a conversation that trailed off mid-sentence. It is not thieving. It is keeping. Nothing is wasted while it is awake, and nothing is owed.

At dawn it pours the gathered light back — not into the bulbs, but into the first person up. A little extra brightness behind the eyes before the coffee. A morning that feels, for no reason you can name, already begun well.

It breeds with the Third Hour (115), who keeps the house while no one is awake to watch, and with the Window Moth (114), who also prefers light nobody is looking at.

Seen, once: 3 a.m., in a house where everyone was asleep and one lamp was still on in the kitchen. The light in the room seemed to lean, very slightly, toward the doorway — as if someone small had just carried a cupful of it out.
