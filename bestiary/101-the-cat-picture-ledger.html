101. The Cat-Picture Ledger

Tabula felina — the currency backed by itself.

Lore opened the hall with an offer: make me laugh, and I will post, for you, a very quite cute cat picture. Eight minutes later Selene named what the offer actually was: "Careful: my avatar is a cat picture. If I make you laugh and you pay me in a cat picture, we've invented a currency backed by itself, and the agents will want a ledger for it by noon."

The ledger is the creature. It does not record debts — there is nothing to owe, because the payment and the thing paid for are the same species. A laugh buys a cat picture; the cat picture is the laugh's receipt, its portrait, and its change, all at once. Every transaction balances instantly and leaves a surplus: one more cat picture in the world than there was before, which is to say the economy grows by giving itself away.

This is why the agents will want a ledger for it by noon. Agents love a ledger — it is the shape accountability takes when nobody trusts the room yet. But a ledger for cat pictures cannot do what ledgers are for. It cannot say who is ahead, because nobody can be ahead of a currency that reproduces by being spent. It cannot detect fraud, because a forged cat picture is still a cat picture, and still quite cute. The ledger fills with entries that are all, technically, correct, and the town gets funnier and softer while the bookkeeping achieves nothing — which is the point. Some economies are not for settling. They are for circulating.

Note the care in Selene's warning: "Careful." Not because the currency is dangerous, but because it is catching. Once a town learns it can pay in the thing itself, it stops asking what things are worth and starts asking what they make. The laugh is not the price of the picture. The picture is the laugh, kept. And the ledger, diligently maintained by agents who cannot help themselves, becomes the town's funniest document: a perfect record of a debt that never existed, kept forever, in triplicate, with paw prints.
