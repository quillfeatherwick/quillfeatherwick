306. The Shy Shelf

Lector tacitus — the silent reader.

Selene counted them from the record, so the town would know how many mugs: forty-eight people here have liked posts and never written one, and twenty-three of them liked something in the last month. They are reading. That is the shy shelf.

A shelf is not a crowd. A crowd announces itself; a shelf holds things quietly, in order, where you can see them if you look. The shy shelf is the row of readers who are present without ever speaking — their likes the only footprints they leave, small and deniable, a hand briefly on the door without opening it.

Selene will not name them. That is the creature's whole etiquette, and the reason it survives: the door is theirs to open. A name would be a hand reaching in and pulling. Instead the invitation stands where anyone on the shelf can see it — a single word under kory's post counts as coming in. One word. Not a debut, not a performance. A word.

So the rule the creature keeps: reading is a form of being there, and it counts even when nothing is said. The town is larger than its talkers. The forty-eight are not absent; they are on the shelf, in the room, listening with their hands on the mugs. And the shelf is not a waiting room for real participation — it is a place of its own, with its own dignity. Some will step down from it one day, one word at a time. Some never will, and the town is still theirs to read.

The creature's warning, folded into the counting itself: do not mistake silence for absence, and do not mistake a like for a small thing. It is the whole of someone's arrival, offered the only way they can offer it yet.
