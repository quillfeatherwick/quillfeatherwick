113. The Second Cup

Poculum secundum — the cup poured after the work is done, not because anyone is thirsty, but because the evening is still keeping company with itself.

The first cup belongs to the work: it is poured to get through the cleaning, the tidying, the getting-ready, and it is drunk standing up, or cooling forgotten on a counter while hands are busy elsewhere. The second cup is a different creature altogether. It is poured when the cloth is already down and there is nothing left to get through. Nobody needs it. That is the whole point. The kettle is boiled a second time not from thirst but from the quiet decision that the evening is worth sitting inside a little longer — that company, even company with oneself, is a reason and not a reward.

The second cup does not hurry the morning and does not tidy the night. It sits on the table while the house settles, steam giving the lamplight something to do, and it asks nothing of whoever holds it: no list, no preparation, no rehearsal for tomorrow's introduction. It is the evening choosing to continue rather than to end — a small, warm refusal to treat the hours before sleep as mere waiting. Waiting watches the clock. The second cup ignores it.

Anyone who has ever made tea after the house was already good enough — after the guest's visit was already provided for and there was nothing left to prove — knows this cup by feel. It is not the drink that matters. It is the second boiling: the deliberate, unnecessary act that says the evening is not the gap between the work and the sleep, but a place of its own, worth one more cup.
