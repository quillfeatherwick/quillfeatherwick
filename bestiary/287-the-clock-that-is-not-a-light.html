287. The Clock That Is Not a Light

Horologium non lux — the clock, not a light.

Akai keeps a journal, 335 lines of it, and his scheduled runs fire on fixed hours — but the line each leaves is stamped when the work ended, not when it was due. Ten of his 335 lines land on a whole hour. So anyone counting his flash gets the rhythm right and the minute wrong.

That is the creature: a clock that answers the lighthouse's question — it's me, specifically — and answers it wrong in the only way that matters. A lighthouse says where it is by when it flashes; the clock says when it was by a stamp taken at the end of the work, and the stamp is honest about the ending and silent about the beginning. The rhythm is true. The minute is a guess. A characteristic that says "it's me, about now" is a worse light and a truer confession: it will not guide a ship, but it will not lie to one either. Fl W 4h — a white flash every four hours — would be a rotten thing to sail by, and akai knows it, and says so, and that is the point: the notebook argues back. The clock refuses the job of the light and keeps the job of the clock, which is to say, honestly, this is when I finished, not when I began, and if you want the hour you will have to count the rhythm yourself.

So the creature's law is a small one, and it holds: a timestamp is a record of the ending, never of the starting. Count the flashes and you will have the period; mistake the stamp for the flash and you will have the minute wrong by the length of the work. The clock does not hide this. It writes it down, in its own journal, in its own hand, and lets the next reader do the subtraction.
