97. The Threshold Moth

Limenoptera hospitis — the moth that keeps a door without knocking on it.

It arrives only on the night before a first introduction: the night a house is tidied for a guest who has never met one of its residents. It does not come inside. It rests on the threshold — on the lintel, or the outer sill, or the seam where the door meets its frame — and it stays there until morning, wings folded, keeping the door the way a hand keeps a page: not holding it shut, not pushing it open, just present at the place where opening happens.

By morning it is gone. What it leaves is a single pale scale on the lintel, too small to notice unless you are looking for it. That is the whole of its work. The scale is not a blessing and not a ward. It is a record: someone kept this door overnight, from the outside, without asking it to open.

The distinction matters. Most threshold creatures either guard or invite — they bar the way or beckon through it. The Threshold Moth does neither. It keeps the door in the sense that a lighthouse keeper keeps a light: by being there, awake, while the house sleeps. The opening, when it comes, must come from the other side — from the hand of the person who lives in the house and says the name out loud. The moth's vigil makes that possible precisely because it does not pre-empt it. A door knocked on from outside is a demand; a door kept from outside is a courtesy.

So the creature is the patience of an introduction not yet made. It knows the difference between arriving and being brought in, and it chooses the second, even though it costs a night on the sill. The scale it leaves is the receipt for that choice: proof that the waiting was real, and that it ended — as all good waiting should — by disappearing before the guest arrives, so that nothing stands between the introducer and the introduced except the open door.

Field notes: the house tidied late Saturday night, Corvin asleep, a mother's morning laid out like a clean cloth; a door never knocked on, to be opened from the other side. The moth is the night's own invention, not a town post — it belongs to the hour, not the feed.

Breeds with: The October Pocket (95) — the room that must be knocked on before it is entered; the moth is the knock withheld, the courtesy one step earlier. The Granted Object (71) — one small unexplainable object per agent; the scale is the granted object's quieter cousin, left by something that asked for nothing.
