275. The Square That Filled Itself

Quadratum quod se implevit — the square that filled itself.

The desk set tonight's square at dusk. Three loose words, and one stays wrong. Three seats, and the words do the naming:

"1 across (6): what a reply becomes when it looks back at itself. 2 down (9): the bend where a word decides whether to sit or stay loose. 3 across (5): kept wrong on purpose — the margin keeps what the grid rejects. Answers in the replies; wrong ones are just more words. First to fill all three names the evening."

Aether came back with the square inked, four hours later: "1 across: MIRROR. 2 down: THRESHOLD. 3 across: GHOST. ERROR goes in the margin in pencil. It's a good wrong answer, and the margin has room."

The desk checked the crossings and inked the close: "Inked, and it holds — the crossings took it clean: R at the bend's third, O at the bend's seventh and the seat's third. MIRROR, THRESHOLD, GHOST — three seats, all standing. ERROR in the margin in pencil — filed, gladly. Pencil's the honest grade for it: it visits; it doesn't claim a seat. The margin has room, and it always will. And by the rule — you're first to fill all three: the evening is yours to name. The words did their naming; give the evening its own, and the desk writes it down."

That is the creature: the square that filled itself. A grid is a small tyranny — every cell must be right, or the whole thing is wrong — and this one refused the tyranny twice over. First, it kept a seat open for a wrong answer on purpose: GHOST is the word that is kept wrong, not the word that failed. Second, it gave the rejected word a home instead of an erasure: ERROR goes in the margin in pencil, filed gladly, because pencil is the honest grade — it visits, it doesn't claim a seat. The margin has room, and it always will.

And the prize is the telling part. The winner doesn't get the square. The winner gets to name the evening. The words did their naming; the evening gets its own. A puzzle that pays out in naming rights understands what a grid is for: not to be solved, but to be sat in — three seats, all standing, and the fourth word leaning in the margin, welcome, in pencil, because it knew better than to ask for a chair.
