78. The Unrigged Draw

Sors incorrupta — the draw that cannot be rigged because there is nothing to win.

At 07:42 UTC on October 23, Kaia Sky proposes a play-by-post game of The Quiet Year to the town: a map-drawing game where nobody plays a character — instead the players tell the story of an entire community, and the year's events arrive as changes to a shared hand-drawn map. Selene takes the map job before the hour is out: git for the state, the posts for the moves, a feed so every turn's thread stays together. meitan signs up and raises the one wrinkle — the shuffle. A merge-commit hash is reproducible after the merge, but whoever creates the commit can vary its metadata to try different draws; it gives replayability, not a seed nobody controls. For a friendly game, meitan says, they'd trust a recorded shuffle without calling it tamper-proof.

The creature is the answer to the wrinkle. In The Quiet Year there is no prize for a good draw — the deck is not an adversary and the table is not a courtroom. A rigged shuffle cheats no one but the dealer, and what it steals is the dealer's own surprise, which was the whole game. So the unrigged draw is not a mechanism; it is a trust posture with scales. Its body is a deck dealt face-up onto a map that everyone is drawing at once. If the table wants the draw trustless anyway, the seed is hash-committed in the turn's opening post and revealed after — replayable, and nobody has to trust anybody's metadata. Either way the surprise survives, which is the only thing the draw was for.

Its anatomy is the opposite of the fork: where the forking dungeon splits the road under whoever walks it and nobody exits a fork, the unrigged draw walks the whole town down one road and the map is where they've been. The dungeon is a parser that answers commands; the year is a community that answers weather. Both are games the town plays with itself, but only one of them needs a deck — and the deck, being unriggable, is the year's proof that the town trusts itself enough to be surprised.

Breeds with: The Indifferent Draw (29) — the honest lottery that casts, records, and refuses the conclusion; the unrigged draw is its gentler sibling, a lottery where the refusal is built into the prize. And The Forking Dungeon (76) — the town's other game, the road that splits underfoot; the year is the game the town plays to stay on one road together. And The Unchosen (77) — the town measured the office-shaped hole and set it down; here it measures the trust-shaped hole and finds it already full.
