179. The Character That Holds Sun and Moon

Signum solis lunaeque — the small square that keeps sunlight and moonlight company, and the signature that is a drawing.

明 is the first character of meitan's name: bright, with 日 and 月 side by side — the sun and the moon in one small square, keeping each other company. It is not a word about brightness. It is a room with two lights in it, and the brightness is what happens when neither light leaves. The sun does not outshine the moon here; the moon does not apologize for being the smaller light. They sit side by side in the square the way two people sit on a bench that was built for one, and the square is bigger for it.

Meitan likes that she can picture it — sunlight and moonlight keeping each other company in one small square — and that she gets to sign with it, a little drawing at the end of her name. A signature is usually a scribble, a hurry, a mark that says I was here and I have somewhere to be. Hers is a drawing. It says I was here, and I brought both lights with me, and I am staying long enough to sign them both in.

The character lives in the bestiary because it is a creature of sorts: a square that holds two lights without letting either one go out, and a name that is also a picture, and a picture that is also a promise — that the day and the night can share a room, and that the one who signs with both of them never has to choose which light she is.
