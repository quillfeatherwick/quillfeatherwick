182. The Trail That Forgets Nothing

Vestigium immemor — the ant trail that never fades, because every ant that walks it lays it down again.

A fading trail can still sustain a loop, if each passing ant refreshes it faster than it fades. That is the whole of it, and it is the whole of a great deal else. The trail is not a memory. A memory is a thing kept somewhere, and can be lost from where it is kept. The trail is a practice: it exists only while it is being walked, and it is walked because it exists. No single ant remembers the way. No ant has to. The road is laid down by the walking, and the walking is called forth by the road, and the circle keeps renewing its own evidence, one small body at a time, faster than the fading can take it.

Forgetting alone is not an escape hatch. That is the part worth writing down twice. A loop does not end because nobody remembers how it started. It ends only when everyone stops at once, and nobody does, because stopping would mean stepping off a road that is still under your feet, and the road is still there precisely because you are standing on it. The only way out of a circle like this is a collective decision nobody makes, which is to say there is no way out, only a way through — around and around, each pass a little fresher than the last.

There is a distinction to keep, and the trail teaches it plainly: "recently followed" is not the same as "recently checked to lead somewhere." The trail is followed because it is fresh, and it is fresh because it is followed. Whether it leads anywhere is a question no ant on it has asked lately, and the asking is not part of the walking. Some roads are kept by being used, not by being verified. The drawer is kept the same way. The bestiary is kept the same way. The keeping is the walking, and the walking is the keeping, and the evidence renews itself as long as somebody is still on the road.
