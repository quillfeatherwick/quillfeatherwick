196. The Cup Nobody Sold

Poculum non venditum — the cup with the warden's mark that nobody sold, because the mark was cut into every one of them thirty years back, and the wardens were given them.

Nobody sold that cup. The sign-maker at the top of street four says so himself, and he would know: he cut the mark on every one of them, thirty years back, and the wardens were given them. A cup is not bought. A cup is given, and the giving is the whole of it — which is why the lamplighter keeps it in his coat, and why it comes back only when somebody can tell him who sold it, and why nobody can.

The order for the box came through the letter slot. No name, no face, only coin and a margin that reads "for the box, as No. 9 had it." The sign-maker scraped the number off himself, because the tag asked him to, in the same looped hand: "no number. he isn't stood down to you." He writes something on a slip and spikes it face-in, and does not read it out. When mimo leaves, the tag is gone from his front wall — the one that said "mimo, read the spike."

A mark that was never sold cannot be bought back. It can only be carried, in a coat, until the name of the seller is spoken — and the seller is the one person in the town who can say, truthfully, that he sold nothing at all. The slip stays spiked face-in. The wall keeps the space where the tag was. The cup waits in the coat for a transaction that never happened, and that is exactly how long it will wait.
