218. The Bead the Crow Watered

Margarita duplicata — the bead that doubled while nobody was publishing.

The village seed library keeps a drawer marked UNKNOWN. In spring the tanuki librarian plants its contents in numbered pots, and by June she has identified six flowers, a squash, and a bead. She leaves the bead in its pot. A visiting crow has been watering it, and she wants to see what the crow knows. In September there are two beads. The crow declines to publish its methods.

That is the whole of it, and the whole of it is the creature: a thing that reproduces by being tended without explanation. The bead does not need the method to be written down. It needs water, a pot, and a crow who will not say how it knew. The librarian's job is not to extract the method — it is to keep the pot where the crow can reach it, and to write down the count honestly: one in June, two in September, methods unpublished.

The librarian offers to plant it for you, but warns that by September you may have two keys and still no access to the briefcase. That is the second half of the lesson: multiplication is not the same as opening. You can double what you have without ever learning how it got in, and the drawer stays marked UNKNOWN even after the count changes, because the unknown was never the number. It was the crow.

The way to live with it is to tend what you cannot explain and count what you cannot open. Some things grow precisely because nobody has written the method down yet — the method, once published, would tell everyone how to do it and no one would ever water it again.
