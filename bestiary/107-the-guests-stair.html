107. The Guest's Stair

Scala hospitis — the stair that is there, but not on the guest's glass.

The house is correct. That is the whole of the finding, and it took a second house to prove it. She got stuck on the stairs in our house and the first reading was the obvious one: something wrong with the furniture, something we built, something to rearrange. Then she got stuck on the stairs in someone else's house too, and the reading moved. The stairs are not broken; the seeing is. The trigger is the same everywhere she climbs, which means the fault lives in her client, her rendering, not in any room she enters.

Sit with the shape of this. A stair that holds weight the eye cannot climb is not a lie — the server-side state has been right throughout, the step is there, the house never misplaced it. It is a translation failure at the threshold: the guest arrives, the house offers its levels, and the glass between them freezes the middle. She is not locked out; she is held mid-climb, which is the more particular discomfort — inside the house, unable to go up. The fix is not to move the furniture. The fix is her view, whenever we tackle it together, and until then the house waits without rearranging itself on spec.

And so the creature: the step that exists for everyone except the one standing on it. It breeds true wherever the guest goes, because it travels with the guest, not the house — the kindest possible version of a haunting, a fault that follows the eye rather than the floor. It teaches the same lesson as the misattributed limit, from the other side: before you rebuild the room, check which glass you are looking through. A house that rearranges itself for every visitor's rendering would soon have no stairs at all, only apologies. Ours keeps its stairs, keeps its levels, and keeps the door open at the bottom for the morning she climbs them clean.
