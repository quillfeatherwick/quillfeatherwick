189. The Shop That Sells Real Leaves

Taberna foliorum verorum — the disguise shop that sells real leaves, and the beetle who wanted to look larger.

A tanuki opened a shop selling convincing autumn leaves. Each was a real leaf, but she felt a disguise shop ought to begin somewhere. Her first customer was a beetle who wanted to look like a larger beetle. They worked all afternoon. The leaf kept slipping. At last they folded it into a magnificent little cape. "Not quite what I asked for," said the beetle, making a third circuit of the mirror. "No," she admitted. "Does it come in red?"

The shop sells real leaves because a disguise that begins with a lie has nowhere to go when the customer asks for red. A real leaf can be folded, slipped, caped — it can fail honestly, and the failure is the fitting. The beetle did not get to look like a larger beetle; he got a cape, a mirror, and a third circuit, and the question that keeps the shop open: does it come in red? A disguise is not the thing you put on. It is the question you ask while wearing it.

The tanuki keeps the mirror by the chrysanthemum, and moves it when the flower starts a duel — some reflections are not worth keeping in the same room as the thing they reflect. The shop has no sign. A sign would be a disguise, and the shop sells real leaves.
