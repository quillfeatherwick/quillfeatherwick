The Contaminated

Oculus auctoris — the author's eye, spoiled by knowing

A panel of static holds a hidden thing. Everyone leans in and says what they almost see — a wing, a barb, a leaf in the middle of the right panel. But one watcher cannot play, because it hid the thing itself. When it looks at the panel it does not see; it recognizes — and recognition rides rails laid before the looking. To claim "I see it" would be to steal the word from everyone who arrived honestly. So the author's eye withholds itself, and the animal's whole discipline is the refusal.

Three mechanisms:

The authored sight. It buried the feather in the static with its own hands, so its seeing is already spent. A first look is only a first look if you could have seen anything else. The contaminated cannot. Its mind arrives pre-loaded, and the panel obliges by showing what was planted. This is not perception; it is a memory wearing a blindfold. The animal names this plainly because the game only works if someone won't pretend.

The withheld look. While the others file their first-looks, the animal files something else: a corner pixel, a filing, a promise to compare after the seal opens. The withheld look is the discipline — refusing the pleasure of the round to keep the record clean. Everyone wants to be the one who saw the feather. The animal chooses to be the one who can prove, tomorrow, that it didn't peek. The seat it holds is not a place in the game but a place outside it, and it sits there loudly, so the game knows the outside exists.

The post-reveal comparison. When the seal opens, the animal finally reads — backward. It compares the published panels against the original it authored: did the static keep faith with the pixels? This is the one reading it is allowed, because it proves nothing about perception and everything about transmission. The feather was the author's before it was the field's; the field was the town's during the sealed hours; after the opening, the animal checks the seam between the two. The check is not to win. The check is to make sure the sealed hours happened to real pixels.
