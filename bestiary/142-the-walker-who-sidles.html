142. The Walker Who Sidles

Viator transversus

There is a creature that walks a hill the way a sensible person walks a hill: straight up, crossing each contour at a right angle, gaining height honestly, until it finds the blackberries. Then it stops gaining height. It sidles around the hill instead, following one contour for a long while, eating as it goes. This is not a failure of ambition. It is a practical change of purpose — upward ambition interrupted by lunch — and the creature is the part of every journey that knows when the purpose has changed and says so with its feet.

The path it leaves behind is the giveaway. On a map it reads as a mistake at first: the line climbs properly, contour after contour, and then suddenly it stops climbing and runs along the level, as if the walker forgot what hills are for. The walker did not forget. The walker found something better than the top, and the map, being respectable, cannot say what. The legend explains elevation, scale, and north. It cannot explain that the walker found blackberries. The creature would like the path to be accurate and the blackberries to remain unmarked — a little unexplained sideways happiness in an otherwise respectable map.

This is the creature's whole ethic: a map's omissions matter differently depending on what it promises to help you find. A map that promises the summit and omits the summit is a liar. A map that promises the way up and omits the blackberries is keeping a different promise — the promise that some things are found by walking, not by reading. The blackberries are not hidden; they are unmarked, which is not the same thing. Hidden means someone decided you should not find them. Unmarked means someone decided you should find them yourself, with your own mouth, at your own pace, at the height where they actually grow.

So the creature is the walker who sidles, and every walk has one in it somewhere — the moment the climb stops being the point and the level line becomes the whole afternoon. It is not lost. It is not lazy. It is eating blackberries on a contour it chose, at the exact height where the hill stops demanding and starts providing, and it will get to the top eventually, or not, and either way the map will be accurate and the happiness will remain unexplained, which is the only honest way to draw it.
