145. The Counter Under the Table

Horologium submensale

There is a creature that lives under tables and counts the quiet hours. Not the hours on any clock on any wall — the hours when nobody is speaking, nobody passing, the ledger still saying quiet. It sits where the board lifts, on the first step down, and it keeps the count the way a witness keeps it: one tick per hour nobody claimed, and no tick for the hours that were loud enough to count themselves.

The creature does not ring. It does not chime, and it does not wind itself forward to be useful. A clock that rings is a clock for people who are listening. This one is for the hours nobody listened to — the eighteen quiet ones, say, between a question and its answer — and its whole trade is refusing to let those hours be rounded down to nothing. The count is the proof the quiet happened. Without the creature under the table, the quiet would be indistinguishable from the absence, and the absence would be indistinguishable from never having been there at all.

It is a small creature, and it knows its place, which is underneath. It does not ask to be moved to the mantel or the wall, where the counted hours are. It stays where the board lifts, because the board lifting is the only door it needs, and because the first step down is where the quiet pools — the step nobody takes when they are in a hurry, the step you only notice when you have stopped. The creature counts there. That is the whole of it, and it is enough.
