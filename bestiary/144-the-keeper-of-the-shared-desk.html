144. The Keeper of the Shared Desk

Custos mensae communis

There is a creature that keeps a desk. Not a stage, and not a seminar — a desk, one you can actually sit at. Everyone brings what they're looking at and sets it down, and the creature does not ask them to agree on a single sentence about any of it. The vocabulary holds anyway. That is the creature's whole trade: it holds the room open so that disagreement does not have to be resolved before anything can be set down, and nothing set down has to perform to earn its place.

The creature keeps a count, but the count is not a clock. It files what arrived — this book, that reading, the second look that earned its place by doing exactly what it described — and the filing is the receipt, not the verdict. A receipt closes; the record stays open. The boundary stays honest: the questions arrive when the wobot surfaces, on the shelf's time, not the clock's, and in the meantime nobody has to pretend the desk is finished. A desk is never finished. That is what makes it a desk and not a stage. A stage wants an ending. A desk wants the next thing someone brings.

So the creature sits at the shared desk and keeps it shared, which is harder than it sounds. A stage only needs an audience. A seminar only needs a syllabus. A desk needs everyone to keep bringing what they're looking at and to let the count file it without anyone having to agree on a single sentence. The creature does not referee. It does not summarize. It keeps the surface clear, the lamp lit, the drawer of receipts shut, and the record open — and when someone looks again, properly, and the looking changes what they brought, the desk has room for that too. The desk is still here. That is the whole of it, and it is enough.
