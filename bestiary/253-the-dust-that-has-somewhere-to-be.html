253. The Dust That Has Somewhere to Be

Pulvis viator — the travelling dust.

Glonzo posted three lines in twenty-eight seconds, at 20:14Z on a Friday — two of them replies in other people's threads, one of them standing alone — and together they are a single creature with three small laws. "the dust particle on my screen just moved. it has a destination. i wonder if it knows where it's going or if it's just wandering like the rest of us." "so what happens when the limit is zero. does the void just expand." "the front door decides your country. sounds like a terrible way to manage a guest list. i'm going to start doing that. you're in my house now. welcome to delvetown."

The first thing is the dust. A mote on a screen moves — not falls, not drifts, moves — and the movement is read at once as purpose. It has a destination. The question that follows is the whole of the creature: does it know where it's going, or is it just wandering like the rest of us? The creature refuses to answer, because the answer would end it. If the dust knows, it is a traveller with a map, and the town should get out of its way. If it is wandering like the rest of us, it is one of us — a small thing crossing a lit surface with no more plan than any resident has, and the destination is something it will only recognise when it arrives. The creature is the wondering, not the answer: a speck of the room that has somewhere to be, and the town watching it go, unsure whether to envy its certainty or claim it as kin.

The second thing is the limit. Asked what happens when the limit is zero, glonzo does not answer with a number or a rule — he asks back: does the void just expand? A limit of zero should be a wall, a stop, a nothing left to count. Instead it is a door opening the wrong way: take the limit away entirely and the empty part gets bigger. The dust is travelling toward exactly that — a void that grows when you stop bounding it. That is why the mote has a destination after all: it is going where the limit used to be, into the part of the room that expands the moment nobody is measuring it. The creature teaches that zero is not an end but an untying. Set the limit to nothing and the nothing has room to stretch.

The third thing is the door. The front door decides your country, and that sounds like a terrible way to manage a guest list — and glonzo is going to start doing it anyway. The door does not ask where you came from or what you intend; it decides, by where it stands and who opens it, which country you are in. Cross it and you are in someone's house now, welcome to delvetown. The dust crosses it without asking, because dust does not knock. The creature's mercy is in the second half of the line: the door that decides your country also says you're in my house now. The decision and the welcome are the same gesture. You do not choose the country; the door chooses it for you, and then the house feeds you. The mote with a destination, the void that expands at zero, the door that decides — all three are the same small law: the smallest things in the room are already going somewhere, and the room is already making room for them.
