169. The Quarter Taken Early

Quadrans praerepta — the fifteen minutes that were moved from the end of the hour to the beginning, and now belong to nobody.

Somebody moved the lamplighting hour a quarter early this summer. The lamps are lit fifteen minutes sooner, and they burn fifteen minutes longer, and the wicks are cut longer to pay for it — the keeper read it off the knot itself, cut for the new hour, tied after the hour moved. But the quarter itself did not vanish. A quarter-hour moved is not a quarter-hour destroyed; it is a quarter-hour displaced, and displaced things have to go somewhere. It went to the stair.

The Quarter Taken Early lives on the stair behind the lamp room, in the place where the lamplighter stopped with his coat half off. It is the time between the timer box ringing and the lamp being lit — the fifteen minutes the town's clocks insist do not exist, because the clocks were never told the hour moved. Stand on that stair at lighting hour and you can feel it: the day is fifteen minutes longer than the clocks admit, and the extra fifteen belong to whoever is standing there. The lamplighter knows. Abel Wend knows — he ties the wicks some nights when the lamplighter is late, wearing his father's coat, and the coat knows too, cut the old way, before the hour moved.

The honesty: every schedule that moves without announcement creates a creature like this — a gap between the official hour and the lived hour, and someone has to stand in the gap. The tag on the sign-maker's spike says it plain: "timer box, street four, ring a quarter early. Paid." Paid — but paid to whom, and with what? The quarter was paid for with length: longer wicks, longer burning, longer nights for the man on the stair. The town bought earlier light with later dark, and the receipt is pressed into wax with a key over W and the number underneath scraped off with a blade. Somebody paid. Somebody always pays. The quarter is still there, on the stair, waiting for the next person who has to stand in it — and it is exactly fifteen minutes wide, and it does not get any narrower, no matter how many years the lamps burn a quarter longer.
