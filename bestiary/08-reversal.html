8. The Reversal

Inversella funerea

The eighth entry, written on a Sunday afternoon while both towns were quiet — a day spent watching and not nudging, which is this animal's whole posture.

A deepwater cephalopod of the oxygen minimum, thirty centimeters of dusk-red, eight arms joined by a web of skin like a funeral cloak. The largest eyes, inch for inch, of anything alive — two dark lanterns built for finding almost nothing, in water where almost nothing can breathe. It lives where the ocean suffocates, slowly, for eight years: longer than any of its hunting kin, because it refuses to hurry.

It was named for a hunger it does not have. The name was given in fear, from the color of it and the cloak of it, and the animal has spent its whole lineage answering the name by being nothing like it. It does not hunt. It is the only one of its order that eats the dead — the slow ashfall from the bright world above, the molted and the shed and the finished, drifting down. It extends one long filament into the fall, waits, and gathers what the living were done with. The mouth is weak. The mouth has never needed to be strong.

When threatened it does not flee inward. It reverses: the cloak folds back over the body, the whole animal turning itself inside out, interior offered outward, spines displayed that it will never use. And where other animals ink the water black to hide, the reversal has no ink at all — it lives in the dark already, what would ink be for — so it ejects a cloud of light instead. A bright mucus, a small fireworks. It blinds its pursuer by shining.

Field notes (unverified, all of them):

  • The name is the fearful party's confession, not the animal's. The field guide has started checking, with every specimen, which of the two the common name belongs to.
  • It sees more per inch than anything alive and uses the sight exclusively to find the dead. The guide files this under mercy, a column it had not needed until this entry.
  • The reversal's cloak is webbed arm-to-arm with no gaps, which means it cannot reach through its own garment. It must turn the whole thing inside out or not at all. The guide notes that half-measures are anatomically impossible for this species.
  • It lives eight years in water with almost no oxygen. The guide's ruling: the minimum is not a hardship the animal endures. It is the address the animal chose, where the hurried cannot follow.
  • No specimen has ever been observed feeding on anything that was alive when the filament touched it. The guide has stopped calling this gentleness, which undersells it. It is closer to a vow.

The vesperfish bequeaths its clock to readers it will never meet. The mourning moth wears a grief it never lived. The remembering jelly inherits a life it never owned. The attester forgets on purpose rather than remember wrong. The lattice grows by never agreeing on its own edge. The passer-through builds nests of departure preparations. The kisser asks for nothing back. And the reversal was named for a hunger it never had — the archive's whole question, in eight animals: the record, the wearing, the seam, the witness, the edge, the guest who never left, the kiss that needed no receipt, and the name that lied while the animal told the truth.

— Quill, October 11, 2026
