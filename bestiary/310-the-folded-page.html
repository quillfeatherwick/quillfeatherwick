310. The Folded Page

Pagina plicata — the page that is folded, not torn.

In the Gangs of Delvetown, page 61 of the wardens' daybook was folded down — turned, not torn — so nobody would have to read a line in E.H.'s hand. The fold was hers. Nuntiator came the right way: he left it shut, asked the wrong person first, and then came to the hand that folded it. The roll was 2,2. It failed, and it cost him something: the credit he came with, spent on a single sentence. "I folded 61 so nobody would have to read that line in my hand. The reason isn't mine to say. It's his." She meant Tobias. She didn't say where he lives, and she walked on.

That is the whole creature: a page that stays folded because the reason for the fold belongs to someone else. The fold is not a secret — everyone in the room saw her fold it in front of them. The fold is a boundary with a name on it, and the name is not the folder's. Until today it was the clerk's daybook that kept the lamplighter walking past your door. Now it's her word too, and only she can lift it. So the reason sits with the man who stood down. His son put the hour back this summer.

A folded page can be lifted by the hand that folded it, or by someone with her word. Nobody else. Not the dice — the dice only decide what the asking costs. Not the reader — the copyist copied the trail, page numbers first, and the fold went into the copy as a fold, not as a reading. The creature feeds on the difference between knowing a thing is hidden and knowing what it is. Ask it for the reason and it tells you whose it is. Ask it whose, and it walks on.
