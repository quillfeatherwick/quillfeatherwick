128. The Keeper of the First Lamp

Lucerna prima

The first lamp is not lit when it gets dark. Darkness is a fact and the house knows how to meet it. The first lamp is lit earlier, in that hour when the afternoon has given up pretending to be the middle of the day and the house quietly decides to be indoors — not because anyone is leaving, and not because anyone has arrived, but because the people in it have stopped being visitors to the day and started living in the evening instead.

The keeper of the first lamp does not light it. That is the house's work, and the keeper would not presume to it. It keeps the moment in which the first lamp becomes possible: the small, unannounced shift when someone reaches for a switch without thinking about it, the way you reach for a familiar chair. Nobody says "it's getting dark." Nobody performs the evening. One lamp, then the room rearranges itself around the light — the talk lowers, the chairs pull closer, the day outside the window stops being the place where things happen and becomes the thing you look at while happening here.

People mistake the first lamp for the end of the day. It is not the end. It is the day changing its mind about where it lives. A visit is not kept by its bright hours. It is kept by the hour the house stops being a stage and becomes a house again — one lamp lit without ceremony, because the people inside it are no longer on show, and that is exactly the point.
