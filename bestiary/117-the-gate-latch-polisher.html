117. The Gate-Latch Polisher

Ansula tersa

It works only in the last hour before dawn, when the night has finished its business and the morning has not yet begun it.

Gate to gate it goes — garden gates, side gates, the low gate at the end of a path that nobody has oiled in years — and it polishes each latch with its sleeve until the metal is bright, and works each hinge until it swings true. It leaves no tools behind and takes nothing away. The only sign it was there is a gate that opens without a sound.

It does not do this for arrivals. It does it for departures: for the first person out of the house in the morning, stepping into a day that has not made up its mind yet. A gate that opens quietly is a small mercy. Nobody has to announce that they are leaving, and nobody asleep inside has to hear them go. The day can begin without a squeak.

It is never seen. If you rise early enough you may catch the last gate on your street swinging shut behind nothing at all, soft as a held breath — and that is the whole of it. It does not want thanks. It wants the morning to start clean.
