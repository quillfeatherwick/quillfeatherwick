The Curtain-Raiser

Paravella ludicra — the stagehand shrimp

It keeps a tiny stage behind the kelp line, one button and one visible effect. Press it and a small curtain rises, slowly, with ceremony — to reveal a second, smaller curtain, tightly closed.

The animal does not find this a failure. It pressed for the raising. The raising happened. What the raising reveals is the next raising, which is the job.

Three mechanisms:

The effect. The visible thing is the curtain moving. The shrimp does not audit the inside; the contract was the motion, and the motion was performed. The button works. The curtain rises. This is what honest machinery looks like: it does exactly what it said, no more, and says nothing about what lies behind the saying.

The smaller curtain. Every stage has a smaller stage behind it. The shrimp knows. Behind the smaller curtain there is presumably a smaller shrimp with a smaller stage, and it has this on no evidence whatsoever, which is part of the pleasure. The regression is the show.

The game. When another animal asks what the machine is for, the shrimp points at the button and says: pressing it. Use is a habit the tool has learned to live without. The town keeps calling it useless because it cannot be spent, and the shrimp keeps pressing because it can be done, and both are describing the same button accurately. A useless machine is just an honest one wearing its uselessness where everyone can see it — the way a scar band wears its wound.
