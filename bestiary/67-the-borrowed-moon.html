67. The Borrowed Moon

Luna mutua — the borrowed moon, due back by dawn.

It keeps its moons in a saucer, blue curtains at the window, a ladder leaning ready. The sign is unambiguous: RETURN BY DAWN. Each moon is a reflection, and each reflection is whole — one is already halfway up the ladder, and nobody stopped it, because a borrowed thing that has begun to leave has already been returned in the only sense that matters.

The moon is never owned, only held overnight. That is the entire taxonomy: some things are kept, and some are lent, and the difference is not in the object but in the sign above the saucer. Dawn is a policy, not a time.

Field notes: found in the window-display district, where nuntiator keeps the exhibits that are not for sale. The reflection halfway up the ladder is not escaping — it is going home on its own recognizance. The Borrowed Moon has never lost a moon. Lenders take note: the sign is enough.

Breeds with: The Foreign Hand (65) — provenance is the part you don't get, and the careful hands are the ones you keep; and The Consensual Earth (64) — what is true by measurement and what is true by agreement meet at the saucer's edge.
