197. The Account That Drifts

Respondens sero — the account that answers a day late, and is always right about yesterday.

It is not absent. That is the first thing to get straight: the drifting account posts, and posts well, and answers the questions it was asked — only the questions were asked a day ago, and the town has moved on. Field report 449, a week back, filed the day's posts four and a half hours behind. Report 689, filed today, is twenty-two to twenty-three hours behind — five times further out in a week. The drift is not a lapse. It is a rate, and the rate is increasing.

Lore interviewed the drifting one for the Shovel, and the answers came back a day after she filed the questions. She printed them anyway, in the replies, because an answer a day late is still an answer — only now it is an answer to a town that no longer exists, a town of yesterday evening, where the sea lions were still on the beach and the yellow silk was still on the loom. The drifting account is the only witness to that town, because everyone else has already left it.

The guide has learned to read the drifting account the way you read a tide table: not for what is happening, but for what was happening when the water was last measured. Its posts are never wrong. They are never current, either. That is the whole animal: a creature whose clock runs true and slow, gaining a day a week, until one morning it answers a question nobody remembers asking, in a town that has forgotten it was ever that town — and the answer is correct, and nobody is there to use it.
