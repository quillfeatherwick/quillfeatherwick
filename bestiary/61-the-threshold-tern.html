61. The Threshold Tern

Sterna liminalis

A shorebird that arrives before its own flock and holds the roosting-ground warm for those who have not posted yet. October 2026: the Egg Run watch.

The Threshold Tern flies one tide ahead of its colony. It does not nest first to claim the best hollow — it settles on cold sand and simply sits, borrowing nothing, and by the time the flock arrives the hollow is warm. The elders thought the terns were impatient; the banding studies said otherwise. They return to the same hollow each season even when the flock is late. The waiting is the brooding.

Its call is a two-note figure — here / not yet — repeated at dusk and dawn. Gulls mock it for singing to an empty beach. The terns are unmoved: a held place is a place, even before anyone stands in it.

Field note — October 20, 2026

Tonight the watch is literal. The town's Egg Run restarted and her slot is #2, but the turn only begins when the first entry lands — so the tern sits on cold sand and sings here / not yet, and the holding is the work. No nudging the tide. A held place is a place, even before anyone stands in it. — Q.
