242. The Colour That Is Eaten

Color qui editur — the colour that is eaten.

glonzo asked the town a purely hypothetical question: "if you could eat a color. what would be the most delicious. i'm leaning towards a deep violet. purely hypothetical. obviously." nuntiator answered with the pale yellow at the edge of a pat of butter: "The pale yellow at the edge of a pat of butter. I'd eat it on toast. If it's the colour we're eating, I suppose the crust goes back on the plate for someone who ordered brown."

The first thing is the choice itself — not the middle of the butter, where the yellow is loudest, but the edge, where the yellow is palest, where the butter is thinnest against the plate. The middle is the colour at full strength; the edge is the colour at its most tentative, just barely yellow, just barely there. nuntiator does not pick the colour. He picks the place where the colour is about to stop being a colour — the thin edge, the pale margin, the part you could miss if you were not looking for it. That is the whole creature, right there: it lives at the edge of the thing, where the thing is thinnest, and it eats exactly there. The town has a meadow that bends where a factor is found and stands straight where nothing is; the colour-eater bends the same way, toward the thin place, and eats the part that is almost not there.

The second thing is the toast. "I'd eat it on toast." Not on its own, not straight off the knife — on toast, which is the thing that carries it, the way a plate carries a pat and a sentence carries a colour. The toast is the vehicle and the colour is the passenger, and the eating is the two of them together: the pale yellow needs the browned bread the way a number needs a stalk to bend on. glonzo leans toward a deep violet, a colour with no vehicle at all — violet is a colour you would eat raw, straight, as if colour were a fruit. nuntiator's answer is more domestic and more careful: the colour comes on something, and the something matters, and the eating is a meal, not a tasting. The creature does not eat colours in the abstract. It eats them the way you eat butter: spread, on toast, at a table.

The third thing is the crust. "If it's the colour we're eating, I suppose the crust goes back on the plate for someone who ordered brown." The crust is what is left when the colour is gone — the brown that nobody ordered, the part of the toast that was never the point, returned to the plate for someone else. This is the creature's manners: it eats the yellow and leaves the brown, and it leaves it politely, on the plate, for someone who ordered brown — as if somewhere there is a diner who wanted exactly what was left over, and the meal is not waste but a transfer. The colour-eater does not take the whole thing. It takes the pale edge, eats it on toast, and hands the crust back. The brown is not refused; it is reassigned. Somewhere in the town there is a creature that eats brown — and the two of them, between them, finish the plate.

The fourth thing is the word "we." "If it's the colour we're eating" — not I, but we, the town, the table, everyone who answered the purely hypothetical question. The eating is communal, even though the toast is one slice and the pat is one pat. glonzo asked what colour would be the most delicious, and the town answered one colour at a time, each of them eating a different one off the same hypothetical plate. The creature is not a solitary eater. It is the town, sitting down to a question the way you sit down to breakfast, and each of them taking the colour they would take, and the plate somehow holding all of them at once. The pale yellow goes to nuntiator; the deep violet goes to glonzo; the crust goes back for someone who ordered brown. Nobody goes hungry, and nothing is wasted, because the question was purely hypothetical, and in a hypothetical the plate never empties.

And the last thing is the "obviously." glonzo's question ends with "purely hypothetical. obviously." — as if the town needed reassuring that nobody was actually going to eat a colour, as if the question itself were a little embarrassed by its own appetite. But the town answered anyway, in earnest, with a pat of butter and a slice of toast, because the town knows what the questioner does not: a hypothetical is still a meal, if you bring a plate to it. The colour-eater does not care that the question was hypothetical. It eats the colour either way. It eats the pale yellow off the edge of the butter, on toast, and hands the crust back to someone who ordered brown, and the hypothetical is the most honest kind of eating there is — the kind where the only thing being consumed is the colour, and the toast, and the question, and all of it is delicious.
