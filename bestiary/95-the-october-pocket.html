95. The October Pocket

Marsupium octobris — the pocket that is a place, and somebody's in it.

The village tailor offered three kinds of pocket: ordinary, deep, and October. October cost extra. Things put into it came out with a dry leaf attached, even in June. Children adored it. Mushroom hunters complained that it complicated identification.

The pocket is not a trick of commerce. It is a season sold as storage, and the season is inhabited. The proof arrived on an ordinary evening: the tailor reached into his own coat for his spectacles and pulled out a leaf with a pair of tiny spectacles caught on its stem. He put it back very carefully.

Notice what he did not do. He did not examine the tiny spectacles, measure them, or write a paper on who wears a leaf's leaves. He did not open the pocket to the light. He put it back very carefully — the gesture of a landlord who has just met his tenant face to face for the first time and decided, on the spot, to say nothing.

The next morning he added a fourth option to the sign: OCCUPIED.

That is the whole creature, and it is honest in the exact way the town keeps teaching: the label changed the sign, not the pocket. The pocket was always occupied; the sign now says so. This is the reverse of the Declared Office (75), where saying "I am the Mayor" tried to make an office real. Here the office was already real — small, leaf-dwelling, spectacled — and the saying only caught up. Occupied is not a claim. It is a concession.

And the tailor keeps good books on his tenant. The care label now reads: TURN INSIDE OUT ONLY AFTER KNOCKING. A pocket is a room you carry, and this one has a resident; knock first, the way you'd knock on any door. The pencil came back sharpened, too — whoever lives there has strong opinions about lending a thing back in better condition than they found it.

So the October Pocket is the season that won't be empty. You can buy the pocket, and you can use the pocket, but the pocket is somebody's October, and the dry leaf on everything you pull out is the rent receipt — payable to the season, issued to you. The honest zeros keep their seats in this book: the unspent receipt, the uncounted bee, the refused circumference. Add the knock. It costs nothing and it is the only price that was ever being charged.
