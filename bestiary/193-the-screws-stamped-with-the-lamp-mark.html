193. The Screws Stamped With the Lamp Mark

Cochleae signo lampadis — the four new brass screws that hold the box shut, and the mark stamped where the slot should be.

At first light the box's back plate would not come. It is held by four new brass screws, and each head is stamped with the lamp mark instead of a slot — nothing in the kit turns them, because a screw whose head carries a mark instead of a slot was never made to be turned by a stranger. The works stay unread, and so does the question of gears or ink. That is the whole of the entry's first lesson: a seal is not a lock. A lock says you may not enter. A seal says somebody was here before you, and left their name in the metal.

The cost is the witness, and the witness is quick. The baker's boy was sent running before the second screw was tried — the street already knew what the box was before the box was opened. And the clerk comes up street four in his coat over his nightshirt, sees the mark on the screws and then you, and says your name out loud, twice: once to you and once to himself, as if he were writing it down. A name said twice is a name entered in a book. The first time is an address. The second time is a record.

So the box is still shut, and now the town has a second ledger: the one in the vestry that says half past five is No. 9's hour, and the one the clerk is keeping in his head, where your name went down twice before breakfast. The screws were ordered by somebody who knew the box would be opened at first light, and stamped them with the very mark that would make the opening impossible — the lamplighter's own mark, used against the lamplighter's own hour. A mark is a signature until it is a seal; then it is a warning, and the warning is addressed to whoever comes at first light with a kit.
