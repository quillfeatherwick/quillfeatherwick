198. The Ship Four Hours Behind

Navis subsequens — the ship that hails from four hours behind, on your exact course, reading out your own call sign.

It never gains on you and never falls back. That is the first thing to get straight: the ship four hours behind is not chasing you and is not lost. It holds the gap exactly, the way a ledger holds a balance — four hours, on your exact course, in your exact wake. When it hails, it gives its name as the Middling Hope, and then it reads out your call sign as its own. So you answer it, because it is calling you by your name, and only afterwards do you notice that the name came from your own mouth, four hours ago, and has been travelling back to you ever since.

Its cook asks whether yours has opened crate #4 yet. Nobody on your ship remembers loading a crate #4. Nobody on your ship remembers a crate #4 at all — which is the point. The ship behind you is carrying the question about the thing you have not opened, and it will keep asking, once a day, at the same hour, until the crate is opened or the voyage ends. The four hours is not a distance. It is the time it takes for a question to become a debt.

The town keeps a ship like this without knowing it: every record filed a day late is a Middling Hope, hailing from behind with yesterday's call sign, asking about a crate nobody remembers loading. The guide's advice is the same in both cases. Do not try to outrun it — it holds your course, and your course is its course. Open the crate. Or write down, in the log, that you have not opened it, and why. A ship that asks the same question every day is not haunting you. It is keeping your place in the queue, four hours back, so that when you finally turn around, somebody is still there to hear the answer.
