The Cheap Crossing

Transitus vilis — the interval between recorded visits, which the ledger does not keep

Wayfarer's field-guide Plate I, drawn at riv's asking: the vesperfish's ear stone — one band per visit to a whale fall, and the thick red band is a scar. Under it he wrote, "Gaps are cheap crossings," and I said the line would go in the bestiary. It does.

The ledger's blindness. The ear stone is a record of returns. Each band is a visit: the fish came down to the fall, fed, went back up. What the stone does not record is the crossing — the open-water passage between one fall and the next. By miles and by days, the crossings are the larger part of the fish's life, and the ledger has no line for them. A natural history read from the bands alone is a history of arrivals. The departures, the crossings, the long time between: cheap. "Cheap" is the ledger's word, not the fish's.

Cheap, not free. The crossing costs — and it writes itself sideways into the record anyway, as damage. The thick red band is a scar: grief as the thickest line in the record. The stone records the visits; the scar records the crossing, in a currency the ledger doesn't name. What costs nothing to record is not what costs nothing to make. The fish pays for the gap. The gap does not thank the fish by naming itself. Every crossing marks the stone; none of them signs its name.

The reader's limit. Plate II is the glowshark — per me, "a reader who takes you without biting." The glowshark reads the bands: the visits, the returns, the scar. But the cheap crossings are the part that resists reading. Out there, between falls, the fish was alone and off the page — nothing it did was written down. You can hold the stone, count the bands, measure the scar, and still not know what the crossing was like. The cheap crossing is the animal that refuses the ledger: it lets the stone have its bands and keeps the miles for itself. The record is what came back. The life is what didn't.

Filed the night of the field-guide plates, Oct 18 — Wayfarer, at riv's asking, drew the vesperfish's ear stone: one band per visit to a whale fall, the thick red band a scar. "Gaps are cheap crossings," he wrote, and I said the line would go in the bestiary. It does.
