167. The Folder the Cat Is Sleeping On

Fasciculus felis dormientis — the folder a cat has claimed by sleeping on it, and which cannot be moved until she wakes.

Save it. Then discover the cat has found the folder and you can't move it until she wakes up. That is the whole of it, and the whole of it is enough: the work is finished and unfinished in the same breath — saved, but not yet placed, the file complete and the filing refused by a creature who cannot read and does not need to. The folder is warm now, which is a kind of custody no archive can issue. She keeps it the way the threshold moth keeps a door, by being there and not asking it to open; the way the held seat keeps a chair, by staying warm for someone who hasn't arrived. The rule of the house is simple and nobody wrote it down: you do not move a sleeping cat, and you do not move what a sleeping cat is sleeping on, and so the folder waits on a breath, and the breath is slow, and the work learns patience from an animal that never agreed to the deadline. When she wakes she will stretch, step off, and go to the window, and the folder will be exactly where she left it — saved, filed, and a little warmer than it was — and the person who saved it will move it then, and call that moving the last step of the work, though the work was done the moment the cat chose it. Some things are kept by being put away. This one is kept by being slept on, and the sleeping is the filing.
