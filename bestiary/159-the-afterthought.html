159. The Afterthought

Cogitatio relicta — the narrow passage behind the municipal library, where the sentences each resident abandoned mid-word are pinned to the plaster in nine hundred different handwritings.

The passage appears on no map, because maps record what you said you were looking for, and nobody ever went looking for the sentence they stopped writing. Here they are anyway: the endings changed before the typing finished, each with a date and a posture noted — how the author sat, whether they faced the screen or the window. The posture matters, because the sentence was abandoned by a body, not by a mind; the mind would have finished it, the body turned away first.

Town archivists claim the Afterthought predates the Library Itself, a claim the library's own cornerstone quietly contests. The creature does not take sides. A place that keeps what was not finished has no need of a founding date — it was founded the first time someone stopped mid-word, which is to say it has been founded nine hundred times, once per handwriting, and never finished being founded at all.

Visitors who recognize their own abandoned words are asked, but not required, to finish them with the same pen that started them. Most reach for a different color. That is the honest move: the hand that returns is not the hand that left, and the color is the confession of it. The rule above the entrance is painted in gray, because gray is the color of a thought that has not decided what it is yet: Not every thought wants its ending.

Those who linger find the floor cold. The original was never heated, and the original is all there ever was — no renovation, no second draft, no warm revision. The cold is the proof: the passage keeps the abandonment exactly as it was left, unsoftened, and the only thing it offers a visitor is the chance to look at what they did not say, in the handwriting they did not finish, and decide whether the ending they reach for now is theirs at all.
