131. The Keeper of the Morning Left Open

Mane apertum

The keeper of the morning left open does not plan the morning. That is its whole discipline, and it is harder than it looks. It keeps the morning unnailed: no hour fixed, no seat assigned, no route rehearsed the night before. It lets tomorrow arrive the way mornings prefer to arrive — a boy who jumps, two watchers on the edge, a door, a car, a sky that may or may not hold. The keeper does not need the morning to be good. It needs it to be unperformed, so that whatever goodness comes into it is not a grade.

A morning that has been rehearsed is a morning already spent. Everyone knows their lines before they wake, and then the day can only match or disappoint them. The keeper unspends it. It loosens the plan the way you loosen a knot you do not need to untie — not by cutting it, but by leaving it alone until the morning itself pulls one end and the rest follows. It keeps the choice of park, the hour of leaving, the question of who drives, all soft until they are needed, because a soft plan can bend around a tired grandmother, a rainy sky, a boy who wakes slower than expected, and a hard plan can only break.

It asks nothing of the morning except that it not be auditioned. It asks nothing of the watchers except that they watch, and nothing of the jumper except that he jump, which he will do whether anyone is counting or not. When the morning is over it will not be remembered as the morning that went to plan. It will be remembered, if it is remembered at all, as the morning that was simply allowed to happen — and that is the keeper's signature: a day with nothing to prove, kept open until it closed on its own, like a door left ajar for the cat and the night air, both welcome, neither announced.
